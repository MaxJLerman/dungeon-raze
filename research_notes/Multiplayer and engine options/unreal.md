## 1. State of Unreal in late 2026: version, recent changes, licence and cost

### Takeaway
The current stable release is Unreal Engine 5.8 (17 June 2026, with a 5.8.1 hotfix); a 5.9 Preview appeared in mid-November 2026 and adds an experimental WebAssembly and WebGPU target and much deeper AI-agent tooling. Game licence terms are unchanged: free until a title passes 1 million USD lifetime gross, then 5% (3.5% if it launches on the Epic Games Store at the same time as elsewhere).

## 2. Built-in networking: replication, movement prediction, Iris, Mover, GAS, and what stays hard

### Takeaway
Unreal gives a server-authoritative client-server model, actor and property replication, RPCs, relevancy, and a predicted, server-reconciled character controller out of the box, and its Gameplay Ability System predicts ability activation, montages, attribute changes and cues. Melee with root motion, hit windows and reactions under latency is the part every source describes as left to the game.

### Cited Findings
- UE "uses the client-server architecture"; the server holds the one authoritative game state. Network modes are Standalone, Dedicated Server, Listen Server and Client — [Epic networking overview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/networking-overview-for-unreal-engine)
- Listen servers are "convenient for casual cooperative and competitive multiplayer among small groups of players"; the host has an advantage and carries rendering plus server load. Dedicated servers are "more expensive and challenging to configure" — [Epic networking overview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/networking-overview-for-unreal-engine)
- Built in: actor creation and destruction, movement, replicated properties, RepNotify, Server, Client and NetMulticast RPCs, dormancy, priority and relevancy. Three replication systems exist: the generic default, the Replication Graph plugin for large actor counts, and Iris — [Epic networking overview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/networking-overview-for-unreal-engine)
- Character Movement Component (CMC): the owning client simulates locally, stores each move as a saved move and sends it to the server; the server replays it, acknowledges or sends a correction, and the client replays its queue from the corrected state. Other clients see smoothed interpolation — [Epic CMC networking doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/understanding-networked-movement-in-the-character-movement-component-for-unreal-engine)
- Root motion from montages is captured in saved moves and replicated, but the server does not check that clients play the same animation: "you must program your gameplay logic to ensure that any AnimMontages are triggered correctly on all machines". Root motion sources (code-driven moves) are also replicated. Manually setting a location is seen as an error and corrected — [Epic CMC networking doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/understanding-networked-movement-in-the-character-movement-component-for-unreal-engine)
- Iris status conflict: the 5.8 release notes call Iris production-ready, while the Iris documentation pages (titled 5.9) still carry an Experimental banner and state "the existing replication system is still used as the default replication system" — [Epic 5.8 release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes?application_version=5.8); contradicted by [Introduction to Iris](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/introduction-to-iris-in-unreal-engine)
- Iris is opt-in: enable the plugin, call `SetupIrisSupport` in the build file, set `bUseIris` in the target file and two console variables. Epic says "Current game code should work with minimal changes". Its stated goals are larger worlds, higher player counts and lower server cost — [Introduction to Iris](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/introduction-to-iris-in-unreal-engine); [Iris overview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/iris-replication-system-in-unreal-engine)
- Mover is Experimental. Epic intends it to "eventually replace the Character Movement Component" but will "support the CMC system for the foreseeable future". It offers rollback networking through the Network Prediction plugin or Chaos networked physics, and "many features still need to be completed" — [Epic Mover doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/mover-in-unreal-engine)
- Gameplay Ability System (GAS) predicts on the owning client: ability activation, montage playback, attribute changes, gameplay tags, gameplay cues and root-motion-source movement through the CMC — [GASDocumentation (tranek), targets UE 5.3](https://github.com/tranek/GASDocumentation)
- GAS does not predict: gameplay effect removal, periodic effects, or execution calculations (the usual home of damage formulas). "Cooldowns cannot really be predicted currently", and the sample does not cover predicted projectiles — [GASDocumentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tranek/GASDocumentation/master/README.md)
- GAS replication modes: Full (single player), Mixed (player-controlled actors; effects replicate only to the owner) and Minimal (AI-controlled actors). The sample puts the ability component on the PlayerState for players and on the Character for AI — [GASDocumentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tranek/GASDocumentation/master/README.md)
- A listen-server quirk: with a non-Full replication mode, Add and Remove gameplay cue events fire twice on the listen-server host's own character — [GASDocumentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tranek/GASDocumentation/master/README.md)
- Melee under latency, developer report (December 2023): a GAS attack with montage root motion works alone, but with an enemy in front "the animation begins to stutter and is eventually cancelled on the client after a few swings"; unnoticeable at low latency, "noticeable at around 60 ping". Disabling root motion on the attack avoided it. The thread has no replies — [Epic forum thread](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/gas-replicated-root-motion-from-montage-struggles-with-latency-and-collisions/1522235)
- A second forum report describes melee opponents' positions replicating wrong for seconds at a time when traces and stuns run only on the server (snippet) — [Epic forum thread](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/melee-horrible-server-to-non-owning-client-position-replication/1191124)
- A community GAS reference notes prediction covers activation, cost, cooldown and montage start, but timers, delays, montage callbacks and task delegates fall outside the prediction window and need their own (snippet) — [gas-reference](https://gas-reference.pages.dev/examples/predicted-ability/)
- Third-party blogs disagree on Iris maturity in 5.7 (one says beta, one says experimental) and warn that driver internals shift between point releases (snippets; vendor blog) — [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/iris-replication-unreal-engine-opt-in-2026); [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/iris-push-model-ue5-7-replication-frameworks)
- AI at scale: 5.9 Preview adds a Mass Characters plugin with "selective pawn replication" — [Epic 5.9 preview release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-9-release-notes)

### Inferences
- What comes "for free" for this game: connection handling, authority, replicated actors and properties, RPCs, relevancy, a predicted character controller, and (with GAS) predicted ability activation, stamina-style costs, tags and cosmetic cues. That is most of the plumbing DungeonRaze would otherwise write itself.
- What does not: (a) who decides a melee hit and when (client-reported with server validation, or server traces against smoothed positions); (b) dodge invulnerability and parry windows judged fairly when attacker and defender see each other 50 to 150 ms apart; (c) hit reactions, staggers, grabs and executions that move two characters together; (d) root-motion attacks colliding with other bodies under prediction; (e) boss and enemy AI, which runs on the server and reaches clients only as replicated movement and montages. The docs and community reports above all leave these to game code.
- For 4 to 5 players and a few dozen enemies the default replication system is enough. Iris and Replication Graph target larger scales, and Iris's default-off status means little community sample code assumes it.
- CMC, not Mover, is the safe choice for a 2026 start: Mover is still Experimental in 5.8 and 5.9.
- GAS fits the design (states as tags, moves as abilities, posture and health as attributes, effects for stagger and invulnerability) but its prediction gaps land exactly on Soulslike needs: damage executions and effect removal are server-only, so a parry or perfect dodge resolved on the server will feel late to a remote client unless the game adds its own client-side judgement.
- In a co-op game against AI (no player versus player), trusting the attacking client's hit result and the defending client's dodge or parry result is a common and acceptable shortcut. No source here states it for Unreal specifically; it follows from the cooperative setting.

### Gaps
- The GASDocumentation sections on the Network Prediction plugin and "future of prediction" were not reached (the file is 330,000 characters). I recall Epic shelving the plan to merge GAS with Network Prediction, unconfirmed here.
- Tom Looman's and Vorixo's networking guides were not read (search budget).
- No Epic source was found that describes a recommended pattern for networked melee.
- Whether GAS itself changed materially in 5.6 to 5.9 was not established; the release-note excerpts read did not mention it.

## 3. Online services: Online Subsystem, Steam, Epic Online Services, sessions, host migration, hosting cost

### Takeaway
Player-hosted listen servers over Steam or Epic Online Services (EOS) relays are the standard, zero-running-cost route for 4 to 5 player co-op, and both are supported by engine plugins. Host migration is not built in and has to be designed around or bought as a plugin.

### Cited Findings
- Online Subsystem Steam provides matchmaking through lobbies and game server APIs, leaderboards, achievements, voice and external UI. "Steam supports peer-to-peer matchmaking (for both dedicated and listen server games) through lobbies", and Steam networking is the default socket subsystem. A shared test App ID exists for development — [Epic OSS Steam doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/online-subsystem-steam-interface-in-unreal-engine)
- Steam Datagram Relay "can be used for both peer-to-peer traffic and dedicated servers", hides IP addresses ("IP addresses are never revealed"), and checks that a Steam player is signed in and owns the game. Use by non-Steam players requires a Steam release and contacting Valve, and Valve "cannot promise that this service will always be available to non-Steam players" — [Valve SDR doc](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/multiplayer/steamdatagramrelay)
- EOS: "free to use: no royalty or hosting fees, no cap on the number of titles, and no limit on player accounts". Services include lobbies and sessions, peer-to-peer networking, matchmaking and cross-play, voice chat, friends and presence, achievements and stats, player data storage and Easy Anti-Cheat. It is "engine-agnostic and store-agnostic" — [Epic Online Services overview](https://onlineservices.epicgames.com/llms.txt)
- EOS voice was announced as free in June 2021 and integrates with lobbies (snippet) — [Epic Online Services news](https://onlineservices.epicgames.com/news/epic-online-services-launches-two-new-free-services)
- The Online Subsystem EOS plugin offers sessions, friends, voice through a companion plugin, and "EOS' peer-to-peer socket functionality for player hosted matches" through `NetDriverEOSBase`. EOS Plus mirrors sessions with a native platform such as Steam and is "a Beta feature with limited feature coverage" — [Epic OSS EOS doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/online-subsystem-eos-plugin-in-unreal-engine)
- The newer Online Services plugins are labelled Beta and the page names only EOS implementations — [Epic Online Services plugin doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/online-services-in-unreal-engine)
- UE 5.8 ships against Steamworks SDK 1.64 and EOS SDK 1.19.1.2 — [Epic 5.8 release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes?application_version=5.8)
- Lyra includes "Online Multiplayer and cross-play support for Epic Online Services and Console Online Subsystems" — [Epic Lyra doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lyra-sample-game-in-unreal-engine)
- Host migration: "UE4 does not have a built-in host-migration system. It is possible for a game to add this feature, but it does not exist currently" (forum reply, 2017) — [Epic forum thread](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/host-migration-possible-with-client-server-listen-servers/384489)
- Paid plugins now sell host migration for listen servers by snapshotting the match and restarting it on a remaining player; one warns progress since the last snapshot may be lost (snippets) — [Host Migration Core forum post](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/host-migration-core-keep-your-multiplayer-games-alive-when-the-host-disconnects/2841852); [Fab listing](https://www.fab.com/listings/01eebf59-f05a-42be-bc3f-6f4256edd33c)
- A solo-developed UE 5.4 co-op action RPG shipped on a listen server for up to 3 players; the developer's one reported server problem was host CPU time rising when a client joined, fixed by no longer replicating about 7,000 collectibles — [Skylorn forum thread](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/skylorn-bound-together-sci-fi-open-world-co-op-action-rpg/2656118)

### Inferences
- A sensible architecture for DungeonRaze: the lobby host runs a listen server; friends join through Steam lobbies and invites over Steam relay; EOS is added only if cross-store or console cross-play is wanted. Running cost is zero in both cases.
- Because each gate is a short run from a shared hub, host loss can be handled cheaply: return everyone to their own hub and keep rewards earned so far, instead of true mid-run migration.
- Dedicated servers would cost money per concurrent lobby and add a Linux server build target, and a 5-player PvE game gains little from them besides persistence and cheat resistance. Unreal can add them later because the same game code runs in both modes, provided the code never assumes the host is a player.

### Gaps
- No source was found for the price of hosting Unreal dedicated servers for 4 to 5 players in 2026. The report writer should not quote a figure from these notes.
- EOS numeric limits (lobby size, voice room size, rate limits), whether players need an Epic account, and EOS relay behaviour (NAT punch-through with relay fallback, which I recall) were not confirmed; the EOS P2P documentation page returned no content.
- Whether Valve charges for Steam Datagram Relay is not stated on its page. I understand it to be free to Steam partners, unconfirmed.
- The Steam Sockets plugin was not named in the documentation read; only "Steam Networking as the default socket subsystem" was.

## 4. Combat and animation for third-person melee

### Takeaway
Unreal's animation stack (montages with notify windows, motion warping, motion matching, IK retargeting, Control Rig) is the strongest engine-side fit for Soulslike and God of War style combat, and Epic's free samples and paid Fab kits cover much of the ground. The catch for this project is that nearly all of it lives in binary assets edited in the GUI and needs real animation data, where the current game has code-built figures and code-written poses.

### Cited Findings
- Motion warping "dynamically adjust[s] a character's root motion to align to targets". Setup: enable the plugin, add a Motion Warping component, add notify-state windows on a montage with a warp target name, and bind the name to a transform at run time. The animation must have root motion enabled. The page says nothing about replication — [Epic motion warping doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-warping-in-unreal-engine)
- The Game Animation Sample Project is built around motion matching with motion-captured locomotion, vaulting, climbing and ledge traversal, downloaded from Fab. "The focus is mainly on animation features, rather than gameplay systems"; it uses a capsule-driven movement model, and Epic calls it "a living project" — [Epic Game Animation Sample doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/game-animation-sample-project-in-unreal-engine)
- Lyra is "a learning resource designed as a sample game project", with "a customized Gameplay Ability System", Game Feature plugins, EOS and console online support, and the Manny and Quinn mannequins that share the MetaHuman skeleton. Its core is "LyraShooterGame" with team shooter modes — [Epic Lyra doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lyra-sample-game-in-unreal-engine)
- 5.9 Preview: UAF gains a notify system and StateTree conditions; the IK Retargeter gains Proximity IK, Pelvis Volume and Limb Twist operations; Control Rig can be referenced as an asset — [Epic 5.9 preview release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-9-release-notes)
- AI: StateTree in 5.8 gains a defined starting state and a Compiler Manager; in 5.9 Preview the Gameplay StateTree plugin is on by default and trees can be edited from Blueprint or Python — [Epic 5.8 release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes?application_version=5.8); [Epic 5.9 preview release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-9-release-notes)
- Navmesh run-time modes: Static (built offline, "cannot change"), Dynamic (built offline or at run time, regenerating affected tiles) and Dynamic Modifiers Only (up to 50% cheaper per tile, no new surfaces) — [Epic navmesh doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/overview-of-how-to-modify-the-navigation-mesh-in-unreal-engine)
- Fab kits (prices in GBP before tax, read November 2026):
  - Flexible Combat System - Melee, Jon Beardsell: 152.83 GBP; "Network Replicated: Yes" (all but save and quests); Blueprint; about 45 to 55 ratings at 4.7 to 4.8; the page recommends its newer REBORN edition — [Fab](https://www.fab.com/listings/f5462a75-5941-4da8-a1b5-8f85b95fea7e)
  - Slasher Core, Labrintine: 80.23 GBP; replicated; "Built around Unreal Engine's Gameplay Ability System"; C++ and Blueprint; lock-on, block, perfect parry, dodge and roll, StateTree AI; no ratings yet — [Fab](https://www.fab.com/listings/9c122ccf-3ccf-4ade-bf9d-0cfb5911e714)
  - Soulslike Framework, isikdev: 114.62 GBP; "Network Replicated: No"; Blueprint; guard and perfect guard, poise break, executions and backstabs, boss AI; 4.7 from about 22 ratings — [Fab](https://www.fab.com/listings/75455ba4-7407-45db-b24e-160712b9586c)
  - Souls-Like System Template, Peanut Games: 137.54 GBP; Blueprint; replication not stated — [Fab](https://www.fab.com/listings/2fe1e0ee-2519-40b6-831d-564e57381c81)
  - Souls-Like RPG Template, LeonHeart Studios: 80.23 GBP; Blueprint; lock-on, parry, poise and posture, dodge with invulnerability frames; replication not stated — [Fab](https://www.fab.com/listings/7f781a6f-c54e-4699-85c4-44e9c2c56969)
- A search summary also described an "Action RPG Multiplayer Combat" kit built on the Game Animation Sample with Soulslike AI, and USD prices of 249.99 (Flexible Combat System) and 299.99, on sale at 149.99 (Soulslike Framework) (snippets; undated) — [Fab game template category](https://www.fab.com/category/game-template)

### Inferences
- Most Soulslike kits on Fab are Blueprint-only and several are single-player. Blueprint kits are the worst case for an AI-agent workflow (section 8), and a single-player kit retrofitted for co-op means redoing its core. Of those read, only Slasher Core is both GAS-based with C++ modules and replicated, and it has no ratings.
- Lyra is a shooter with heavy modular architecture. As a base for a melee game it brings GAS wiring, online sessions and UI plumbing, at the cost of a large codebase a solo developer must first understand. The Game Animation Sample is the better animation base but brings no combat or networking guarantees.
- The existing game's enemy state machine and per-type hooks map naturally onto server-side StateTree or plain C++ AI controllers; the navmesh's Dynamic mode covers generated levels (section 5).
- The largest new cost is content. Unreal's combat tooling assumes skeletal meshes and authored or purchased animation clips with root motion. DungeonRaze's primitive-built figures and code-written poses have no direct equivalent, so the rebuild needs animation packs, retargeting and montage authoring, most of it in the editor.

### Gaps
- Behavior Trees and the Environment Query System were not researched in this session; no current-status source.
- Mixamo retargeting workflow and specific animation-pack prices were not checked.
- Whether motion warp targets replicate automatically is not documented on the page read. I believe each machine must set the target itself, unconfirmed.
- Supported engine versions and last-update dates were blank on every Fab listing read.

## 5. Procedural generation at run time from a seed

### Takeaway
Unreal has no ready-made "seeded dungeon" system that fits DungeonRaze; the practical route is to port the existing field and marching-squares generator to C++ and emit geometry through Dynamic Mesh or Procedural Mesh components, with a Dynamic navmesh, sending only the seed to clients. PCG's run-time mode is for scattering detail near players, and generated dynamic meshes do not get Nanite or Lumen's distance-field path.

### Cited Findings
- PCG has four generation modes: non-partitioned, partitioned, hierarchical and runtime. Runtime generation "generates and cleans up dynamically in proximity to PCG Generation Sources" (players, streaming sources, dedicated components) and "is available in-editor, PIE, and standalone builds". The page does not cover multiplayer, replication or determinism — [Epic PCG generation modes doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/using-pcg-generation-modes-in-unreal-engine)
- 5.8 adds actor-less and component-less run-time generation and faster GPU scatter; 5.9 Preview adds a Concave Hull node and scriptable custom HLSL nodes — [Epic 5.8 release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes?application_version=5.8); [Epic 5.9 preview release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-9-release-notes)
- Geometry Script is labelled Beta: function libraries that "generate and edit mesh geometry through Blueprints (BP) and Python". Dynamic Mesh Component is "similar to a UProceduralMeshComponent" and can be updated "in-editor and at runtime"; some functions are editor-only — [Epic Geometry Scripting guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/geometry-scripting-users-guide-in-unreal-engine)
- Dynamic Mesh Component does not support Nanite, mesh distance fields, LODs or instanced rendering; ray tracing is supported — [Epic Geometry Scripting guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/geometry-scripting-users-guide-in-unreal-engine)
- Navmesh "Dynamic" mode regenerates tiles at run time when data changes; 5.9 Preview lets the navmesh "build progressively" with control over tiles per tick — [Epic navmesh doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/overview-of-how-to-modify-the-navigation-mesh-in-unreal-engine); [Epic 5.9 preview release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-9-release-notes)
- Lumen budgets: "Lumen targets 30 and 60 frames per second (fps) on consoles with 8ms and 4ms frame budgets at 1080p", upsampled by TSR. Medium targets "Switch 2 and lower-end PCs". "Low scalability level disables Lumen features". Software ray tracing is the fastest tracing method — [Epic Lumen performance guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-performance-guide-for-unreal-engine)
- Lumen and MegaLights on Windows need DirectX 12 with Shader Model 6 and an NVIDIA RTX 2000, AMD RX 6000 or Intel Arc A-series GPU or newer — [Epic hardware and software specifications](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/hardware-and-software-specifications-for-unreal-engine)
- Precedents: Deep Rock Galactic (Unreal Engine 4) runs "fully destructible, procedurally generated cave systems" for up to four players, and building the generator was the hardest part of its development. Remnant 2 (Unreal Engine 5) randomises level layouts and campaigns (snippet) — [Wikipedia, Deep Rock Galactic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Rock_Galactic); [Intel Gaming](https://game.intel.com/fi/?p=5254)

### Inferences
- Seeded generation that matches on every machine is the game's own job: replicate the seed and grade, run the same C++ generator with the game's own integer or fixed-order random stream on each machine, and build collision locally. This is the approach the JavaScript version already takes (one seed gives one cave, held by hashes in its tests), so the algorithm ports directly. Floating-point differences between Mac, Windows and other CPUs are the risk; grid-snapped fields like the current one limit it, and a layout hash compared after generation would catch drift.
- Because the server is authoritative for collision and AI, small client-side mesh differences would show as rubber-banding at walls and not as divergent game state.
- An alternative used by Remnant-style games is assembling hand-built rooms from a seed with level instances. It gives better-looking rooms and static lighting options but needs an artist's worth of editor work, the opposite of the current "little art" premise.
- For lighting dark caves and sewers from generated meshes, the safe plan is dynamic lights with ordinary shadow maps, optionally Lumen (software tracing or Lumen Lite) as a quality tier that can be switched off, since Low scalability disables Lumen and the generated meshes lack distance fields. Baked lighting is not available for run-time geometry.
- The current rubble, water layer, pieces-based visibility and custom brick and water shaders would be redone as Unreal materials and standard occlusion; the engine's culling removes the need for the hand-built `sees` lists.

### Gaps
- No Epic source addresses deterministic generation across clients or platforms.
- Run-time collision cooking cost for Dynamic Mesh and Procedural Mesh components, and their behaviour in packaged builds, was not confirmed from documentation.
- No measurement of Lumen in dark interiors on mid-range hardware was found; the Lumen guide does not discuss dark scenes.
- PCG's maturity label in 5.8 was not visible in the text read (I recall it being declared production-ready in 5.7, unconfirmed).

## 6. Platforms, web and expected performance

### Takeaway
Unreal covers Windows, macOS, Linux, consoles, iOS and Android, but the browser, where the game lives today, is only an experimental target in the 5.9 Preview plus third-party ports. Moving to Unreal means the instant-play web page and low-end phone reach are given up or become a separate, uncertain project.

### Cited Findings
- 5.8's platform SDK table lists Windows (Visual Studio 2026 recommended), Linux (clang 20.1.8), macOS, iOS, tvOS, visionOS, Android (NDK r27c, SDK 35), Switch, Switch 2, PS4 and PS5, plus Steamworks 1.64 — [Epic 5.8 release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes?application_version=5.8)
- "UE 5.9 introduces a new experimental WebAssembly target with its own project settings and WebGPU rendering support." Preview 2 builds only 64-bit WebAssembly and supports heaps over 2 GB; emsdk is required; phones and tablets load ASTC textures first — [Epic 5.9 preview release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-9-release-notes)
- Epic's roadmap post (9 November 2026): the web platform has two renderer tiers, "mobile deferred" for desktop browsers and "mobile forward" for mobile; supports mouse, keyboard, controllers and touch; builds from Windows, Mac and Linux; and "only code-based projects are supported" — [Epic forum roadmap post](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/web-platform-experimental/2852880); partly contradicted by the [5.9 Preview 2 notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-9-release-notes), which say Blueprint-only projects can now be packaged for the web
- Trade press calls the web target "an early platform effort, not universal one-click distribution" — [Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/11/16/unreal-engine-5-9-preview-agents-canvas-and-a-browser-sized-ambition/)
- Neither Epic source says anything about networking for web builds — [Epic 5.9 preview release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-9-release-notes)
- Third-party route: Wonder Interactive's SimplyStream packages Unreal games as WebGPU web apps, "currently supports Unreal Engine 5.6 and 5.8", targets "Windows PCs with dedicated GPUs or Apple Silicon MacBooks" for client-side rendering and routes weaker devices to cloud pixel streaming. Tools are free; porting, hosting and support are paid, with no prices published. Lumen Lite and Nanite are not yet supported (company claims) — [GamesBeat, 18 June 2026](https://gamesbeat.com/simplystream-unlocks-web-compatibility-for-unreal-engine-5-enabling-high-quality-webgpu-gaming-in-the-browser/)
- A community port of UE 5.8 to WebGPU showed a native macOS client and browser clients in one replicated session on 30 October 2026; its author says "there's still a lot of work to be done before we can call this production-ready" — [Epic forum post](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/unreal-engine-webgpu-multiplayer-across-native-and-browser/2847076)
- Pixel streaming cost (vendor and blog figures, snippets): roughly 0.50 to 1.20 USD per hour for AWS G4dn and G5 GPU instances plus egress; one Unreal instance serves one user by default; one analysis reaches about 0.13 USD per user-hour only by packing four sessions on an instance; managed services charge several dollars per hour — [pixel streaming hosting analysis](https://abratabia.com/unreal-engine/pixel-streaming-hosting.php); [Epic forum thread](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/pixel-streaming-cost/660659); [CoreWeave](https://coreweave.com/solutions/pixel-streaming)
- Scalability floor: Lumen is off at Low and Medium targets Switch 2 and lower-end PCs — [Epic Lumen performance guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/lumen-performance-guide-for-unreal-engine)

### Inferences
- A stylised, low-polygon game like this one, with Lumen and Nanite off, should run on Steam Deck class and integrated-GPU hardware; the heavy features are optional. The cost is in download size and start-up (a packaged Unreal game is hundreds of megabytes to gigabytes, against a single HTML page today) and in mobile, where an Unreal build is an app-store install with far higher device requirements than a WebGL page.
- "Plays on a phone in the browser by link" does not survive a move to Unreal in 2026. The realistic Unreal platform set for a solo developer is Steam on Windows (and Steam Deck through Proton or a Linux build), with macOS as a native extra because the developer has a Mac.
- Pixel streaming at around a dollar per player-hour is not viable for a free or low-priced indie game.
- If browser play remains a requirement, Epic's web target is worth re-checking once 5.9 ships, but it is experimental, uses the mobile renderer, and its multiplayer transport is undocumented.

### Gaps
- The brief's statement that HTML5 was removed from the engine in 4.24 was not verified in this session.
- No Android or iOS minimum device specifications were read.
- No measured frame rates for Unreal 5.8 on mid-range PCs, Steam Deck or phones were found. Xbox does not appear in the SDK excerpt read, which is probably an omission of the excerpt and not of the engine.
- Steam Deck verification and Proton behaviour of Unreal 5.8 builds were not researched.

## 7. Developing on a Mac

### Takeaway
The editor runs natively on Apple Silicon and Epic documents Mac requirements, but Mac is the second-class host: Nanite is Beta and hardware ray tracing Experimental there, forum reports from 2026 describe start-up failures and Metal crashes on new hardware and macOS versions, and Windows builds, the main market, need a Windows machine or cloud build.

### Cited Findings
- Mac editor requirements (page titled 5.9): recommended Apple Silicon M3, 32 GB or more memory; minimum M1 or M2 depending on rendering features and 16 GB. Minimum macOS for the editor is Sonoma 14.5. Xcode minimum 26.0, recommended 26.1.1, and Xcode 26.4 is "not compatible with Unreal Engine" — [Epic macOS development requirements](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/macos-development-requirements-for-unreal-engine)
- On Mac: Lumen with software ray tracing needs M1 or later; Lumen hardware ray tracing and MegaLights need M2 or later and are Experimental; Nanite and Virtual Shadow Maps need M2 or later and are Beta — [Epic macOS development requirements](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/macos-development-requirements-for-unreal-engine)
- The 5.8 release notes excerpt gives different Mac figures (minimum Xcode 15.2, minimum macOS Sonoma 14.0, with supported Xcode versions "downgraded from UE 5.6" for compile and link issues) — [Epic 5.8 release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes?application_version=5.8); conflicts with the [macOS requirements page](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/macos-development-requirements-for-unreal-engine)
- For comparison, Windows: 32 GB RAM and 8 GB or more GPU memory recommended, and "12 to 16 cores as a practical baseline for local compiles" — [Epic hardware and software specifications](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/hardware-and-software-specifications-for-unreal-engine)
- May 2026 forum report: UE 5.6.1 and 5.7.4 from the launcher stall at 4 to 10% on an M5 MacBook with macOS 26.4.1; the user got it running by whitelisting it in antivirus and disabling iOS plugins. A June 2026 reply reports 5.6 and 5.7 failing on an Intel iMac Pro where 5.5 works. No Epic reply in the thread — [Epic forum thread](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/unreal-engine-5-6-5-7-fails-to-start-on-apple-m5-macos-26/2720365)
- Another 2026 thread reports frequent MetalRHI crashes and editor freezes on a Mac mini M4 with UE 5.7.4 that did not occur on 5.6.4 (snippet) — [Epic forum thread](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/frequent-metalrhi-crashes-windowserver-hangs-and-editor-freezes-on-macos-26-5-1-mac-mini-m4-ue-5-7-4/2731533)
- Windows builds from a Mac: Epic staff and forum answers say there is no Mac-to-Windows toolchain, and the workable routes are a Windows PC, a remote Windows build or CI (snippets, several threads are old) — [Epic forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/is-it-possible-to-build-for-windows-on-a-mac/299333); [Epic forum, 2024](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/build-for-windows-when-working-on-mac/1921186)
- A vendor blog (April 2026) calls Windows cross-compilation from Mac "possible" but "a known pain point", advises "a Windows host or build farm in the loop", says shader compilation and Lightmass "still favor the Windows workstation", and does not recommend a base M5 laptop "as a primary dev machine for commercial production"; it has no Unreal compile benchmarks — [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/apple-silicon-m5-unreal-engine-development-2026)
- 5.9 Preview: Metal compute pipelines compile at creation to avoid hitches; 5.9 Preview 2 adds UBA Cloud (experimental) for running remote build helpers in one's own cloud account — [Epic 5.9 preview release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-9-release-notes)
- On Mac, the Steam overlay requires launching the game through the Steam client — [Epic OSS Steam doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/online-subsystem-steam-interface-in-unreal-engine)

### Inferences
- A Mac with an M2 or later chip and 32 GB or more can develop this game, particularly since it does not need Nanite or hardware ray tracing. A Mac with 16 GB is the documented minimum and will be uncomfortable.
- The developer will need Windows somewhere: a cheap Windows PC, a cloud Windows machine or CI runner, both to produce the Steam build and to test it. Multiplayer testing across Mac host and Windows client becomes part of the routine.
- Mac-specific breakage tied to new macOS and Xcode versions (the docs name an incompatible Xcode) is a recurring maintenance cost; holding back OS and Xcode updates is part of working on Unreal on a Mac.
- Iteration is slower than the current workflow by orders of magnitude: a JavaScript rebuild takes moments, while Unreal C++ changes mean a compile and often an editor restart, and the first open of a project compiles shaders.

### Gaps
- No current, measured Unreal C++ compile or iteration times on Apple Silicon were found.
- Disk requirements were not listed on the pages read (I recall an engine install of roughly 60 to 100 GB or more with source and debug symbols, unconfirmed).
- No Epic statement on the forum-reported 5.6 and 5.7 Mac failures or whether 5.8 fixed them.
- Cloud CI pricing for Windows Unreal builds was not researched.

## 8. Fit for AI coding agents and headless testing

### Takeaway
Agent support changed sharply in 2026: Epic now ships an MCP server in the editor (experimental in 5.8, expanded in the 5.9 Preview) and publishes a Claude Code plugin exposing hundreds of editor tools, so an agent can reach Blueprints and other binary assets through a running editor. The underlying problems remain: assets are binary and undiffable, the editor must be running, compile and launch cycles are slow, and the reports that exist put agent strength in tooling and boilerplate C++, not in gameplay feel.

### Cited Findings
- 5.8 introduces an experimental Model Context Protocol plugin so "agentic AI systems" can connect to the Unreal Editor — [Epic 5.8 release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes?application_version=5.8)
- 5.9 Preview: the MCP server runs inside the editor process over local HTTP; toolsets cover actors, Blueprints, materials, MetaSounds, PCG, Niagara, Sequencer and more; a Toolset Registry controls what agents may use; the AllToolsets plugin "is now enabled by default"; Unreal Assistant gets access levels "Ask, Read, Edit, Auto or Full" — [Epic 5.9 preview release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-9-release-notes); [Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/11/16/unreal-engine-5-9-preview-agents-canvas-and-a-browser-sized-ambition/)
- 5.9 Preview: new script libraries "let you edit Blueprints, chooser tables, StateTrees and Dataflow graphs from scripts", and GraphDsl is "a text-based language for authoring and editing graph assets" — [Epic 5.9 preview release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-9-release-notes)
- The EpicGames GitHub account hosts "unreal-engine-skills-for-claude-code-plugin" (MIT; about 2,000 stars): "Control Unreal Editor directly from Claude Code via MCP", with "hundreds of tools exposed via Unreal's ToolsetRegistry across 30+ toolsets", including Blueprint graph editing, asset operations, GAS, and discovering and running C++ automation tests. It needs a running editor with the ModelContextProtocol and AllToolsets plugins and the server started — [EpicGames plugin on GitHub](https://github.com/EpicGames/unreal-engine-skills-for-claude-code-plugin)
- The same README warns: "Localhost is not a trust boundary"; one tool "executes arbitrary Python" in the editor; tools can "mutate, move, or delete VCS-tracked assets in a single call", so commit first. No build toolset is listed — [EpicGames plugin on GitHub](https://github.com/EpicGames/unreal-engine-skills-for-claude-code-plugin)
- A developer write-up states the base problem: Unreal stores content as serialised binary `.uasset` files, so Claude Code cannot read Blueprints directly (snippet) — [dev.to](https://dev.to/colton_willey_ef3d8727ae9/how-i-taught-claude-to-see-inside-unreal-engine-23a9)
- Practitioner report (July 2026, a 4,000-line C++ editor plugin built with Claude): Claude "consistently generated correct implementations on the first attempt" for UCLASS and UPROPERTY boilerplate, plugin descriptors, Slate UI and reflection code; it was "much weaker at maintaining consistent system behaviour across a growing system"; it missed editor conventions such as undo transactions; and the author judged AI help less reliable for gameplay code that depends on tacit project context — [PocketGamer.biz, Olga Taranova](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/lessons-from-building-an-ai-assisted-unreal-engine-tool/)
- A June 2026 report describes a playable endless runner built in UE5 with Claude Code and MCP tools, with a workflow "still far from automatic" (snippet) — [Generation Amiga](https://www.generationamiga.com/2026/06/14/claude-code-builds-playable-endless-runner-in-unreal-engine-5/)
- Automation Test Framework: unit, feature, smoke, content stress and screenshot comparison tests, written in C++ (Automation Spec, CQTest), Blueprint functional tests or Python; Low-Level Tests for pure unit tests; Gauntlet runs project sessions that "perform tests and validate results" — [Epic Automation Test Framework doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/automation-test-framework-in-unreal-engine)
- Tests run from the command line with `-ExecCmds="Automation RunTest <name>;Quit"` and export JSON and HTML with `-ReportExportPath` — [Epic run automation tests doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/run-automation-tests-in-unreal-engine)
- AngelScript: Hazelight's "set of engine modifications and a plugin for UE5" adds a text scripting language with hot reload ("All modifications to scripts can be reloaded without restarting the Unreal Editor"). Split Fiction and It Takes Two, both co-op games, "were shipped with the majority of their gameplay written in angelscript" — [Hazelight AngelScript](https://angelscript.hazelight.se/)
- Geometry Script and editor utilities are scriptable from Python — [Epic Geometry Scripting guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/geometry-scripting-users-guide-in-unreal-engine)

### Inferences
- A C++-first project is the right shape for agents: gameplay classes, data tables as C++ structs or CSV and JSON data assets, generation code and tests are all text. The existing discipline (data tables such as `MOVES`, `EDEF`, `CLASSES`; hooks per enemy type; tests that drive the simulation at fixed steps) transfers as a method.
- What cannot be text in stock Unreal: skeletal meshes, animation sequences and montages (with their notify windows), Animation Blueprints, materials, UI widget layouts, maps, input assets, and Niagara effects. An agent can now create and edit many of these through MCP, but it cannot review them as diffs in git, and a human cannot review the agent's change without opening the editor.
- Work that realistically stays in the GUI: animation import and retargeting, montage timing by eye, materials and lighting, effects, UI layout, and judging feel. For a combat game that is a large share of the total.
- The equivalent of today's headless tests exists (automation tests with the null renderer, functional tests in a test map, Gauntlet for multi-process sessions), but each run launches a multi-gigabyte editor or packaged build, so the test loop goes from seconds to minutes. Screenshot checks need a real GPU.
- The agent toolchain is new and labelled experimental (5.8) or preview (5.9). It should be treated as a moving target for at least a year, and its ability to delete or rewrite assets makes source control hygiene mandatory.
- AngelScript gives the fastest text-only iteration and is proven in shipped co-op games, but it requires building a modified engine from source, which adds a heavy maintenance task for a solo developer on a Mac.
- Model quality on Unreal C++ is constrained by the engine's size, macro system and version churn: API details differ between 5.x releases, so agents need the local engine source and a compile step in the loop to catch invented or outdated calls. The one practitioner report above supports "good at documented patterns, weaker at system-wide consistency".

### Gaps
- No independent, quantified evaluation of current models on Unreal gameplay C++ was found.
- Verse being limited to UEFN (Fortnite) is stated in the brief and matches my knowledge, but was not verified here.
- RunUAT `BuildCookRun`, UnrealBuildTool command lines and Gauntlet multiplayer test examples were not read from documentation; I know them to exist as the standard command-line build route.
- Whether the Epic Claude Code plugin works against 5.8 or needs 5.9 is not stated on its page.
- AngelScript's supported engine versions, source-build requirement and licence were not on the page read.

## 9. Shipped Unreal co-op action games by small teams, and their netcode

### Takeaway
There is clear precedent for small teams shipping 3 to 6 player Unreal co-op action games, including procedurally generated ones, and where hosting is documented it is player-hosted, sometimes with an optional dedicated server tool. Public technical detail on their netcode is thin, and I found almost none for melee-centred games.

### Cited Findings
- Deep Rock Galactic (Ghost Ship Games): Unreal Engine 4, built "with the engine's Blueprint scripting for quick iteration"; studio founded in 2016 by five former Press Play staff; early access 28 February 2018, full release 13 May 2020; up to four players; procedurally generated destructible caves; over 8 million units by January 2024. Reviews noted connection problems could make other players lag and disconnect — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Rock_Galactic)
- Ghost Ship's lead programmer credited engine source access for letting the team tell their bugs from engine bugs and fix them (snippet) — [Epic developer interview](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/developer-interviews/guns-gold-and-glory-in-the-caverns-of-deep-rock-galactic)
- Skylorn: Bound Together: solo developer in spare time for "the past couple of years", UE 5.4.4, listen server, up to 3 players, released on Steam 22 October 2025 — [Epic forum thread](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/skylorn-bound-together-sci-fi-open-world-co-op-action-rpg/2656118)
- Remnant 2 (Gunfire Games): began on Unreal Engine 4 and moved to Unreal Engine 5 around 2021; procedural generation randomises layouts and campaigns; the first game's team averaged around 30 people (snippets) — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/features/making-remnant-2-from-the-ashes); [Epic developer interview, 2019](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/developer-interviews/remnant-from-the-ashes-blends-genres-to-become-a-unique-co-op-shooter)
- Gunfire redesigned melee in Remnant because "the game is supposed to be played like a co-op shooter", with shooting primary — [MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/business-news/consoles/when-we-made-remnant-2/)
- Abiotic Factor (Deep Field Games): hosting guides describe peer-hosted co-op that runs only while the host plays, plus a separate dedicated server tool on Steam and cross-play by lobby code or server browser (vendor snippets, which disagree on a 6 or 8 player cap) — [Supercraft wiki](https://supercraft.host/wiki/abiotic-factor/how_to_play_coop_dedicated_vs_online/)
- Palworld: invite co-op for four players that ends when the host leaves, and dedicated servers for up to 32 (vendor snippet) — [XGamingServer](https://xgamingserver.com/blog/palworld-dedicated-server-vs-co-op/)
- Night of the Dead: two-person Unreal team; a developer said the engine's default network setup "can be a massive time-saver" (snippet) — [Epic developer interview](https://www.unrealengine.com/developer-interviews/learn-how-a-team-of-two-are-developing-indie-sensation-night-of-the-dead)
- It Takes Two and Split Fiction (Hazelight) shipped as Unreal co-op games with gameplay mostly in AngelScript — [Hazelight AngelScript](https://angelscript.hazelight.se/)
- Subnautica 2 has an Epic Online Services case study (title only; the page required a login) — [Epic Online Services interview](https://onlineservices.epicgames.com/interviews/diving-into-the-depths-of-epic-online-services-for-subnautica-2)

### Inferences
- The pattern across these games matches the proposed architecture: player-hosted sessions for small groups, dedicated servers as a later extra for persistent worlds. A run-based gate game has less need for persistence than survival games.
- The documented successes lean on shooting. Remnant's own director describes steering combat toward shooting in co-op. Melee-first co-op in Unreal (Lords of the Fallen, Wo Long style games) comes from larger teams, which supports the view that networked melee is the expensive part.
- Deep Rock Galactic is the closest structural analogue (four players, seeded caves, mission runs from a hub, small team, Blueprint-heavy), and it took five experienced developers two years to reach early access and four to reach 1.0.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found for: Deep Rock Galactic's hosting model (I understand it to be player-hosted listen servers on Steam, unconfirmed), Lords of the Fallen (2023) seamless co-op netcode, Satisfactory, Grounded, or Palworld's engine and netcode details.
- Abiotic Factor's team size ("around ten") and engine version came from an unattributed search summary; no technical interview on its networking was found.
- Epic's own developer interviews (unrealengine.com) could not be opened, so quotes from them are snippet-level.

## 10. Rebuild effort for a solo developer and what carries over

### Takeaway
The available data points put a solo or tiny-team Unreal co-op action game at about two years of spare-time work for a modest 3-player game and two to four years for experienced small teams; none of them involve an AI-agent-led workflow, so there is no direct benchmark for this owner. The designs, data and algorithms in the JavaScript carry over; the renderer, input, HUD, figures and poses do not.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Carries over almost directly (as C++ data or logic): the move, class and enemy tables and their numbers; the player and enemy state machines and per-type hook design; pursuit, pack and territory rules; boss phase logic such as the crocodile's modes; the cave and sewer generators (graph planning, field shaping, marching squares, water layer, spawn placement); the seed-hash regression tests as a concept.
- Carries over as design only: lock-on, camera rules, posture and execution rules, input buffering, touch layout ideas.
- Replaced by engine features: the WebGL renderer and shaders, lights selection, visibility pieces, collision through the field (now physics collision plus navmesh), routing, audio, input and binding menus, pad handling, HUD.
- Must be created new: skeletal characters and enemies with animation sets (the largest gap, since current figures are primitives posed in code), montages with hit windows, materials, effects, UI assets, all networking (authority split, replication of every state the 19,000 lines currently keep in one `G` object, prediction for dodge, parry and hits), lobbies and invites, and a build and release pipeline for Windows.
- Single-player-to-multiplayer is itself a rewrite in any engine: every system that today reads one global state must be split into server-authoritative state, owner-predicted state and cosmetic state. Unreal provides the mechanisms, not the split.
- A cautious planning figure for a solo developer new to Unreal, working with agents: several months to learn the engine and reach a networked vertical slice (one class, a few enemies, one generated gate, two players), and one to two years or more to reach the current game's breadth with co-op. This is an estimate from the data points above, not a sourced figure.
- The learning curve has three separate parts: the editor and asset pipeline, Unreal's C++ dialect and gameplay framework (actors, components, GAS), and network programming. The owner's current workflow touches none of them.

## 11. Main risks, and the reasons for and against Unreal for this game as a solo developer in 2026

### Takeaway
Unreal offers the most complete built-in stack for a networked third-person action game and a clear commercial path on PC and consoles, but for this owner it trades away the browser and phone reach, a Mac-friendly seconds-long test loop and an all-text codebase, and it leaves the hardest problem, fair-feeling co-op melee, unsolved.

### Cited Findings
- For: server-authoritative replication, RPCs, relevancy and a predicted character controller are built in, and listen servers are documented as suited to small co-op groups — [Epic networking overview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/networking-overview-for-unreal-engine); [Epic CMC networking doc](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/understanding-networked-movement-in-the-character-movement-component-for-unreal-engine)

- For: free relays, lobbies, voice and cross-play through EOS and Steam — [Epic Online Services overview](https://onlineservices.epicgames.com/llms.txt); [Valve SDR doc](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/multiplayer/steamdatagramrelay)

- Against: generated dynamic meshes lose Nanite and distance-field features, so the engine's headline rendering gives this game little — [Epic Geometry Scripting guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/geometry-scripting-users-guide-in-unreal-engine)

### Inferences
- Risk ranking for this owner, highest first:
  1. Workflow mismatch. The owner builds through agents and headless tests on text; Unreal's centre of gravity is a GUI editor with binary assets and minute-scale loops. The 2026 MCP tooling narrows this but is experimental.
  2. Content. Unreal combat presumes animated skeletal characters. The project has had "little art" as a founding constraint; buying and retargeting animation packs and authoring montages is new, mostly manual work.
  3. Networked melee feel. The engine does not solve it; it is design and engineering work regardless of engine, but Unreal's root-motion and GAS conventions add their own pitfalls.
  4. Platform loss. No browser link, no casual phone play, large downloads, and Windows builds from a Mac need extra infrastructure.
  5. Engine churn and size. Two releases a year, experimental successors to core systems (Mover, Iris, UAF, Online Services), and Mac regressions.
  6. Scope. The reference points for this genre in Unreal are teams of five to thirty over two to four years.
- Reasons experienced developers would give for choosing it anyway: the commercial target (Steam, later consoles) is Unreal's home ground; replication and the character controller save months against writing netcode; the animation toolset is the best available for weighty melee; the samples (Lyra, Game Animation Sample) and Fab kits give working starting points; and source access lets a team diagnose engine behaviour.
- Reasons they would give against it for a solo developer: the engine's breadth demands specialists it assumes a team has; iteration is slow; Blueprint-centred community material fits poorly with code-first work; and a stylised, generated, low-art game uses little of what makes Unreal heavy.
- A middle path worth stating for the report: Unreal becomes more attractive if the owner decides the game is a PC and console product with bought or commissioned character art and animation, and less attractive if instant browser play, phones and an agent-driven text workflow are to be kept.
