## 1. Models: listen server, dedicated server, distributed authority, lockstep, rollback

### Takeaway
For 4 to 5 player PvE the vendor documentation and the shipped-game evidence point the same way: a player-hosted (listen server) session where the host owns the world and AI, with each client given authority over its own character where that keeps the game responsive, validated only loosely. Lockstep and rollback buy bandwidth and consistency that this game does not need and cost bit-exact determinism that a 19,000-line JavaScript simulation does not have.

### Cited Findings
- **Listen server, definition and fit.** Unreal: a listen server is "a server that also has a local player who is hosting the game"; the host has "an advantage over the players who are playing as clients" and carries extra processing load; listen servers suit casual cooperative or competitive play in small groups and are "less suitable for games in highly competitive settings or games with very high network loads". Dedicated servers are "more expensive and challenging to configure". — [Unreal Engine networking overview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/networking-overview-for-unreal-engine)
- **Listen server, costs and limits.** Unity: host architecture is "essentially free compared to dedicated server options", recommended for smaller groups (fewer than 12 players) and games without a persistent world; drawbacks are dependence on the host's home connection and machine, a latency edge for the host, easier cheating for the host (it sees all world state), and "the game ceases to exist when the host player leaves the game". — [Unity Netcode for GameObjects: listen server host architecture](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/listenserverhostarchitecture.html)
- **Connectivity for player hosting.** Port forwarding "may not be a viable option for a released game"; NAT punch-through "often fails" depending on NAT types; punch-through with relay fallback is "widely used" because it lowers relay cost; a relay always works but costs money and adds round-trip time. — [Unity: listen server host architecture](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/listenserverhostarchitecture.html)
- **Server authority versus client authority.** Under server authority a client must "wait for a full RTT" to see an input's effect; under client authority "any malicious player can forge messages". Soft validation (server does only basic checks such as range) is "acceptable for most PvE games", while PvP "usually require[s] server authority". Owner authority avoids race conditions; shared objects need an ownership request first. — [Unity: dealing with latency](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/dealing-with-latency.html)
- **Why authoritative servers exist at all.** "Don't trust the player": the server holds the real state and clients send inputs; a naive implementation "may make games quite unresponsive". — [Gabriel Gambetta, Client-Server Game Architecture](https://www.gabrielgambetta.com/client-server-game-architecture.html) (page undated; copyright line 2026; the content is the long-standing standard text)
- **Deterministic lockstep.** Only inputs are sent, so bandwidth is independent of object count ("a physics simulation of one million objects with the same bandwidth as just one"), but results must match "down to the bit level", and inputs play out through a delay buffer (100 ms in the demo). Fiedler's companion article recommends lockstep for "2-4 players at most". — [Gaffer on Games, Deterministic Lockstep, Nov 2014](https://gafferongames.com/post/deterministic_lockstep/); [Gaffer on Games, Snapshot Interpolation, Nov 2014](https://gafferongames.com/post/snapshot_interpolation/)
- **Lockstep feel.** "If one player is lagging - all of the players lag"; Diablo 1 found "players didn't hate the delay as long as it was the same delay every time". — [Rune, Modern Game Networking Models, July 2024](https://developers.rune.ai/blog/modern-game-networking-models)
- **Lockstep in an action RPG.** Path of Exile added a lockstep mode in 2015 so that "monsters are always exactly where you see them"; the cost is that your action waits for your ping, noticeable at around 100 ms, and the older predictive mode was kept for higher pings. — [PCGamesN, May 2015](https://www.pcgamesn.com/path-of-exile/path-of-exile-s-new-lockstep-mode-banishes-desync-problems). The auto mode switches between the two at about 150 ms. — search-result snippet of [Path of Exile wiki, Networking mode](https://poewiki.net/wiki/Desync)
- **Rollback (predict and re-simulate).** Responsive and compatible with an authoritative server, and "lag is limited to the player with the bad connection", but "it's hard to implement", rollbacks cause CPU spikes, and the render layer must hide corrections. Rune calls it "more and more the standard approach". — [Rune, July 2024](https://developers.rune.ai/blog/modern-game-networking-models)
- **Rollback as a product.** Photon Quantum is a deterministic predict/rollback engine: clients "only exchange player input", mispredictions are fixed by "restoring game state and re-simulating", it targets up to 128 players and latency-sensitive genres, and it requires a pure deterministic C# ECS simulation with its own deterministic (fixed point) maths, no heap allocation and all state in one frame object. — [Photon Quantum introduction](https://doc.photonengine.com/quantum/current/quantum-intro)
- **Distributed authority in shipped games.** Destiny has each console simulate its whole "bubble"; a physics host is authoritative for damage while each console is authoritative for its own character's "movement and abilities", to make the game "feel like a single-player shooter" (fan write-up of Bungie's design). — [nessus42 Destiny networking write-up](https://gist.github.com/nessus42/17c433143c68bc4500c5637616557e86). Valheim hands "simulation of an area to the first client that enters said area", and "if the player in charge of the area has a poor connection all other players in that area will suffer". — [Serverside Simulations mod page](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/mvp/Serverside_Simulations/)
- **Dedicated servers for 4-player PvE have a feel cost.** Darktide moved to dedicated servers; a February 2025 player thread on Fatshark's forum lists "dodges failing to work in times when needed" and "block cancels not working" and complains that "all player inputs (except camera movement)" depend on latency. No Fatshark reply in the thread. — [Fatshark forums, Feb 2025](https://forums.fatsharkgames.com/t/server-authoritative-gameplay-is-terrible-and-needs-to-go/105184)
- **Host authority over a client's dodge is the complaint in a co-op Soulslike.** Lords of the Fallen (2023) players report that the game "essentially turned the host into a server" and that perfect dodges on the client's screen "did not matter"; one contrasts this with Dark Souls and Elden Ring, where the host "does not have a total say in everything". Player opinions, no developer source. — [Steam discussion, Nov 2023](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1501750/discussions/0/3934517778349929251)
- **A cheap dedicated component is possible.** Destiny's cloud "activity host" runs only script state (spawn status, enemy counts, objectives) at 10 Hz; about 5,000 instances fit on one 40-core server. — [Edgegap, Destiny 2 network architecture](https://edgegap.com/blog/destiny-2-s-network-achitecture---multiplayer-game-deep-dive)

### Inferences
Comparison for this game (my synthesis of the sources above):

| Model | Feel | Cheating exposure | Running cost | Host leaving | Effort for this codebase |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Listen server, host fully authoritative | Host perfect; clients feel RTT on dodge and parry unless predicted | Host can cheat freely | Relay traffic only | Session ends unless migration is built | Medium |
| Listen server, host owns world and AI, each client owns its character and its own "was I hit" | Every player close to single-player feel | Any client can cheat about itself; host about the world | Relay traffic only | Same as above | Medium; the least rework of combat code |
| Dedicated authoritative server | Everyone feels RTT unless full prediction is built; consistent | Lowest | Server fleet per session | No host to leave | High (prediction and reconciliation of the whole player state machine), plus operations |
| Full peer-to-peer distributed authority (zone or entity owners) | Good near the owner, bad when the owner lags | Everyone trusted | Relay traffic only | Ownership moves, so no single point | High; ownership hand-off bugs |
| Deterministic lockstep | Input delay equal to the worst ping; dodge timing shifts | Clients trusted, but state cannot be forged, only inputs | Lowest bandwidth | Any peer can continue | Very high: bit-exact determinism across browsers |
| Rollback (GGPO or Quantum style) | Best when it works | Same as lockstep | Low bandwidth, high CPU | Any peer can continue | Highest: determinism plus save, restore and re-simulate of all state |

- The recommendation that keeps recurring for co-op PvE is "authoritative host for the world, generous trust of each client about itself". Unity says so directly (soft validation for PvE), Destiny shipped it at scale, and the two negative examples (Darktide, Lords of the Fallen) are complaints about a remote authority overruling a local dodge.
- Rollback is not ruled out by player count (Quantum targets up to 128), but it is ruled out by what it demands of the simulation. DungeonRaze uses floats and `Math` functions throughout and was never written to be deterministic or to snapshot and restore all state.

### Gaps
- No source ranks these models specifically for melee PvE with numbers; the ranking above is inference from documentation and from shipped designs.
- I found no first-party statement from Fatshark on why Darktide chose dedicated servers; only a community post paraphrasing it (see section 7).

## 2. How shipped co-op action games do it: who decides hits, who runs AI

### Takeaway
Player hosting dominates the genre (Souls games, Monster Hunter by community account, Vermintide 2, Helldivers 2, Lethal Company, Valheim's zone owners, Destiny's bubbles). Developer-published detail on who decides a hit exists only for Bungie's games; for FromSoftware, Capcom, Ghost Ship, Hopoo and Gunfire the evidence is player reports and mod descriptions, so the "attacker decides hits on enemies, victim decides hits on themselves" picture is well supported as a design pattern but only weakly documented per title.

### Cited Findings
- **Destiny / Destiny 2 (Bungie).** The physics host is authoritative for damage; a console shows its own damage and movement immediately and is authoritative for its own character's "movement and abilities"; the two reconcile periodically. Failure modes: lost damage packets make enemies appear to heal or refuse to die; delayed host updates make enemies teleport or seem immortal. — [nessus42 write-up](https://gist.github.com/nessus42/17c433143c68bc4500c5637616557e86) (fan write-up based on Bungie's talk). The source talk is "Shared World Shooter: Destiny's Networked Mission Architecture", GDC 2015 (Justin Truman). Damage from everyone is "accumulated by the Physics Host at 30 ticks", and Bungie judged Destiny "too latent for cooperative lockstep", the model Halo Reach co-op used. — search-result snippets of related write-ups, see [Edgegap](https://edgegap.com/blog/destiny-2-s-network-achitecture---multiplayer-game-deep-dive) for the part I read (activity host in the cloud at 10 Hz, some raid boss logic on clients, the Crota exploit blamed on unaudited client-side raid scripts).
- **Halo Reach (Bungie, GDC 2011, "I Shot You First", David Aldridge).** Host-authoritative with three channels: state (eventual consistency), events (unreliable context) and control data (about 20 bits per player of sub-sampled inputs for prediction). Per-client priority by distance, screen presence, threat and recent interaction. Over 2,000 synced entities in a 16-player match in 250 kbps against a 384 kbps goal. — [Edgegap summary of the talk](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-halo-reach-netcode-host-migration). Bungie's stated view that "trades are more fair and cause less anguish than seemingly arbitrary decisions about who won a fight based on who has the least lag" — search-result snippet of [Wolfire's GDC session summary, March 2011](https://wolfire-blog.appspot.com/2011/03/GDC-Session-Summary-Halo-networking) (page returned 404 when opened).
- **Valheim (Iron Gate).** No central simulation: the first client into an area owns and simulates its terrain, monsters and objects; a laggy owner makes "monsters jump around, hits land late" for everyone. Mods that move simulation to the dedicated server do it "at the cost of increased latency for the client which would ordinarily own the area", and it "does not prevent cheating or any kind of client manipulation". — [Serverside Simulations](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/mvp/Serverside_Simulations/) and search-result snippets of its fork, [Dedicated Simulation](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/Sarkastic_gg/Dedicated_Simulation/v/1.29.1/)
- **Vermintide 2 and Darktide (Fatshark).** Vermintide 2 missions run on a listen server hosted by one player and the game has host migration; Darktide runs on dedicated servers, private 2 to 4 player lobbies included; a community post gives Fatshark's reasons as avoiding the host leaving and ending the game, and inconsistent ping, at the price of no offline play. — search-result snippets of [Fatshark forum thread](https://forums.fatsharkgames.com/t/we-need-servers-like-vermintide-2/112835). Player view that Vermintide 2 was closer to client-driven while "defensive mechanics are still tied to latency". — [Fatshark forums, Feb 2025](https://forums.fatsharkgames.com/t/server-authoritative-gameplay-is-terrible-and-needs-to-go/105184)
- **Elden Ring / Dark Souls (FromSoftware).** Peer-to-peer since Demon's Souls by community account, sharing netcode across titles: a modder said before release that Elden Ring had the same netcode vulnerabilities as Dark Souls 3. — search-result snippet of [PC Gamer, Feb 2022](https://pcgamer.com/modder-says-elden-ring-may-be-vulnerable-to-the-same-security-flaws-as-dark-souls). Players report a 0.3 to 0.5 s delay between landing a hit on another player and seeing the reaction, and say hit detection is client-side. — search-result snippet of [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1245620/discussions/0/3758850346171791546). A Dark Souls 3 PvP netcode patch mod describes fixing "higher latency increasing the effective invulnerability window of rolls". — search-result snippet of [Nexus Mods, DS3 mod 1021](https://www.nexusmods.com/darksouls3/mods/1021) (page refused a direct read). The Seamless Co-op mod is "an overhaul to Elden Ring's peer-to-peer connection system, using Steam's newer networking API". — search-result snippet of [Nexus Mods, Seamless Co-op](https://www.nexusmods.com/eldenring/mods/510)
- **Elden Ring Nightreign.** Third-party guides describe one player as host with the others connected through that link, and a player reports "The current session cannot be restored" on trying to rejoin. Vendor guides with a commercial interest and forum posts only; one forum post claims the opposite (client-server). — search-result snippets of [ExitLag guide](https://www.exitlag.com/blog/nightreign-server-issues/) and [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2622380/discussions/0/501685815345171977)
- **Lords of the Fallen (2023).** Players describe the host as a full authority and clients' dodges being overruled. — [Steam discussion, Nov 2023](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1501750/discussions/0/3934517778349929251)
- **Helldivers 2 (Arrowhead).** The search tool surfaced a January 2024 Reddit comment attributed to Arrowhead's technical director saying the game uses a peer-to-peer model, and that Helldivers 1 did too, where "each game client blindly trusts the others". I could not read Reddit, so this is unverified; a vendor guide says in-mission latency depends on the host's home connection. — search-result snippet of [ExitLag guide](https://www.exitlag.com/blog/helldivers-2-lag/)
- **Lethal Company.** Player-hosted on Unity Netcode for GameObjects by community account; when the host leaves the session ends and is not handed over. — search-result snippet of [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1966720/discussions/0/4038103682226821807/); general rule in [Unity's docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/listenserverhostarchitecture.html)
- **Risk of Rain Returns (Hopoo, the 2D game, not Risk of Rain 2).** Patch 1.0.4 made "most enemy attacks" client authoritative and added client prediction to some online interactions. — search-result snippet of [GoNintendo patch notes](https://gonintendo.com/contents/30787-risk-of-rain-returns-updated-to-ver-1-0-4)
- **Monster Hunter World.** Players describe matchmaking on Capcom's servers with quests hosted on a player's machine; other players dispute it. No Capcom source. — search-result snippet of [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/582010/discussions/0/1745594817439932072)
- **No Rest for the Wicked (Moon Studios).** Co-op is four players on a "persistent, shared realm" that "remembers you" and changes while you are offline; the update shipped 22 January 2026 and outlets describe realms as not needing a host online. The developer post says nothing about netcode. — [Moon Studios, Nov 2025](https://norestforthewicked.com/news/being-wicked-together); search-result snippet of [GamingOnLinux, Jan 2026](https://gamingonlinux.com/2026/01/no-rest-for-the-wicked-co-op-update-lands-on-january-22-and-it-hit-a-big-sales-milestone)
- **Diablo-style ARPGs.** Path of Exile is server-authoritative with a choice of lockstep or predictive client modes. — [PCGamesN, 2015](https://www.pcgamesn.com/path-of-exile/path-of-exile-s-new-lockstep-mode-banishes-desync-problems)
- **Unity's Boss Room co-op sample.** Server-authoritative: clients send inputs, the server sends positions, so "every feature in the world [is] on the same world time"; clients play an anticipatory animation while waiting. The docs concede it "might have benefited from using client authority", but mixing client-driven players with server-driven AI "can easily have become a mess". — [Unity: dealing with latency](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/dealing-with-latency.html); [Boss Room actions](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/samples/bossroom/bossroom-actions.html)

## 3. Prediction, reconciliation, interpolation and lag compensation: the recipe and how much a PvE game needs

### Takeaway
The standard recipe is four parts: predict the local player and reconcile against the server, show everything else slightly in the past by interpolating between snapshots, and rewind the world on the server to judge shots. A co-op PvE game that lets each client own its character needs only the second part in full (snapshot interpolation of remote players and enemies) plus light validation; prediction with replay and server rewind are mostly replaced by "the owner says what happened".

### Cited Findings
- **Client-side prediction and reconciliation.** Apply each input locally at once, number it, keep unacknowledged inputs, and on each server state (which carries the last processed input number) reset to the server's value and replay the inputs after it. Irreversible events such as death should wait for the server. — [Gambetta, Client-Side Prediction and Server Reconciliation](https://www.gabrielgambetta.com/client-side-prediction-server-reconciliation.html)
- **Entity interpolation.** With 10 updates a second other entities are drawn about 100 ms in the past, between two known states: "show the other players in the past relative to the user's player". Dead reckoning only suits entities that cannot change direction abruptly. — [Gambetta, Entity Interpolation](https://www.gabrielgambetta.com/entity-interpolation.html)
- **Lag compensation.** The client sends the shot with its timestamp; the server rebuilds the world as that client saw it and judges the hit there. The cost falls on the target, who "may still be shot up to a few milliseconds after they took cover". — [Gambetta, Lag Compensation](https://www.gabrielgambetta.com/lag-compensation.html)
- **Interpolation buffer sizing.** For 2 to 5% packet loss the delay that works best is three send intervals: 350 ms at 10 packets a second (with jitter allowance), 150 ms at 30, 85 ms at 60. Extrapolating 200 ms "performed poorly". Hermite interpolation fixes the artefacts of linear interpolation at 10 Hz; "linear interpolation is good enough at 60HZ". — [Gaffer on Games, Snapshot Interpolation, 2014](https://gafferongames.com/post/snapshot_interpolation/); [Snapshot Compression, 2015](https://gafferongames.com/post/snapshot_compression/)
- **Overwatch (Tim Ford, GDC 2017, "Overwatch Gameplay Architecture and Netcode").** Fixed 16 ms command frames (7 ms in tournament configuration); the client runs ahead of the server by half the round trip plus one buffered frame (about 96 ms at 160 ms RTT); every packet carries all inputs since the last acknowledged one; a misprediction rolls back to the server snapshot and replays; when the server starves for input it repeats the last input and tells the client to speed up slightly (a 16 ms frame treated as about 15.2 ms). For shots the server "rewinds the world back to the shooter's frame of reference"; above about 220 ms RTT hit prediction is disabled. — [Edgegap summary](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-overwatch-2016-netcode-architecture-rollback); talk listing at [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024001/-Overwatch-Gameplay-Architecture-and)
- **Valorant.** 128 Hz server and client simulation; half a frame of movement buffered on the server and one frame on the client; rewind for shots is capped per region because "a player with 500ms latency could kill you a half second after you'd moved behind cover". — [Riot Games, Peeking into VALORANT's Netcode, July 2020](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/peeking-valorants-netcode)
- **Rocket League (Jared Cone, GDC 2018, "It IS Rocket Science! The Physics of Rocket League Detailed")** covers networked physics with prediction of all cars and the ball. — [GDC Vault listing](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024972/It-IS-Rocket-Science-The) (listing only; content not read)
- **Unity's menu of cheaper patterns.** Client authority for low-impact things ("Can the server correct me on this?"), client-side prediction, **controlled desync** ("as long as the clients end in the same state and [don't] accumulate discrepancies, your players will be fooled"), **action anticipation** (play animation, effects and sound at once; "the worst that happens client side is you've played a useless quick animation"), and server-side rewind, which favours the attacker because it is "way more frustrating for an attacker to always miss". — [Unity: dealing with latency](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/dealing-with-latency.html)
- **Transport.** For time-critical streams TCP "behaves terribly under bad network conditions" (hitches every few seconds at 100 ms latency and 1% loss); Fiedler's alternative is UDP with every unacknowledged input resent in each packet. — [Gaffer on Games, Deterministic Lockstep](https://gafferongames.com/post/deterministic_lockstep/)

### Inferences
- What a 4 to 5 player PvE game with owner-authoritative characters needs from the recipe:
  - **Needed in full:** snapshot interpolation with a jitter buffer for remote players and all enemies; redundant unreliable sends for state; a reliable ordered channel for events (deaths, executions, terrain changes, loot).
  - **Needed lightly:** validation on the host of what clients claim (range, rate, cooldown, line of sight within a tolerance).
  - **Replaced:** prediction with replay is not needed for movement if the client owns its position; lag-compensating rewind is not needed if the attacker's client judges its own hits against the enemies it sees and the host accepts them.
  - **Still worth predicting locally:** the visible result of your own hits (flash, damage number, hit-stop, the enemy's flinch) before the host confirms, corrected if the host disagrees. This is Unity's anticipation and controlled desync applied to melee.
- These sources are 6 to 12 years old (Gaffer 2014 to 2015, Halo 2011, Overwatch 2017, Valorant 2020). They are still what current engine documentation teaches (Unity's page is current), so they hold.

### Gaps
- Valve's Source Multiplayer Networking page and Bernier's "Latency Compensating Methods" paper could not be read (403). The commonly quoted Source defaults (a 100 ms interpolation delay, about 20 to 30 snapshots a second, one second of lag-compensation history) are from memory and unverified here.
- The Overwatch and Rocket League talks were read only through summaries or listings.

## 4. Timing-critical mechanics under latency

### Takeaway
The known fixes all move the decision to the machine of the player whose reflexes are being tested, and make everything else cosmetic or tolerant: the victim's client judges dodge, parry and block against the attack as it saw it; binding animations (executions, grabs) are blended in visually over about half a second or more; and time effects stay local. Published tolerances suggest that corrections under roughly 150 to 200 ms go unnoticed by most players.

### Cited Findings
- **Favour the acting player and prefer trades to arbitrary winners.** Server rewind "favors the attacker" in shooters. — [Unity: dealing with latency](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/dealing-with-latency.html). Bungie preferred trades to deciding "who won a fight based on who has the least lag". — search-result snippet of [Wolfire GDC summary, 2011](https://wolfire-blog.appspot.com/2011/03/GDC-Session-Summary-Halo-networking)
- **Victim-side judgement in practice.** Destiny treats each console as authoritative for its own movement and abilities. — [nessus42 write-up](https://gist.github.com/nessus42/17c433143c68bc4500c5637616557e86). Risk of Rain Returns made most enemy attacks client authoritative. — search-result snippet of [GoNintendo](https://gonintendo.com/contents/30787-risk-of-rain-returns-updated-to-ver-1-0-4). In Dark Souls 3, latency lengthened the effective invulnerability of rolls. — search-result snippet of [Nexus Mods](https://www.nexusmods.com/darksouls3/mods/1021)
- **What goes wrong without it.** Remote authority over the dodge is the stated complaint in Darktide ("dodges failing to work in times when needed") and Lords of the Fallen (perfect dodges that "did not matter"). — [Fatshark forums](https://forums.fatsharkgames.com/t/server-authoritative-gameplay-is-terrible-and-needs-to-go/105184); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1501750/discussions/0/3934517778349929251). In Souls PvP, parries "must often be made before your opponent even begins to swing" under lag. — search-result snippet of [Fextralife Dark Souls combat guide](https://darksouls.wiki.fextralife.com/COMBAT)
- **Defensive ability that must start on time (Halo Reach armor lock).** After two failed iterations, the host activates the shield early by the measured host-to-client round trip, so the shield is up when the client expects it. — [Edgegap summary of Aldridge, GDC 2011](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-halo-reach-netcode-host-migration)
- **Two-entity binding (Halo Reach assassination).** A visual-only blend of about 0.75 s absorbs the position disagreement between the two machines, hidden by pulling the camera out to third person. — [Edgegap summary](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-halo-reach-netcode-host-migration)
- **Predicted action, late confirmation (Halo Reach grenades).** The throw animation is predicted on the button press and the grenade is created on the release frame after the host confirms; gaps up to 150 ms go unnoticed by most players and up to 200 ms by casual players. — [Edgegap summary](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-halo-reach-netcode-host-migration)
- **Anticipation animation.** Boss Room plays the melee animation on the owner at once to "hide network latency" while the server decides. — [Unity Boss Room actions](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/samples/bossroom/bossroom-actions.html)
- **Ownership follows the interacting player.** Unity: owner authority avoids races, and a client must request ownership of a shared object from the server. — [Unity: dealing with latency](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/dealing-with-latency.html). Valheim's server-simulation mod still leaves a ship simulated by its driver, to keep steering responsive at high latency. — [Serverside Simulations](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/mvp/Serverside_Simulations/)
- **Cosmetic divergence is acceptable.** Halo Reach syncs only a ragdoll's initial state and lets it diverge. — [Edgegap summary](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-halo-reach-netcode-host-migration). Unity's "controlled desync" states the rule. — [Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/dealing-with-latency.html)
- **Prediction breaks on state the client cannot foresee.** A stun arriving 200 ms late is Unity's example of a mismatch. — [Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/dealing-with-latency.html)

### Inferences
Mapping onto DungeonRaze's mechanics (my design reasoning; the project already routes all damage to the player through `hurtPlayer` and all damage to enemies through `hitEnemy`, which are the natural seams):

- **Dodge invulnerability, parry, block, guard break (0.15 to 0.3 s windows).** Run `hurtPlayer` only on the victim's client. The host runs the enemy, broadcasts "enemy E started attack A at host time T", and does not itself decide the outcome against a remote player. Each client plays the telegraph, tests the hit against its own player at the active frame, and reports the result (hit for N, perfect dodge, parry, block, guard break). The host applies consequences that belong to the enemy (posture from a parry, stagger, recoil) when the report arrives. The player gets the full window at any latency. The cost is that the enemy's reaction to a parry is seen by the others, and applied on the host, roughly one round trip plus the interpolation delay late.
- **Telegraph alignment.** A client sees a host-run enemy late by half the round trip plus its interpolation buffer. Because wind-ups are data (`wind`, `act`, `rec` in seconds), a client can start a wind-up late and play it slightly faster so that the active frame lands at the host's time. This is the same idea as Halo's early armor lock (shift the start by the measured latency) applied in the other direction. It keeps players on different pings roughly in step, and it needs checking by eye that a compressed telegraph still reads.
- **Enemy aim at a remote player is stale** by about a round trip plus the buffer, so tracking attacks will miss a moving client slightly more often than they miss the host. That favours the defender and is the right side to err on in PvE.
- **Hit-stop and slow motion.** `G.hitstop` and `G.slowT` are global time effects today. In co-op they cannot stop or slow the shared simulation. Hit-stop becomes local and cosmetic: freeze the attacker's and the target's poses on the attacker's screen for a few frames and let them catch up (controlled desync). Slow motion on a perfect dodge either becomes a local presentation effect (camera, sound, a brief pose slow-down of the dodged enemy only) or is dropped in co-op; a true shared slow-down would slow every other player.
- **Player hits on enemies.** The attacker's client decides, against the enemies as it sees them, shows the result at once and sends the hit; the host applies damage and posture and is the only one that kills. The host should accept hits a little late and a little out of range, and resolve conflicts in the player's favour (a hit that arrives on an enemy that died is dropped; an enemy that died on the host but still stands on a client is removed on the next snapshot).
- **Executions.** The host must arbitrate, because two players can press Execute on the same staggered enemy. The requester starts an anticipation move at once; the host grants to the first request, marks the enemy as claimed, and the others' requests fall through to a normal attack. Positions are blended over the first part of the animation, as Halo does over 0.75 s. Because the enemy is frozen by the execution anyway, giving the executing client temporary authority over that enemy's pose for the duration removes the remaining mismatch.
- **Enemy grabs (`grabPlayer`, the siren's drag, the grabber, the crocodile's death roll).** The victim's client decides whether the grab landed (it is the one that knows about dodge invulnerability and grace time), then drags its own player, since it owns its position; the host puts the enemy in `hold` on the report and moves the enemy to follow the player's reported position rather than the reverse. Mashing free and the release are decided by the victim and reported. The host needs a timeout so that a client that goes silent cannot leave an enemy in `hold` for ever.
- **Knockback** on the player is applied by the victim's client as part of the hit it accepted. Knockback on enemies is applied by the host on the hit event; the attacker's client may show a predicted shove.
- **Area effects that hit both sides** (the bloater's cloud, the eel's shock, falling ceiling chunks): the host owns their position and timing, each client tests its own player, the host tests the enemies.

### Gaps
- I found no published write-up that describes victim-side parry judgement for a shipped Soulslike in the developer's own words; the pattern is inferred from Destiny, a Risk of Rain Returns patch note, a Dark Souls 3 mod description and Unity's guidance.
- No source on how any shipped co-op game handles hit-stop or slow motion in multiplayer. The local-only treatment is my inference from the "controlled desync" principle.
- Overwatch is often said to make exceptions to "favour the shooter" when the target used a dodge ability; I found only forum claims for this (and one 403), so it is not cited.

## 5. Enemy AI in co-op

### Takeaway
One machine runs each enemy, and in small-lobby games it is the host (or the area's owner). Party-size scaling is well documented for two reference games and is done with multipliers on enemy count and on the health of large enemies and bosses, not on small enemies. I found no sourced description of threat tables or attack-token schemes in the named games.

### Cited Findings
- **AI authority.** Destiny: the physics host is the reference for enemy state and damage. — [nessus42 write-up](https://gist.github.com/nessus42/17c433143c68bc4500c5637616557e86). Valheim: the area's owning client simulates its monsters. — [Serverside Simulations](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/mvp/Serverside_Simulations/). Unity's Boss Room: server-driven AI, and mixing it with client-driven players needs care. — [Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/dealing-with-latency.html)
- **Risk of Rain 2 scaling by party size.** `playerFactor = 1 + 0.3 × (players − 1)`; `timeFactor = 0.0506 × difficulty × players^0.2`; `coeff = (playerFactor + minutes × timeFactor) × 1.15^stages`. Spawn credits and prices follow `coeff`; each enemy level adds 30% health and 20% damage. Special bosses: `HP = base × (1 + coeff/2.5) × livingPlayers` (rounded to a tenth), damage `× (1 + coeff/30)`. Worked example on the page: four players at ten minutes on the middle difficulty gives a boss health multiplier of about 9.2 and a damage multiplier of 1.1. The player count includes disconnected players. — [Risk of Rain 2 wiki, Difficulty](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Difficulty)
- **Deep Rock Galactic scaling by party size.** At Hazard 3, enemy count is ×0.50 / 0.50 / 0.80 / 1.10 for 1 to 4 players; small enemies keep the same health multiplier at every party size (1.10); large enemies get 0.80 / 0.90 / 1.00 / 1.10 and the Dreadnought class 0.50 / 0.55 / 0.90 / 1.20. At Hazard 5 the count is 0.85 / 0.85 / 1.25 / 1.50. — [Deep Rock Galactic wiki, Difficulty Scaling](https://deeprockgalactic.wiki.gg/wiki/Difficulty_Scaling)
- **Halo Reach prioritises what each client hears about** by distance, screen presence, threat and recent interaction, so low-priority objects update more slowly. — [Edgegap summary](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-halo-reach-netcode-host-migration)
- **Boss logic on clients is a risk.** Destiny built some raid boss logic on client-side systems, and Truman called the Crota exploit "an engineering failure" of auditing those scripts. — [Edgegap, Destiny 2](https://edgegap.com/blog/destiny-2-s-network-achitecture---multiplayer-game-deep-dive)
- **A stealth rule is a targeting rule.** Boss Room's imps "ignore invisible players". — [Unity Boss Room actions](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/samples/bossroom/bossroom-actions.html)

### Inferences
- **Authority.** Run every enemy on the host. DungeonRaze's enemy code reads "the player" in many places (sight memory, `canSee`, `huntOver`, `G.inBoss`, the crocodile's spot picking). The first refactor is to give each enemy a target and replace the single player with "my target" or "any player", which is needed whatever netcode is chosen.
- **Targeting.** A simple threat score per enemy per player (damage dealt recently, distance, who it can see, who hit it last, with stickiness so that it does not flip every frame) is the usual shape. Bound and ambush types keep their own rules (nearest player in reach). No source consulted describes one, so the numbers will have to come from playtests.
- **Attack tokens.** The current rule is two attackers at once against one player. In co-op the choice is per target (two per player, so up to ten attackers for five players) or a global pool that grows with party size. Per target with a global cap keeps each player's fight the same as single player and stops a pack ignoring four players to queue on one.
- **Scaling.** Both reference games scale count for everything and health only for big enemies and bosses, with a sub-linear party factor (0.3 per extra player in Risk of Rain 2; ×1.1 enemies at four players against ×0.5 solo in Deep Rock at Hazard 3). Boss health scaling close to linear in living players (Risk of Rain 2) keeps fight length steady. For DungeonRaze: scale pack sizes and boss health, leave rats and bloaters alone, and let the crocodile's mark thresholds stay fractions of its scaled health.
- **Boss mechanics that single out a player** (the death roll, plates aimed at "the player", chunks falling 3 to 8 m from "the player", the centre-pool punishment): each needs an explicit pick (highest threat, random with no repeat, or furthest) made on the host and broadcast, and the "player leaves the cavern" reset becomes "all players out or dead".
- **Regular enemies and the boss chamber rule** (`G.inBoss` makes regular enemies give up) needs restating per player or for the party.

### Gaps
- No sourced description of aggro or threat tables, or of attack-token or slot systems, for Vermintide, Darktide, Monster Hunter, Elden Ring or the others. Vermintide's and Darktide's AI directors and slot systems are discussed in GDC talks I did not reach within the search budget.
- No source on how Nightreign or Monster Hunter scale monsters by party size.

## 6. Syncing procedural worlds: seed versus data, determinism, runtime terrain changes

### Takeaway
Sending only the seed is safe only if generation is bit-identical on every machine, and in JavaScript that fails for any generator that uses `Math.sin`, `cos`, `pow`, `exp` and similar, whose results are not fixed by the language and have changed between engine versions. The low-risk pattern is seed plus a hash check, with the host able to send the generated data, and runtime terrain changes sent as a short reliable list.

### Cited Findings
- **JavaScript maths is not specified exactly.** ECMA-262 says the behaviour of functions including sin, cos, tan, exp and pow "is not precisely specified". Observed: `Math.tanh(0.1)` differs between Node 4 and Node 6; `Math.pow(1/3, 3)` differs between Node 10 and Node 12. V8 and SpiderMonkey use "slightly different ports" of fdlibm; JavaScriptCore (Safari) uses the platform's cmath. Workarounds: a library that computes these with arithmetic alone, or compare with a tolerance. — [Tom MacWright, Math keeps changing, Feb 2020](https://macwright.com/2020/02/14/math-keeps-changing) (2020; engines have changed since, so the specific pairs may differ but the spec position holds)
- **A reported case.** `Math.sin` gave different results on a server and a client in one app. — search-result snippet of a [Stack Overflow question](https://kiwix.lema.org/stackoverflow.com_en_all_2023-11/questions/33373338/math-sin-gives-different-results-on-server-client). A polyfill set, trigfills, exists for cross-browser consistency at about 10 digits of accuracy. — search-result snippet of [trigfills on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/trigfills)
- **Native engines.** Floating point determinism is "yes, if": the same executable from the same compiler on the same architecture can match, but assuming identical results across compilers, architectures or debug and release builds is naive; AMD and Intel processors "produced slightly different results for transcendental functions"; strict IEEE 754 with a restricted set of operations can work at a performance cost. — [Gaffer on Games, Floating Point Determinism, Feb 2010](https://gafferongames.com/post/floating_point_determinism/) (2010; the principle is unchanged). "Differences in floating point behavior between compilers, OS's and even instruction sets make it almost impossible to guarantee determinism." — [Gaffer on Games, Deterministic Lockstep](https://gafferongames.com/post/deterministic_lockstep/)
- **Why deterministic engines use fixed point.** Photon Quantum runs a pure C# simulation with its own deterministic maths library. — [Photon Quantum introduction](https://doc.photonengine.com/quantum/current/quantum-intro)
- **What a determinism failure looks like and how a shipped game recovers.** Factorio (deterministic lockstep): any difference is a desync; causes are bugs in the game or mods or a hardware miscalculation, not the network; the client disconnects and downloads the map and state from the server again. — [Factorio wiki, Desynchronization](https://wiki.factorio.com/Desynchronization)
- **Snapshots must be able to carry state for late joiners.** Arrows in Boss Room are full network objects "so late joiners see them", unlike effects sent as one-off messages. — [Unity Boss Room actions](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/samples/bossroom/bossroom-actions.html)

### Inferences
- **Seed-only is fragile here.** DungeonRaze generates its caves from a seeded stream plus noise and trigonometry, and the only check that a seed gives the same world is a hash test run in headless Chromium. Different players will be on Chrome, Firefox and Safari, on x86 and ARM. Addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and `Math.sqrt` are exact under IEEE 754; the transcendental functions are not.
- **The cheap, robust design** fits this codebase well because collision, sight, routes, shots and the camera all read one scalar field:
  1. The host sends the seed and a hash of the quantised result (field, water layer, spawn list).
  2. Each client generates and compares hashes.
  3. On a mismatch the client asks the host for the data itself: the field and water grids quantised to a byte or two per cell and compressed, plus the spawn list. Meshes and decoration are rebuilt locally from the field; small float differences there are only cosmetic.
  - A simpler variant is to always send the data and never rely on the seed. Either way the host's spawn list should be the one used, since enemies are host-owned.
- **Making generation deterministic by construction** is also possible: replace `Math.sin`, `cos`, `atan2`, `pow` and `exp` inside the generators with pure-arithmetic versions. It is worth doing only if seed-only joining matters (for example for sharing seeds).
- **In Unity, Unreal or Godot** the same rule applies more strongly: different builds and CPU families may disagree, so generate on the host and replicate the data, or use fixed point for the generator.
- **Runtime terrain changes.** The Sapper's rubble and the crocodile's fallen ceiling are capsules added to the field by `addRock` and removed by id. Sync them as reliable, ordered host events ("add rock id, endpoints, radius", "remove rock id") and include the live list in the join snapshot. The same goes for mines, tripwires, rift ends and holes, which are short lists with owners and timers. The host should decide where a cave-in's ridge runs, because it depends on the field and on standing rubble.
- **Decoration and effects** that block nothing (drips, lamp flicker, brick heaps) can stay local and unsynchronised.

### Gaps
- I did not find a current (2025 to 2026) test of whether Chrome, Firefox and Safari now agree bit for bit on `Math.sin` and friends. It must be measured: hash the generated field for a set of seeds on each browser and device.
- No source consulted describes how Deep Rock Galactic or Risk of Rain 2 sync generated levels (seed or data) or Deep Rock's terrain carving.

## 7. Joining and leaving: late join, disconnects, host leaving, reconnect

### Takeaway
Small co-op games mostly do not migrate hosts: when the host leaves, the session ends. Host migration exists (Halo Reach, Vermintide 2, Photon Fusion) but developers describe it as a significant undertaking with visible failure modes, and Fatshark's move to dedicated servers is attributed to the host-leaving problem.

### Cited Findings
- **Default behaviour of player hosting.** "The game ceases to exist when the host player leaves the game." — [Unity: listen server host architecture](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/listenserverhostarchitecture.html). Lethal Company does not hand the session to another player. — search-result snippet of [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1966720/discussions/0/4038103682226821807/)
- **Host migration is expensive.** Aldridge called it a "significant engineering undertaking" worth its own talk; Reach's migrations produced "black screens, duplicate flags, broken progression", which led Bungie to make reconciliation part of Destiny's core design. — [Edgegap, Halo Reach](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-halo-reach-netcode-host-migration); [Edgegap, Destiny 2](https://edgegap.com/blog/destiny-2-s-network-achitecture---multiplayer-game-deep-dive)
- **What an off-the-shelf migration looks like.** Photon Fusion: off by default; the host uploads a state snapshot at an interval (about 30 s recommended); when the host drops, the cloud picks a new host and sends it the last snapshot; the developer rebuilds the objects by hand; "changes made to the game state between the last snapshot and the actual host migration will be lost". — [Photon Fusion host migration](https://doc.photonengine.com/fusion/current/manual/host-migration)
- **Vermintide 2 has host migration; Darktide avoids the problem with dedicated servers.** A patch note says it is "once again possible to cancel host migration"; a community post gives Fatshark's reason for dedicated servers as avoiding "the host leaving and the game ending prematurely like in VT2". — search-result snippets of [Fatshark forum thread](https://forums.fatsharkgames.com/t/we-need-servers-like-vermintide-2/112835)
- **Destiny** migrates when the physics host leaves a bubble. — search-result snippet of a write-up; the design intent is in [Edgegap, Destiny 2](https://edgegap.com/blog/destiny-2-s-network-achitecture---multiplayer-game-deep-dive)
- **Reconnect is weak in peer-hosted Souls-likes.** A Nightreign player reports "The current session cannot be restored" on rejoining; a vendor guide says no central server holds the dropped player's place. — search-result snippets of [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2622380/discussions/0/501685815345171977) and [GearUP guide](https://www.gearupbooster.com/en/blog/fix-elden-ring-nightreign-reconnect-error.html) (vendor, commercial interest)
- **Rejoin by full state download.** Factorio reconnects a desynced client by sending it the server's map and state. — [Factorio wiki](https://wiki.factorio.com/Desynchronization)
- **Disconnected players and scaling.** Risk of Rain 2's player count for difficulty "includes disconnected players". — [Risk of Rain 2 wiki](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Difficulty)
- **Late-join state must be replicated state, not past events.** — [Unity Boss Room actions](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/samples/bossroom/bossroom-actions.html)
- **A relay can tell clients the host is gone.** — [Unity: listen server host architecture](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/listenserverhostarchitecture.html)

### Inferences
- **First version: no host migration.** If the host drops, the run ends for the clients and they return to their own hall with what they had earned up to then, which requires rewards to be banked per player as they are earned rather than at the end.
- **A cheap middle step is "resume from checkpoint", not live migration.** A run's durable state is small: seed (or the field), which rooms are cleared, which enemies are dead, the boss's mark, standing rubble. If the host sends that to every client at each rest shrine, a surviving client can start a new session from the last shrine and the others rejoin it. This is Fusion's snapshot scheme with a 30-second interval replaced by natural checkpoints.
- **Late join.** Simplest rule: join in the hall, or at a rest point of a run in progress. The join snapshot is the world data from section 6, the lists of rocks, mines and rifts, every enemy's full state, and the dead list.
- **A client dropping mid-run.** The host removes the player, releases anything bound to them (an enemy in `hold`, an execution claim, their mines and rifts on a timer), and must decide scaling: Risk of Rain 2 keeps counting the player. Keeping enemy count but rescaling boss health by living players, as Risk of Rain 2 does, avoids punishing the rest.
- **Reconnect.** Keep the player's slot for a grace period keyed by a session token so the same player can come back into the same run through the late-join path.

### Gaps
- No source on how Deep Rock Galactic, Remnant 2, Monster Hunter or Helldivers 2 handle a host leaving.
- No measured data on how often hosts drop in small co-op sessions, which is what decides whether migration is worth building.

## 8. Persistence and trust

### Takeaway
Small co-op PvE games accept that hosts and clients can cheat and rely on soft validation; server-held progression is what larger titles use when rewards matter to strangers. With player hosting and local saves, the practical rule is that nobody's machine may write another player's save.

### Cited Findings
- **Soft validation is the PvE norm.** Basic server checks are "acceptable for most PvE games". — [Unity: dealing with latency](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/dealing-with-latency.html)
- **The host is trusted by construction.** The host can see all world information, "making cheating easier". — [Unity: listen server host architecture](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/listenserverhostarchitecture.html)
- **Client authority can be forged.** "Any malicious player can forge messages." — [Unity: dealing with latency](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/dealing-with-latency.html)
- **Moving simulation to a server does not by itself stop cheating.** — [Serverside Simulations](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/mvp/Serverside_Simulations/)
- **Peer netcode is also a security surface.** A modder reported crash and vulnerability classes shared between Dark Souls 3's and Elden Ring's netcode. — search-result snippet of [PC Gamer, Feb 2022](https://pcgamer.com/modder-says-elden-ring-may-be-vulnerable-to-the-same-security-flaws-as-dark-souls)
- **Client-side boss logic led to a famous exploit.** Destiny's Crota raid. — [Edgegap, Destiny 2](https://edgegap.com/blog/destiny-2-s-network-achitecture---multiplayer-game-deep-dive)
- **Server-held worlds.** No Rest for the Wicked keeps a shared realm that persists and "remembers you" whether you started or joined it. — [Moon Studios, Nov 2025](https://norestforthewicked.com/news/being-wicked-together). Destiny keeps mission state on a cloud activity host. — [Edgegap, Destiny 2](https://edgegap.com/blog/destiny-2-s-network-achitecture---multiplayer-game-deep-dive)
- **Anti-cheat is a losing race for small teams.** "1000s of players" against "only a few devs". — [Rune, July 2024](https://developers.rune.ai/blog/modern-game-networking-models)

### Inferences
- **Recommended for a solo developer with no PvP and no shared economy:** per-player saves kept on the player's own device (the game already saves to local storage), friends-only or code-based lobbies, and no attempt at anti-cheat. Cheating then harms only sessions the cheater is invited into.
- **Each client writes only its own save.** The host announces facts ("boss dead", "grade D cleared", "enemy killed, credit to these players") and each client applies its own rewards and unlocks. A host must not be able to push items, unlocks or a wiped save into a client. A client should also check announcements for plausibility (it was in that run; the grade is the one it entered).
- **Treat every message as hostile input for safety, not fairness:** bounds-check sizes, ids and counts so a malformed packet cannot crash or corrupt a peer.
- **If rewards later matter between strangers** (leaderboards, trading, public matchmaking), the step up is a small trusted service that validates run results or a headless authoritative host; Destiny's 10 Hz activity host shows such a component can be cheap if it holds only mission state.
- **Hidden-class codes** are already stored only as hashes in the client; in co-op an unlock should stay a local fact and not be granted by a peer.

### Gaps
- No readable developer source on how Deep Rock Galactic, Risk of Rain 2, Lethal Company or Monster Hunter store progression and what they validate. The common belief that the first three keep saves locally with no validation is unsourced here.

## 9. Typical numbers

### Takeaway
Shipped reference points span 10 Hz (state scripts) to 128 Hz (competitive shooters); a host sending 20 to 30 snapshots a second to four clients, with inputs or own-state sent 30 to 60 times a second, sits comfortably inside what a home connection and published budgets allow. For 5 players and 40 enemies the whole session should need on the order of a few hundred kilobits a second of host upload before delta compression.

### Cited Findings
- **Tick and send rates.**
  - Overwatch: 16 ms command frames (about 62.5 Hz), 7 ms for tournaments. — [Edgegap, Overwatch](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-overwatch-2016-netcode-architecture-rollback)
  - Valorant: 128 Hz server and client. — [Riot Games, 2020](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/peeking-valorants-netcode)
  - Destiny's activity host: 10 Hz. — [Edgegap, Destiny 2](https://edgegap.com/blog/destiny-2-s-network-achitecture---multiplayer-game-deep-dive). Its physics host accumulates damage "at 30 ticks". — search-result snippet of a write-up
  - Gambetta's worked example: 10 server updates a second. — [Gambetta](https://www.gabrielgambetta.com/entity-interpolation.html)
  - Fiedler's demos: 10, 30 and 60 packets a second. — [Gaffer on Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/snapshot_interpolation/)
  - Unity's examples: 30 or 60 Hz tick, 10 to 60 Hz updates; the update rate must be high enough that each fast event gets its own update. — [Unity: ticks and update rates](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/ticks-and-update-rates.html)
- **Interpolation delay.** About one update interval at the least (100 ms at 10 Hz). — [Gambetta](https://www.gabrielgambetta.com/entity-interpolation.html). For lossy links, three intervals: 350 ms at 10 Hz, 150 ms at 30 Hz, 85 ms at 60 Hz. — [Gaffer on Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/snapshot_interpolation/)
- **Bytes per entity.** A physics cube: 321 bits raw; 80 bits (10 bytes) after quantisation, with orientation as "smallest three" in 29 bits and position in 50 bits at 512 steps per metre; with delta encoding position averages 26.1 bits and orientation 23.3 bits, unchanged objects cost 1 bit, and a stationary scene of 901 cubes at 60 Hz costs about 15 kbps of headers. — [Gaffer on Games, Snapshot Compression, 2015](https://gafferongames.com/post/snapshot_compression/)
- **Inputs.** Halo Reach's control data is about 20 bits per player. — [Edgegap, Halo Reach](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-halo-reach-netcode-host-migration). Fiedler: 6 bits of input at 60 Hz; resending all unacknowledged inputs costs about 15 bytes in the worst case with delta encoding. — [Gaffer on Games, Deterministic Lockstep](https://gafferongames.com/post/deterministic_lockstep/)
- **Whole-session budgets.** Halo Reach: 16 players and over 2,000 synced entities in 250 kbps, against a 384 kbps goal and about 20 Mbps for naive send-everything; game design changes gave four of the five largest savings. — [Edgegap, Halo Reach](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-halo-reach-netcode-host-migration) and search-result snippet of the same talk's summaries. Fiedler's target for 901 cubes was 256 kbps. — [Gaffer on Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/snapshot_compression/)
- **Packet size.** About 1,500 bytes per packet (MTU). — [Rune](https://developers.rune.ai/blog/modern-game-networking-models). IP and UDP headers are 28 bytes. — [Gaffer on Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/snapshot_interpolation/)
- **Latency in the wild and tolerances.**
  - Typical: about 50 ms one way coast to coast in the US as an assumption; 100 to 500 ms occur. — [Gambetta](https://www.gabrielgambetta.com/client-server-game-architecture.html). Spikes at five times the average. — [Rune](https://developers.rune.ai/blog/modern-game-networking-models). Halo 3 averaged around 80 to 100 ms, about three frames. — search-result snippet attributed to a community Halo 2/3 networking guide; the search tool did not say which result it came from, and [this guide](https://halo.bungie.org/misc/networkguide.html) is my best match, not opened. Low confidence.
  - 200 ms between input and screen is "frustrating". — [Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/dealing-with-latency.html). Half a second is "unplayable". — [Gambetta](https://www.gabrielgambetta.com/client-server-game-architecture.html)
  - A predicted action confirmed up to 150 ms later goes unnoticed by most players, 200 ms by casual players. — [Edgegap, Halo Reach](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-halo-reach-netcode-host-migration)
  - Input delay equal to ping is noticeable at about 100 ms in an action RPG. — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/path-of-exile/path-of-exile-s-new-lockstep-mode-banishes-desync-problems); Path of Exile's auto mode switches at about 150 ms. — search-result snippet of [Path of Exile wiki](https://poewiki.net/wiki/Desync)
  - Overwatch turns hit prediction off above about 220 ms round trip. — [Edgegap, Overwatch](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-overwatch-2016-netcode-architecture-rollback)
  - Valorant aims for 35 ms ping for 70% of players; gunfights turn on 20 to 50 ms. — [Riot Games](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/peeking-valorants-netcode)
  - Academic thresholds: 100 ms for first-person avatar games and 500 ms for third-person avatar games (sports, RPG); in the EverQuest experiment going from 0 to 500 ms added only about five seconds to two minutes of combat. — [Claypool and Claypool, Latency and Player Actions in Online Games, CACM Nov 2006](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~claypool/papers/precision-deadline/final.pdf) (2006, and measured on auto-attack RPG combat, not on dodge and parry timing; treat 500 ms as an upper bound for the slow parts of the game only)
  - Elden Ring PvP players report 0.3 to 0.5 s before a hit registers. — search-result snippet of [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1245620/discussions/0/3758850346171791546)

### Inferences
Estimate for DungeonRaze, 5 players and 40 awake enemies (my arithmetic, using Fiedler's quantisation approach; the game's floor is flat, so most entities need no height):

- **Enemy state:** x and z at 1 cm over a 512 m span (16 bits each, 32), yaw (8), a height flag (1), state (4), attack index and state timer (12), health (10), posture (7), target player (3), flags such as aware, hidden, safe, aloft (4): about 81 bits, plus an id (7): **about 11 bytes**.
- **Player state:** position (32), yaw (8), state (4), move id (6), state timer (8), health (10), stamina (7), focus (7), lock target (7), flags (8): about 97 bits, **about 12 bytes**.
- **Full snapshot:** 40 × 11 + 5 × 12 = 500 bytes, about 540 with headers. That fits in one packet under the 1,500-byte MTU with room to spare.
- **Host to each client:** 540 bytes × 20 Hz ≈ 10.8 kB/s ≈ **86 kbps** with no delta compression; × 30 Hz ≈ 130 kbps. With delta against the last acknowledged snapshot (idle enemies cost a bit each, moving ones roughly half), expect roughly **25 to 50 kbps** per client.
- **Host upload for 4 clients:** about 350 kbps uncompressed at 20 Hz, about 100 to 200 kbps with deltas. Halo Reach fitted 16 players and 2,000 entities in 250 kbps, so this is conservative.
- **Client to host:** own state at 30 Hz is (12 + about 36 bytes of headers) × 30 ≈ 1.4 kB/s ≈ **12 kbps**, plus hit, parry and ability events of a few bytes each.
- **Reliable events** (kills, rocks, mines, rifts, boss mode changes) are small and rare: well under 1 kbps on average.
- **Suggested rates:** simulate at 60 Hz as now; host sends snapshots at 20 Hz to start (30 Hz if bandwidth allows), clients send own state at 30 Hz and events at once; remote entities interpolated 100 ms behind at 20 Hz (two intervals) on good links, widening to 150 ms under loss.
- **What a remote player experiences at round trip R with a 100 ms buffer:** enemies seen about R/2 + 100 ms behind the host; own dodge and parry windows unaffected (victim-side); own hits confirmed after R; an enemy's reaction to their parry seen by others about R + 100 ms late.
- **Latency targets (design judgement from the tolerances above):** up to about 100 ms round trip should be indistinguishable from single player with owner authority; 100 to 200 ms playable with visible lateness in other players and in kill confirmation; above about 250 ms show a warning. This is why region-aware lobbies or friends-only play matter more than raw tick rate.

### Gaps

- I have no sourced figure for typical home upload capacity or for relay bandwidth pricing in 2026.

## 10. Recommended architecture, fallback, and what to prototype first

### Takeaway
Recommended: a relayed, player-hosted session in which the host owns the world, enemies and bosses, each client owns its own character and judges hits on itself, attackers propose hits on enemies that the host soft-validates, and everything else is snapshot-interpolated; no lockstep or rollback, and no host migration in the first version. The fallback is the same protocol with the host role moved to a headless process. The first prototype should test dodge, parry and a grab against a host-run enemy under injected latency, because that is the part no source can settle for this game.

### Cited Findings
- Soft validation suits PvE; listen servers suit fewer than 12 players and casual co-op. — [Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/dealing-with-latency.html); [Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/listenserverhostarchitecture.html); [Unreal](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/networking-overview-for-unreal-engine)
- Owner authority for the local character, host authority for damage to enemies, shipped in Destiny to "feel like a single-player shooter". — [nessus42 write-up](https://gist.github.com/nessus42/17c433143c68bc4500c5637616557e86)
- Remote authority over dodges is what players of Darktide and Lords of the Fallen complain about. — [Fatshark forums](https://forums.fatsharkgames.com/t/server-authoritative-gameplay-is-terrible-and-needs-to-go/105184); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1501750/discussions/0/3934517778349929251)
- Mixing client-driven players with server-driven AI "can easily have become a mess" without discipline. — [Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.5/manual/learn/dealing-with-latency.html)
- Lockstep and rollback need bit-exact determinism, which JavaScript's maths functions do not give across engines. — [Gaffer on Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/deterministic_lockstep/); [MacWright](https://macwright.com/2020/02/14/math-keeps-changing)
- Unreliable, redundant sends beat TCP for time-critical data. — [Gaffer on Games](https://gafferongames.com/post/deterministic_lockstep/)
- Host migration is a significant undertaking and loses state since the last snapshot in off-the-shelf form. — [Edgegap, Halo Reach](https://edgegap.com/blog/game-backend-deep-dive-halo-reach-netcode-host-migration); [Photon Fusion](https://doc.photonengine.com/fusion/current/manual/host-migration)

### Inferences
**Recommended design ("host owns the world, each player owns themselves")**

| Thing | Authority | How others see it |
|---|---|---|
| A player's position, state machine, stamina, dodge, guard | That player's client | Interpolated from 30 Hz owner state |
| Whether an enemy's attack hit, was dodged, parried or blocked | The victim's client (`hurtPlayer` runs only there) | Result event to host, relayed |
| Whether a player's attack hit an enemy | Attacker's client proposes; host validates loosely and applies (`hitEnemy` runs on host) | Attacker shows it at once; others on the next snapshot |
| Enemy AI, targets, tokens, health, posture, death, boss modes and marks | Host | Snapshots at 20 to 30 Hz plus reliable events for attack starts and mode changes |
| Projectiles | Whoever fired: host for enemy shots (each client tests its own player), the firing client for player shots (proposes hits) | Spawn event, simulated locally by all |
| Executions, grabs | Host arbitrates the claim; the bound pair is driven by the involved player's client for the duration | Blended in over the first part of the animation |
| Terrain changes, mines, rifts, holes | Host, as reliable ordered events; owner-predicted placement | Event list, included in join snapshot |
| World | Host's seed plus hash check, data on mismatch | Section 6 |
| Hit-stop, slow motion, camera shake, rumble | Local only | Not sent |
| Saves, unlocks, rewards | Each client, for itself | Not sent |

- **Transport:** unreliable unordered for snapshots and owner state, reliable ordered for events, through a relay with direct connection where it works. In a browser this implies WebRTC data channels or WebTransport rather than a plain WebSocket for the state stream; that choice belongs to the engine and transport research, but the netcode needs both delivery modes.
- **Clock:** clients estimate the host clock from ping measurements; events carry host time so telegraphs can be aligned.
- **Why this over the alternatives:** it keeps the feel of the existing single-player combat for every player at any reasonable ping, costs no servers, and reuses the two functions through which all damage already flows. Its weaknesses are accepted ones: cheating among invited friends, the session ending with the host, and late visual consequences of parries.

**Fallback**
- **If hosts' home connections prove too poor or sessions keep ending:** run the host role as a headless process on a small server. The game already advances its simulation without rendering in tests (a fixed 60 steps a second through `DF.sim`), which is the same capability a headless host needs. The protocol and the owner-authoritative split do not change, so this is a deployment decision, not a rewrite.
- **If owner authority proves too exploitable once rewards matter:** tighten host validation step by step (movement speed, cooldowns, damage ranges) before considering full server authority with prediction.
- **Not recommended as a fallback:** lockstep or rollback. They would require rewriting the simulation for determinism and state restore, and lockstep puts input delay on dodges.

**What to prototype first, in order of risk removed**
1. **Latency rig and one fight.** Two instances on one machine joined by a message channel with adjustable delay, jitter and loss (100, 200 and 300 ms round trip; 2 to 5% loss). One host-run pack and the client player. Victim-side `hurtPlayer`, attacker-proposed `hitEnemy`, 20 Hz snapshots with a 100 ms buffer. Question answered: do dodge, parry and block feel the same as single player on the client, and do telegraphs still read when they arrive late or are compressed?
2. **The bound interactions.** A siren or grabber hold and one execution across the same rig, including two players pressing Execute at once and a client dropping mid-hold. Question answered: can the binding rules stay simple.
3. **Multi-target AI on the host with no networking.** Two or three bot players in one process: target selection, tokens per target, the boss chamber rule and the crocodile's player-specific mechanics. This is needed for any architecture and is the largest pure refactor.
4. **World determinism check.** Hash the generated field, water and spawns for a handful of seeds in Chrome, Firefox and Safari on desktop and phone. Decides seed-only versus send-the-data.
5. **Measure.** Log real snapshot sizes and host upload with 40 enemies and 4 fake clients against the estimate in section 9.
6. **Only then:** real transport and relay, lobbies in the hall, join and leave, and scaling.

### Gaps
- The recommendation rests on documentation and on other games' designs; nothing substitutes for step 1, since no source covers a Soulslike with executions, grabs and terrain changes together.
- I did not research relay providers, WebRTC or WebTransport specifics, or engine networking libraries (Unity NGO, Unreal replication, Godot, Photon, Colyseus and the like); those are outside this topic and are assumed to be covered by the engine and transport researchers.
- Whether a compressed telegraph (playing a wind-up faster on a lagging client) is acceptable to players is untested and is my suggestion, not an established named technique in the sources I read.
