## 1. Which official games exist as of today, with developer, publisher, platforms, release dates and business model?

### Takeaway
Netmarble holds the game licence and has three titles, all developed by its Netmarble Neo studio: the free-to-play gacha action RPG *Solo Leveling: Arise* (mobile and PC, 8 May 2024, still live), the premium no-gacha *Arise Overdrive* (PC 24 November 2025, PS5 and Xbox Series X|S 4 November 2026) and the unreleased isometric roguelite *Solo Leveling: Karma*, which on 5 November 2026 slipped from the second half of 2026 to Q1 2027. Everything else is tabletop, collectibles or skin crossovers in other publishers' games; I found no confirmed licensed Roblox game.

## 2. Solo Leveling: Arise in depth: the combat system, controls, camera and lock-on

### Takeaway
Arise is a stage-based third-person action RPG built around one loop: fill a break gauge and land perfect dodges to open short defence-down windows, then spend weapon skills and the ultimate inside them. The protagonist has a deeper kit than everyone else (two swappable weapons, a counter after a perfect dodge, shadows, support calls), and the game offers auto and semi-auto play alongside manual control.

### Cited Findings
- [Fact] Jinwoo's inputs are basic attacks, two basic skills, QTE skills, weapon skills, support skills, dodge and dash. He equips two weapons and using a weapon skill also switches to that weapon — [Talk Android beginner's guide](https://www.talkandroid.com/460555-beginners-guide-solo-leveling-arise/)
- [Fact] In "Hunter mode" the player fields three Hunters and tags between them; Hunters lack weapon skills and their support skill swaps the active character instead — [Talk Android beginner's guide](https://www.talkandroid.com/460555-beginners-guide-solo-leveling-arise/)
- [Fact] The director said the two modes exist to balance Jinwoo's overwhelming power against other Hunters and to vary play; in Jinwoo mode he fights alongside Hunters and summoned Shadows — [Final Weapon interview](https://finalweapon.net/2024/04/23/solo-leveling-arise-interview-netmarble-seong-keon-jin-inho-kim)
- [Fact] Jinwoo brings up to three teammates whose skills are called in during the fight — [Pocket Gamer review](https://www.pocketgamer.com/solo-leveling-arise/review)
- [Guide] Extreme Evasion is the perfect dodge: pressing dodge just before a hit lands slows time and gives brief invulnerability — [Talk Android, Igris guide](https://www.talkandroid.com/455563-how-to-beat-igris-solo-leveling-arise/)
- [Guide] The dodge button lighting up is used as a timing cue for some attacks — [Talk Android, Igris guide](https://www.talkandroid.com/455563-how-to-beat-igris-solo-leveling-arise/)
- [Guide] Shadow Step is the follow-up: after an Extreme Evasion Jinwoo teleports behind the target, deals damage and applies a Defense Decrease debuff. It has a 10-second cooldown — [Talk Android, Igris guide](https://www.talkandroid.com/455563-how-to-beat-igris-solo-leveling-arise/)
- [Snippet] Only Jinwoo can counter after an Extreme Evasion; other Hunters can perfect-dodge but get no counter. Some QTE skills trigger only after an Extreme Evasion — [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/how-use-extreme-evasion-solo-leveling-arise)
- [Guide] Bosses have a Break gauge. Emptying it stuns the boss for a few seconds and applies Defense Decrease; the gauge then refills, so breaking is repeated through the fight — [Talk Android, Igris guide](https://www.talkandroid.com/455563-how-to-beat-igris-solo-leveling-arise/)
- [Guide] Break damage comes from specific skills tagged "Break" and from support skills of Hunters built as breakers; matching the boss's weak element increases break damage — [Talk Android, Igris guide](https://www.talkandroid.com/455563-how-to-beat-igris-solo-leveling-arise/)
- [Guide] Some bosses take heavily reduced damage while their break gauge is up, making the break mandatory rather than optional — [Inven Global, Kargalgan guide](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/18942/guide-for-almighty-shaman-kargalgan-use-meilin-or-hwang-dongsoo-to-break-the-shield)
- [Guide] The standard burst pattern is to hold the ultimate and weapon skills until the target has Defense Decrease from a Break or Shadow Step — [Talk Android, Igris guide](https://www.talkandroid.com/455563-how-to-beat-igris-solo-leveling-arise/)
- [Guide] Elemental weakness is per enemy: Kargalgan resists Fire and Light and is weak to Wind and Dark; Igris is Dark and countered by Light weapons — [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/18942/guide-for-almighty-shaman-kargalgan-use-meilin-or-hwang-dongsoo-to-break-the-shield); [Talk Android](https://www.talkandroid.com/455563-how-to-beat-igris-solo-leveling-arise/)
- [Fact] Auto and semi-auto combat are available in both modes; guides advise manual play for hard content because auto does not time dodges or ultimates — [Talk Android beginner's guide](https://www.talkandroid.com/460555-beginners-guide-solo-leveling-arise/)
- [Critic] Pocket Gamer found combat engaging, compared it to *Punishing: Gray Raven* but less forgiving, and noted that mashing auto-attack does not clear stages. ### Inferences
- The combat is a mobile "character action" template (perfect dodge with slow motion, tag-in supports, break gauge, ultimate meter). The Solo Leveling flavour comes from who does it and what it is called, not from novel rules.
- Shadow Step is functionally a "perfect dodge, then teleport backstab with a defence-down debuff on a cooldown". DungeonRaze's existing perfect dodge and the Assassin's blink already cover the same ground; the specific name and its tie to Jinwoo are the licensed part.
- Giving only the protagonist a dodge counter is how Arise expresses "Jinwoo is special" mechanically. A class-based game can do the reverse and give each class its own perfect-dodge reward.

## 3. Arise's progression and structure

### Takeaway
Progression is a layered live-service stack. Jinwoo levels and allocates points into five stats, changes job through story fights, and is powered up through weapons, skill runes, artifacts with set bonuses, blessing stones and, later, cores and statues. Other Hunters are a gacha roster advanced with duplicates. Content is a set of key-gated instanced modes that split into "Jinwoo battles" and "Hunter battles".

## 4. How Arise models each hunter class and element, and how named hunters play

### Takeaway
Each Hunter has one fixed class and one fixed element. The launch classes were Tanker, Fighter, Mage, Assassin, Ranger and Healer, and the five elements are Fire, Water, Wind, Light and Dark. ## 5. How Arise models gates and difficulty by rank

### Takeaway
Gates in Arise are a repeatable resource-farming board, not a difficulty ladder for the story. Each gate has a type (shown by colour) that decides the reward table and a rank from E to S that scales difficulty and yield. Entry costs a daily key, the board can be re-rolled, and a cleared gate keeps producing through "mining".

- In Arise, "red gate" and "dungeon break" are colours and reward tables. - Difficulty in Arise is mostly stat scaling plus a timer. Reviews of both games show players read that as grind.
- [Web-2] Arise's operators dismantled much of their own gate board after two years: re-rolling for a good gate was named as a chore, and the 2026 version auto-sets difficulty and lets the player choose the reward. - [Web-2] The only per-rank numbers found are indirect (S-rank mining up to three hours, auto-clear allowed at B and below, premium currency from A up). They show rank being used as a gate on convenience and reward type as well as on difficulty.

## 6. Boss design in Arise and Overdrive

### Takeaway
Arise's bosses are pattern fights with readable tells (a glow, a stance, ground markers), a refilling break gauge, an elemental weakness and usually a timer, so they play as "learn the dodge timings, break, burst". Named bosses add one signature mechanic each. Overdrive keeps the same bosses and adds parry, but reviewers found its bosses over-padded with health and reused too often.

### Cited Findings

- [Guide] Tells are physical: red glowing eyes and a pause before a thrust, a low sword stance before a two-hit slam, a spin before a three-hit combo.

### Inferences
- The Igris design is a good model of a "teaching boss": a fixed opener, body-language tells, and a small set of spacing tests. Nothing in that structure is specific to the licence. The specific moveset, look and name are.
- Arise's bosses lean on one gimmick each (shield needing a specific helper, destructible totems, pull-in). Because the gimmick is often solved by owning the right gacha Hunter or element, the fight becomes a roster check. A skill-based game should make the answer something the player does, not something they own.
- Arise's break gauge refills and gates damage. DungeonRaze's posture meter that breaks into a stagger and an execution is closer to Sekiro (my own comparison, from memory of that game, not sourced). The games share the idea of "open a window" but DungeonRaze's ends in a kill animation while Arise's ends in a debuff.
- The clearest "avoid" from Overdrive: do not solve difficulty with health. Reviewers accepted the mechanics and rejected the padding.

## 11. Lessons for an original game: generic staples versus licence-specific expression

### Takeaway
The mechanics these games use are common action-RPG vocabulary and reviewers repeatedly describe them by comparison to other games. What belongs to the licence is the specific expression: names, characters, creature and boss designs, the story, coined terms and the recognisable look of the "System". An original game can use the former freely and should rename and redesign anything from the latter. This is design guidance, not legal advice.

### Inferences

**Generic staples, safe to use under your own names and presentation**
- Light attack chains, cooldown skills, an ultimate meter, weapon swapping.
- Perfect dodge with slow motion and a follow-up; parry and guard; a break, stagger or posture gauge that opens a damage window; back-attack bonuses; elemental weaknesses.
- Class archetypes (fighter, mage, assassin, tank, ranger, healer) and skill trees.
- Letter-graded difficulty tiers from E to S. Unverified, from memory: this scale is widespread across Korean and Japanese hunter and dungeon fiction and games, so it is a convention rather than this series' invention. If so, the risk is in combination with other distinctive elements, not in the letters.
- Dungeons entered through portals, instanced runs with a boss at the end, a hub with a blacksmith and mission board, crafting from boss parts, co-op raids, rarity-tiered gear and set bonuses, roguelite buff drafting.
- Boss-design grammar: a fixed opening attack, body-language tells, ground indicators, a mid-health escalation, one signature mechanic.

**Distinctive to the licence, avoid reproducing** (items tagged "unverified, from memory" describe the novel and webtoon from my own knowledge, not from a page fetched for these notes)
- Every character, monster, place, guild and item name, and their visual designs. That includes game-original characters such as Emma Laurent, and bosses recognisable by silhouette, such as a giant stone statue with a grin in a temple or an ant king, and (unverified, from memory) a red-plumed black knight.
- The shadow army. Sourced above: the hero absorbs defeated enemies into a personal force that fights for him. Unverified, from memory: the trigger is the spoken command "Arise" and the soldiers share a purple-black look. DungeonRaze has already dropped its raise-the-dead class, which removes the largest overlap.
- The "System" (the term appears in reviews of the games). Unverified, from memory: it is shown as a blue pop-up window that issues daily fitness quests, penalties and level-up notices to one chosen player. Level-up UI is generic; that particular presentation and premise is the series' signature.
- The plot beats. Sourced above: the weakest E-rank hunter who rises, the job change to necromancer, the Monarch of Shadows. Unverified, from memory: the double dungeon and its rules, re-awakening, the wider Monarch and Ruler cosmology.
- Coined skill and system names. Sourced above: Shadow Step, Extreme Evasion, Monarch's Awakening, Chain Smash. Unverified, from memory: Shadow Extraction, Ruler's Authority, Dominator's Touch. Use plain or newly invented names.
- The specific combination of terms "Gate", "Red Gate", "Dungeon Break" and "Hunter's Association" as a set. Individually these are ordinary words; together with ranks and hunters they read as this series. DungeonRaze's "portal", "hall" and "cave" already differ.
- Trade dress: logos, typography, the comic-panel cut-in style, voice lines and music.

**Design lessons from what worked and what did not**
- The break-then-burst loop and the dodge-counter loop are the parts players enjoy. DungeonRaze already has both in the form of posture, stagger, execution and perfect dodge. Keep investing there.
- Do not lean on timers and health pools for difficulty. Both licensed games do, and both are called grindy for it. Scale grades with behaviour (new attacks, more simultaneous attackers, less forgiving tells) as well as numbers.
- Avoid solving bosses with ownership (the right element or helper). Let any class beat any boss through play; let preparation shorten the fight, not gate it.
- Give each class a different reward for a perfect dodge or parry, rather than reserving it for one hero.
- If a reward is banked for later use, show how long it lasts.
- Vary the space. "Corridor, wave, wave, boss" in reused rooms is the single most repeated complaint. Generated caves help only if rooms change how fights play.
- Lock-on and camera failures were called out by name in Overdrive. DungeonRaze lists a camera clipping problem as known; it is worth fixing before widening the game.
- Skip auto-play, sweep, idle income and daily keys. They exist to pace a live-service economy and work against a skill game.
- Two axes for portals (grade for difficulty and payout, a second property for rule changes) is a clean idea from Arise's gate board that carries no licence baggage.
