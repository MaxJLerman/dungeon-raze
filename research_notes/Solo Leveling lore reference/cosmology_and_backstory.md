## Who is the Absolute Being, why did it create the Rulers and the Monarchs, and how and why was it overthrown?

### Takeaway
The Absolute Being is the creator god of the setting. It split primordial light into the Rulers and primordial darkness into the Monarchs, set them at war, and was killed on its own throne by the Rulers once they concluded it was keeping the war going for its own entertainment.

## What was the war between Rulers and Monarchs, how long did it run, what did each side want, and how did Ashborn become the Shadow Monarch?

### Takeaway
The war is the Absolute Being's staged conflict between protectors and destroyers, which outlived its maker. Ashborn, the strongest Ruler, stayed loyal, was cut down by his own kind, and rose through a hidden power of death the Absolute Being had placed in him. He then fought beside the Monarchs, was betrayed by them, and withdrew. No source gives the war a length in years.

## Why do gates open on Earth? What is the Rulers' plan and what is the Monarchs' plan?

### Takeaway
The Rulers open the gates on purpose. Earth has no mana of its own and cannot survive being a battlefield, so they flood it with mana through gates and captured monsters, which also lets some humans awaken and serve as vessels. The Monarchs mean to destroy humanity, kill the Rulers' vessels and use Earth to bring their armies through for a final war with the Rulers.

### Cited Findings

## What is mana, where does it come from, and what are magic beasts really?

### Takeaway
Mana is the power of the gods and their creations and is foreign to Earth. It arrives through the gates. Magic beasts are soldiers of the Monarchs' armies, natives of the Chaos World, whom the Rulers captured in the old war, fitted with essence stones and released into dungeons as a delivery system for mana.

## Who is the Architect, what deal did he make with Ashborn, why was the System built, why was Jinwoo chosen, and what is a vessel?

### Takeaway
The Architect, Kandiaru, is a mage who offered to find Ashborn a human vessel in return for immortality and built the System, modelled on a video game, to grow one. A vessel is a human body that lets a spiritual being act on Earth. Ashborn chose Jinwoo himself after watching him through the System, then cut the Architect out and gave Jinwoo his power outright.

## What happens in the final war and the ending?

### Takeaway
Jinwoo wounds Antares, the Rulers' armies arrive and kill him, and Jinwoo then asks the Rulers to use the Cup one last time. Time rewinds ten years, Jinwoo alone keeps the fight away from Earth by hunting every Monarch in the gap for 27 years, which is two years on Earth, and he comes home to a world with no gates, no hunters and nobody who remembers.

### Inferences
- The reset world is our world: no mana, no awakenings, no hunter economy. The only supernatural things left in it are Jinwoo, his shadow army and whatever the Rulers still watch.
- The Rulers never needed vessels in the reset timeline because Jinwoo removed the reason for the gates. This also means the national-level hunters are ordinary people there.
- "Two years later" on the wiki's Yogumunt page and "27 years" elsewhere are the same event counted on the two clocks.

## A dated timeline of in-universe events

### Takeaway
Canon dates events relative to the story, not by calendar year. The fixed points are: gates about ten years before the story, Kamish about eight, the Jeju gate four, and a ten-year rewind at the end.

- The final war falls roughly ten years after the first gates, give or take the length of the story itself. The story's span from double dungeon to final war was not stated in my sources, so "ten years before the story" and "ten years before the final war" may differ by a year or two.

## What remains unexplained or contradictory in canon, and how do the novel, manhwa and anime differ?

### Takeaway
The big unknowns are the number of resets, the length of the war, how a creator god could be killed, and the unseen 27 years. I found no documented contradiction between novel and manhwa on the cosmology. The anime has not reached any of it, and the sequel Ragnarok adds a layer above the Absolute Being that the original never mentions.

### Inferences
- For a design team, the safest canon tier list is: Chugong's novel and the manhwa (agree on cosmology as far as I could find), then Chugong's Karma story for the 27 years, then Ragnarok by a different author, then the anime, which so far adds nothing to the cosmology.
- Since the anime stops at about ch. 124 and the first lore exposition is around ch. 128, a viewer who has only seen the anime knows none of sections 1 to 8 beyond "gates opened ten years ago".
- The Itarim layer is best treated as a sequel extension. Nothing in the original requires it, and it reframes the Absolute Being from "the god" to "a god".
- Several explainer sites contain plain errors (wrong Ruler count, misspelt names, mixed-up event order, and Popverse dating the "novel ending" to 2021, which is the manhwa's end date). Any single explainer should be checked against a second.
