## 1. What is a gate physically, and how is its rank measured? How reliable is the measurement?

### Takeaway
A gate is a blue passage from another dimension into the ordinary world, with a dungeon full of monsters inside. Gates are graded E to S, the Association can usually assess one from outside, and the grade can be badly wrong: a low-rank dungeon hid the double dungeon and a C-rank gate turned out to be a red gate.

[Fandom-API-dependent] The reading is a measurement of leaked mana by sensors; size grows with rank (door-sized at D, hurricane-like at S); no case was found of a gate reading too high.

### Inferences
- The grade is assessed from outside and is a floor, not a guarantee. Both headline disasters of the early story are gates that held more than their grade. For a graded-portal game this supports "the letter is the measured grade; a small share of portals are worse than measured, and you find out inside".
- Colour is the visible state signal: blue for normal, red for sealed.
- [Fandom-API-dependent] The measurement sees only what leaks from the entrance, so anything that withholds mana (an inner dungeon behind a door, an untriggered red gate, a boss hiding its strongest unit) produces an underestimate.

## 2. The gate lifecycle: dungeon break timing, what a break is, the monsters afterwards, and closing after the boss dies

### Takeaway
A gate closes only when its boss is killed, about an hour after the kill. If it is not cleared within seven days of opening, its monsters cross into the human world: this is a dungeon break. Broken-out monsters persist and evolve, as on Jeju.

[Fandom-API-dependent] A break does not close the gate; in Tokyo and on Jeju the boss still had to be found and killed. The Ragnarok sequel adds "field-type dungeons", where a neglected break turns the surrounding land into monster territory.

### Inferences
- The one-hour close is the exit window after the kill, and it is why materials are gathered before the gate shuts.
- Monsters outside a gate breed, adapt and spread (Jeju). A game could treat an uncleared portal as an escalating world state rather than a single failure event.
- [Fandom-API-dependent] A break does not end the gate, and whether the boss itself comes out varies: Kamish and the stone golem did; the chained Tokyo boss did not.

### Gaps
- No chapter number found for the seven-day rule or the one-hour rule. The only normally served source for both is one explainer article.
- Not found: whether the seven days run from opening or discovery (Animehunch says "being opened"), and whether higher ranks break sooner.
- Not found: what happens to monsters still inside when a gate closes, or to a hunter still inside at the one-hour mark.
- The snippet claim that Jeju is the only break with large-scale destruction conflicts with the Kamish case and is not used.
- [Web-2] Still open after the second round: a chapter or episode for the seven-day rule and the one-hour rule. Besides Animehunch, the seven-day rule is now stated by four more articles (two CBR explainers, one CBR list, one Game Rant explainer) and the one-hour rule by two (both CBR explainers), so neither rests on one article any more, but none of them cites where the story says it. Two anime-only pieces give no number at all. The Korean fan encyclopedia that might cite chapters refused the fetch.
- [Web-2] Still open: what happens to monsters still alive inside when a gate closes, and to a hunter still inside at the hour. About sixty pages were read and none says. The nearest statements are that everyone has 60 minutes to leave (CBR) and that closing a gate takes killing all monsters and the boss (Fiction Horizon), which conflicts with the boss-only rule.
- [Web-2] Still open: when the seven days start. One writer's guess that high-rank gates break sooner is speculation.
- [Web-2] New conflict: whether a broken gate stays until every escaped monster is dead (Game Rant) or until its boss is dead (CBR on Tokyo, and the wiki-derived account).

## 3. Special gate types and their exact rules

### Takeaway
Red gates seal on entry, run one day inside per hour outside, drop the party into a hostile climate and hide their true grade. The double dungeon is a hidden second dungeon governed by three commandments. System dungeons are private instances opened by keys from loot boxes, with a penalty zone for missed daily quests.

[Fandom-API-dependent] Each type changes only one or two base rules. Detailed mechanics of the temple, the Demon Castle's 100 floors and permits, the instance rule cards and the giant end-game gates rest on the wiki alone.

### Inferences
- Red gate in rule terms: a normal portal with four modifiers (exit locked until the boss is dead, outside clock slowed 24 to 1, biome swapped to a survival climate, measured grade void).
- Double dungeon in rule terms: a second dungeon revealed only after the first is beaten, containing a rules puzzle with lethal penalties and no ordinary monsters, with a reward for the one who stays.
- System dungeons are the only type with consumable entry (a key from a loot box) and the only one shown as leave-and-return.
- [Fandom-API-dependent] The 24 to 1 ratio fits the incident: three hours outside is three days inside, matching "several days". Minimum true grade for a red gate is B.
- [Fandom-API-dependent] Instance dungeons carry explicit rule cards (no shop, no potions) and a guaranteed emergency-exit item.

## 5. How a dungeon is structured for a raid

### Takeaway
A dungeon is monsters plus one boss; killing the boss is what clears it. The boss may be passive (the spider slept in its chamber) or may run the place (the A-rank orc boss blocked the exit). Guilds send a mining crew to clean up an A-rank dungeon, and squads carry a porter.

[Fandom-API-dependent] The standard guild sequence is: strike squad kills everything except the boss, mining and collecting crews strip the dungeon over several hours, then the boss is killed and everyone leaves within the hour.

## 6. What is harvested and how

## 7. How raids are organised around a gate

### Takeaway
The Hunters Association oversees every gate. Rights to raid a gate can be bought, a C-rank dungeon needs a party of at least eight, and the minimum is met in practice with hired low-rank hunters or, in a shortage, with prisoners serving for reduced sentences.

[Fandom-API-dependent] The Association cordons the site and issues the permit; guilds hold territories and bid for reservations; licensed freelancers form ad hoc parties for low-rank gates; one wiki page gives a ten-person minimum instead of eight.

## 8. How difficulty scales by gate rank

### Takeaway
Rank predicts monster type, boss strength and the size of the response: a C-rank dungeon is an eight-person job with a giant spider, an A-rank dungeon has an orc army whose boss rivals an S-rank hunter, and an S-rank gate needs a country's S-ranks and can still kill them.

[Fandom-API-dependent] The gaps between ranks are steep and uneven, the top of each rank overlaps the bottom of the next, and the step from A to S is the largest.

## 9. Time, communication and escape inside a gate

### Takeaway
A normal gate can be walked out of at will, and parties do retreat to fetch help. A red gate cuts all contact with the outside and runs a day per outside hour. Bosses can block the exit, people can be lost inside for years, and what happens inside is effectively beyond the law.

[Fandom-API-dependent] Nothing electrical works inside a dungeon, so there are no phones, cameras or powered tools; System dungeons lock the player in unless a teleport stone is broken.
