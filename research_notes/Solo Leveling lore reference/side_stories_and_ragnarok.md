## 1. The side stories and epilogue (novel ch. ~244-270, manhwa ch. ~180-200)

### Takeaway
The side stories are a 27-chapter novel epilogue (2017-18) adapted as a 21-chapter webtoon run (January to May 2023) drawn by DUBU's former colleagues under the group credit DISCIPLES (REDICE STUDIO). They show Jinwoo living in the reset, gate-free timeline after a 27-year solo war against the Monarchs: schoolboy and track athlete, target of Detective Woo Jinchul's investigation, reluctant ally of the wary Rulers, later a police detective nicknamed "The Phantom", husband of Cha Hae-In and father of Suho, whose toddler shadow powers and teenage dungeon dreams are the direct hand-off to Ragnarok. I could not obtain a reliable chapter-by-chapter breakdown; what follows is the sourced set of events.

Publication and credits
- **[Novel]** The side story ran as 27 web chapters on KakaoPage from August 7, 2017 to March 13, 2018, was printed by D&C Media (Papyrus imprint) on April 18, 2018 as a separate side-story volume, and in the 2019 reissue is bundled into volume 8 of the main series. MAL lists the print side-story book as 12 chapters. — [MyAnimeList: Solo Leveling Side Story](https://myanimelist.net/manga/155115/Solo_Leveling_Side_Story)
- **[Novel]** Korean first edition: 13 main volumes plus the side-story volume ("나 혼자만 레벨업 외전", April 18, 2018); revised edition: 8 volumes. Whole novel serialisation: July 25, 2016 to March 13, 2018. — [Korean Wikipedia](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/나_혼자만_레벨업); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling)
- **[Novel]** English: Yen Press vol. 8 (the final novel volume) was released July 18, 2023; the listing's blurb covers only the Antares war and does not mention the side stories. — [Yen Press vol. 8 (novel)](https://yenpress.com/titles/9781975319410-solo-leveling-vol-8-novel)
- **[Webtoon]** The main webtoon ended December 29, 2021 with chapter 179. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling)
- **[Webtoon]** DUBU (Jang Sung-rak, CEO of Redice Studio) died July 23, 2022 of a cerebral haemorrhage. The side-story webtoon was announced January 9-10, 2023 as a tribute, to be drawn by "a group of artists" who were his colleagues; it launched on KakaoPage on January 21, 2023 with weekly Wednesday updates, and was published January to May 2023 with Chugong returning as writer. — [Anime Corner](https://animecorner.me/solo-leveling-webtoon-to-get-side-story-in-honor-of-dubu/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling)

The 27-year war and the return (bridge from main story; manhwa ch. 177-179)
- **[Both]** After Antares falls (manhwa ch. 177), Jinwoo asks the Rulers to use the Cup of Reincarnation one last time. They warn that the Monarchs will come back with their memories and that the Cup cannot be used again. Manhwa ch. 178-179 are the epilogue; ch. 178 mentions him fighting Yogumunt, Monarch of Transfiguration, before Antares. — [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-new-final-arc-release/); [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/solo-leveling-manhwa-ending-explained/)
- **[Both]** He returns to his younger body, lives briefly as a schoolboy, then enters the gap between dimensions and kills the Monarchs one by one over 27 years, leaving Antares for last. Only about two years pass outside, so afterwards he gradually ages his appearance. Gates, magic beasts and hunters no longer exist; only the Rulers' former vessels know what he did. — [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/solo-leveling-manhwa-ending-explained/)
- **[Both]** Shadows that already existed before the reset point (the non-human ones) stay with him in the gap; shadows raised later, such as Iron and Greed, are gone because those people are alive again. — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-ending-jinwoo-fate-explained/); [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/solo-leveling-every-character-fate-explained/)
- **[Both]** The manhwa never shows the 27-year war or the second Antares fight, and the epilogue skips how Jinwoo regained his strength; CBR calls the manhwa ending a more rushed version of the novel's. — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-ending-jinwoo-fate-explained/); [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-new-final-arc-release/)

When they meet, a touch from Jinwoo gives Jinchul knowledge of the erased timeline and he keeps the secret. — [Yen Press vol. 14](https://yenpress.com/titles/9798400904646-solo-leveling-vol-14-comic); [The Outer Haven review](https://www.theouterhaven.net/comic-review-solo-leveling-vol-14/)

- **[Webtoon, vol. 14]** Jinwoo joins the school track team with no experience because he wants to meet someone at competitions: Cha Hae-In, a track star in this timeline. At a meet he also runs into Kim Chul (the former shadow Iron), who feels a strong urge to kneel to him. — [The Outer Haven](https://www.theouterhaven.net/comic-review-solo-leveling-vol-14/); [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/solo-leveling-every-character-fate-explained/)

- [Web-2] **[Novel]** Story by story, part 1. A reader's notes on Yen Press novel vol. 8 show how the English novel carries the material: ten "Bonus Stories" and six "Epilogue" parts after the main text, with no web chapter numbers. Bonus 1: an Association man tries to make Jinwoo give up hunting to save his life, then looks for healers and others to support the hunters. ## 2. Solo Leveling: Ragnarok publication facts

### Takeaway
Ragnarok is a sequel/spin-off web novel by Daul (not Chugong), published by D&C Media's Papyrus imprint on KakaoPage from April 10, 2023 to July 2025 and complete at 375 chapters. The Redice Studio webtoon launched July 31, 2024; it ran two seasons (ch. 1-47, 48-68) drawn by JIN, paused in January 2026 when JIN left for military service, and resumed in Korea as Season 3 on November 5, 2026 with a new artist (Sanjimseung, credited in English as M.Beast). The latest Korean episode as of 2026-11-16 is 72.

## 3. Ragnarok's premise and plot so far

### Takeaway
With one of the universe-creating "Absolute Ones" dead, the other creator gods (the Itarim, or Outer Gods) move to claim Jinwoo's masterless world. Jinwoo leaves Earth to fight them alongside the Rulers, sealing his son's powers and memories first; when gates reopen and the invasion reaches Earth, Beru unseals university student Sung Suho and a modified System starts levelling him. The webtoon (72 chapters) has covered his awakening, a Shadow Dungeon, the A-rank antagonist Lee Minsung and the Apostle Tiel, a Job Change Quest, a hunter-prison break and an ongoing S-rank raid over Seoul; the completed novel goes much further and differently, to Suho becoming a Monarch in his own right and Jinwoo's return.

## 4. Ragnarok's power system and how it differs

### Takeaway
Suho runs on a version of Jinwoo's System that was modified for him and trains him faster, but his shadow power is deliberately narrower and different: without Shadow Preservation he can keep only a small number of soldiers, and his signature job skill, Shadow Creation, turns shadows permanently into levelling equipment that he can hand to other hunters. He also draws "bond skills" from allied Monarch heirs. Hunter ranks (E to S, then National Level), the Hunter's Association and the old guilds are back, staffed by the same people re-awakened.

### Inferences
- The design shift is from "one player who hoards an army" to "one player who is a crafting and buffing hub for a party": shadows become a consumable resource spent on permanent, levelling, tradeable gear, and guild S-ranks stay relevant. CBR and Screen Rant both frame this as the sequel's defining mechanical idea.
- The three job options map neatly to three build identities: summoner (Heir of the Shadow), duelist (Soul Striker) and crafter/irregular.
- Because villains and enemy heirs also have Systems and growth mechanics (Arsha's System, Tiel reassembling himself), Ragnarok treats "levelling" as a general property of the setting, no longer unique to the protagonist.

New antagonists come in three layers: the Itarim themselves, their mass-produced Apostles (Tiel is the named one on Earth), and human or monster proxies (villain hunters, hostile Monarch heirs, revenant Monarchs). New friendly factions are the Monarch heirs and their peoples. New places are mostly otherworldly lands reached through gates. Individual monster species are poorly documented in accessible sources.

## 6. How Ragnarok expands or changes the cosmology

### Takeaway
Ragnarok reframes the original's single Absolute Being as one of many creator gods, the Itarim, each of whom made a universe and set its creatures fighting for entertainment. The Rulers' killing of their own creator left Jinwoo's universe, with its effectively unlimited mana, ownerless, and the other Itarim are racing to seize it through Apostles. Jinwoo is off-world holding them back with the Rulers, strong enough to fight them off but not to kill them.

### Cited Findings
- **[Both]** The Itarim are ancient beings who existed before time, created their own universes and species, and, bored, made their creations wage endless wars for amusement. — [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-ragnarok-itarim-outer-gods-origin-explained/); [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-ragnarok-apostles-vs-monarchs-rulers-explained/)

### Inferences
- Terminology overlaps and trips readers up: "Absolute Being" (the original's single creator), "Absolute Ones" (official English synopsis for the group), "Itarim" (the in-story name) and "Outer Gods" (common English gloss) all refer to the same class of entity.
- The Titans of Chugong's side stories and the Apostles of Ragnarok both fill the role of "external invaders drawn to Jinwoo's world"; one blog merges them into the same first wave, but no stronger source confirms that Titans are Itarim forces.

## 7. Other official material

### Takeaway
Beyond the novel and manhwa there is an anime (two seasons, a compilation film, a sequel film announced July 2026), a Netflix live-action series in production, an August 2026 art book, and a Netmarble game line whose original stories now extend the lore, most importantly Solo Leveling: Karma, which will depict the unseen 27-year war. No further Chugong-written sequel exists, though he has floated an Igris prequel.

## 8. Fan reception and common points of confusion about canon

### Takeaway
The side stories are received as a pleasant, slightly over-long epilogue to an ending many found rushed. Ragnarok is contested: its webtoon is popular and praised by some critics for worldbuilding and for Suho, its novel ended to open disappointment, and Chugong's own remarks place it as an alternate-universe spin-off, so "is Ragnarok canon?" has no single agreed answer. No reddit threads could be read; reception below is from press coverage and article comment sections.

### Cited Findings

Side stories and the ending
- The finale divided readers: an anticlimactic first Antares kill by the Rulers, the Cup of Reincarnation erasing consequences, the second Antares fight and the 27-year war left off-page, and the Rulers' ultimatum to Jinwoo seen as hypocritical. — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-ending-jinwoo-fate-explained/)

### Inferences
- A practical canon ladder for a design team: (1) Chugong's novel including its side stories; (2) the manhwa and its side stories, where they differ in presentation only; (3) Ragnarok novel and Ragnarok webtoon as two separate, officially licensed alternate continuations that disagree with each other; (4) Netmarble game stories; (5) explicit what-ifs. This ordering is my synthesis of the sources above, not an official statement.
- Much English-language "lore" about the Itarim circulates through fan wikis and power-scaling blogs; the named-Itarim and full-Apostle lists in particular should be verified against the Fandom pages or the licensed Wuxiaworld text before being relied on.
