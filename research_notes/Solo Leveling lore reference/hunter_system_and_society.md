## Awakening: how does a person become a hunter, what is fixed at awakening, what is a re-awakening / second awakening, and how rare is each?

### Takeaway
Awakening is an apparently random event that gives a person magic power; the amount of power is set at that moment and normally never grows, so rank is for life. A second ("double") awakening is a rare, poorly explained exception that the Association actively tests for, and the Association licence, not the awakening, is what makes someone a hunter.

### Cited Findings

- [explainer] Awakened people are not automatically hunters. Only holders of a hunter's licence may join dungeon raids, and some awakened people stay in ordinary life. — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/); [Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-hunters-guilds-explained/); [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-the-six-hunter-types-explained/)
- [explainer] Magic strength is fixed at awakening: rank reflects only the magic power a person awakened with, not skill or combat talent, and ordinary hunters cannot level up. — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling)
- [explainer] A double awakening is a rare, largely mysterious case where a hunter awakens a new level of magic power after the first awakening; it is the only normal route to a rank change. — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/); [Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-hunters-guilds-explained/)

### Inferences
- For game design, the canon rule is "your rank is your ceiling"; progression fantasy in the story comes only from the protagonist breaking that rule. A game that lets every player grow is already departing from the setting, which is a free design choice.
- Re-awakening is treated as an administrative event (testing, re-registration) as much as a power-up, which gives a natural hook for a "re-assessment" ceremony at a base.

### Gaps
- Amended 2026-11-16: the saved Reawakening page says only a few cases exist worldwide (wiki cites novel ch. 39) and gives example rank jumps, but there is still no sourced number of cases and no named ordinary hunter confirmed to have had one. Fan sites say no named character has.
- No reliable source for the total number of awakened people or licensed hunters in Korea. The saved wiki pages give none either.
- Closed 2026-11-16 by the `[Fandom-API]` lines above (was an unverified recollection): Association staff and a guild scout both suspect Jinwoo of a reawakening, and he applies for re-assessment himself.

## Ranks: E, D, C, B, A, S and National Level Hunter. What defines each, how are they measured, how many hold each rank, and how large is the gap between ranks?

### Takeaway
Six official ranks, E to S, are assigned by measuring mana output on a device; S simply means "the device cannot measure it", so S is an open-ended catch-all with large internal gaps. "National Level" is an honorific above S held by about five people, not a seventh measured rank. Hard population numbers per rank were not found. Addendum (Web-2): the fifth title-holder is named, as Antoine Martinez, only outside the original story (sequel and game); Japan had eleven S ranks before Jeju; the sequel states a rough tenfold step between ranks below S. Per-rank population numbers are still missing.

### Inferences
- The gap between ranks is roughly an order of magnitude per step (CBR's ten C ranks per B rank is the only quantified step), and the gap inside S is larger than any gap below it.
- Rank is a measure of mana quantity only, so two hunters of the same rank can differ greatly in usefulness depending on class and skills.
- Korea is portrayed as S rank poor: ten S ranks after Jinwoo, against a United States said to have the most.

## Hunter classes / roles: what does each do in a raid, typical abilities, how common, named members, and how do classes combine?

### Takeaway
Six classes recur across sources: Fighter, Mage, Assassin, Tanker, Ranger and Healer, with class independent of rank. Tankers pull monster attention off the fragile Mages and Healers, Healers keep the party alive and buff it, and the rest deal damage. "Support" and "Summoner" are not separate official classes in the sources read; barrier, summoning and curse magic sit under Mage, and necromancy is unique to Jinwoo.

### Cited Findings
- [explainer] Hunters split first into combat and non-combat types, then into six class types. Class is unrelated to rank: any class can appear at any rank (Lee Joohee is a B rank Healer, Cha Hae-In an S rank Fighter). — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-the-six-hunter-types-explained/)
- **Fighter.** Melee damage dealer using swords, axes, maces, spears or fists; high attack, decent defence, good stamina. — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-the-six-hunter-types-explained/); [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- **Mage.** Physically weak, strong at range, hits groups; specialisms are elemental, curse, summoning and barrier magic. — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-the-six-hunter-types-explained/); [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- **Assassin.** Close-quarters specialist built on speed and stealth who kills before the enemy reacts. — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-the-six-hunter-types-explained/); [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- **Tanker.** Heavy defence at the cost of agility; uses aggro (taunt) abilities to draw monsters away from Mages and Healers. — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/); [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-the-six-hunter-types-explained/)
- **Ranger.** Long-range physical attacker using archery magic with bows; shares the Mage's weakness up close and covers allies' blind spots. — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-the-six-hunter-types-explained/); [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- **Healer.** Heals wounds and casts buffs; very weak one to one; described as essential to high-level raids. — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-the-six-hunter-types-explained/); [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- [explainer] A well-rounded party matters, but rank still caps what a party can beat. — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- [explainer] Jinwoo is formally a Mage type (his necromancy is summoning magic) even though he fights like an Assassin with daggers. — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-the-six-hunter-types-explained/)
- [fan lists] Some fan lists count seven types or treat Necromancer as an unofficial extra class tied to Jinwoo; others count five combat classes and leave Healer out. — [FandomWire, seven types](https://fandomwire.com/all-7-types-of-hunters-in-solo-leveling-explained/); [FandomWire, five combat classes](https://fandomwire.com/solo-leveling-all-combat-classes-ranked-by-power/) (titles and search summaries only; pages refused fetches)

## How do abilities and skills work for ordinary hunters (innate skills, mana, weapons and artifacts, rune stones)?

### Takeaway
Ordinary hunters awaken with a fixed mana pool and a small set of class-typed skills, and they improve mainly by buying better equipment, not by growing. The sources read say little about rune stones or artifacts directly, so this question is thinly evidenced. ## The Korean Hunters Association: roles, key people, and the laws that apply to hunters

### Takeaway
The Korean Hunters Association is the government body that licenses and ranks hunters, finds and secures gates, certifies guilds, staffs raids from freelancers, and polices hunters through its Surveillance Team. It exists partly because the ordinary state cannot restrain hunters. Formal laws are barely described in the sources read. Addendum 2026-11-16: the `[Fandom-API]` lines below name several working rules (no solo raids, headcounts, gate permits, convict hunters); no statute text or penalty was found.

### Cited Findings
- [explainer] The Association is South Korea's principal government organisation for hunters, gates, dungeons and raids. It is a state institution, not a profit-seeking guild, and does mostly administration and oversight, only sometimes taking part in hunting. — [Fiction Horizon](https://fictionhorizon.com/solo-leveling-the-korean-hunters-association-explained/)
- [explainer] Functions listed: certifies hunters and sets their rank (an Evaluation Office handles registration); certifies guilds; locates and secures gates and responds to possible dungeon breaks; invites guilds and freelance hunters to clear dungeons; keeps a staff member near a gate, especially when it is cleared; collects and oversees objects taken from dungeons. — [Fiction Horizon](https://fictionhorizon.com/solo-leveling-the-korean-hunters-association-explained/)
- [explainer] The Association decides who may challenge which gates and sets the rules for founding a guild. A hunter may only enter gates of their own rank or lower. — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-guilds-explained/)
- [explainer] Its political role is to stop any one guild becoming too powerful, because the government and military cannot check hunters on their own. — [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)

### Inferences
- The Association combines four real-world roles: licensing board, emergency service, labour exchange for freelancers, and internal affairs police. A game hub that offers rank assessment, a job board and a "surveillance" faction is close to canon.
- The convict-hunter raid shows the Association is short of hunters and will fill headcounts with whoever is available, including prisoners.
- Crimes inside dungeons are hard to prove because the only witnesses are the party; both murder plots in season 1 depend on that.

## Guilds: major Korean guilds, their masters, specialities and rankings; structure; recruitment and pay

### Takeaway
Guilds are private companies of hunters that hold most of the economic power. Korea has five great guilds (Hunters, White Tiger, Reaper, Fame, Knights) and later Jinwoo's tiny Ahjin Guild. Large guilds are organised into strike squads, mining squads and administrative departments, and they compete for high-rank recruits with large signing packages.

## The economy: what gates are worth, uses of essence stones and mana crystals, income splits, low-rank versus S rank earnings, artifact trade, and what Jinwoo was paid

### Takeaway
Hunting is a very large industry built on materials taken from dungeons: essence stones from monsters, mana crystals mined from the dungeon, and monster corpses. Rights to a gate can be bought, income scales steeply with gate rank, and low-rank hunters can fail to cover their equipment costs. Concrete prices are scarce and the few figures found come from snippets or blogs. Addendum 2026-11-16: the `[Fandom-API]` lines below add won figures for C rank gates, seat-filler pay and the Yoojin deal, some of them contradicting each other; unit prices, tax rates and salaries are still missing. Addendum (Web-2): in the anime the resale is 600 million won for three C rank gates against a going price of 70 million each, and seat-fillers get 3 million won per gate. The 40 and 10 percent tax rates trace to one wiki passage seen only as a search snippet. Stated unit prices, prices of B, A and S rank gates, and salaries are still missing.

### Inferences
- Value flows from three things a design team can model separately: monster drops (essence stones, corpses), environmental resources (mana crystals needing labour to extract), and unique boss rewards (rune stones, artifacts).
- The headcount rule and purchasable gate rights together create a labour market for low-rank hunters as paid seat-fillers.
- If the tax snippet is right, tax policy is the state's lever for pushing hunters into guilds.

## Daily life of a low-rank hunter: Association raid parties, risks, insurance, stigma, and exploitation

### Takeaway
A low-rank hunter is a freelancer who joins Association-organised pick-up raids for small returns and real risk of death, and who can be used as bait or a seat-filler by stronger hunters. Jinwoo's nickname, "the weakest hunter", marks the bottom of that ladder. Insurance and compensation were not documented in sources read. Addendum 2026-11-16: a `[Fandom-API]` line below says hunter work carried healthcare benefits; death compensation is still undocumented. Addendum (Web-2): a search snippet of a fan guide says the licence also carries death insurance for the family; no amount or chapter was found.

## How do the anime's Japanese localisation and the Korean original differ in names for people, guilds and places?

### Takeaway
The Japanese-market version, which began with the Japanese novel and manga release and carries into the Japanese broadcast of the anime, moves the story from Korea to Japan and renames almost every Korean character. The international release keeps the Korean names and setting. Japan's role as antagonist in the Jeju arc is reassigned to a fictional country.

### Cited Findings
- [Japanese version] Seoul becomes Tokyo and Jeju Island becomes the fictional Kanan Island. — [Anime Feminist](https://www.animefeminist.com/solo-levelings-adaptation-anti-korean-sentiment/)
- [Japanese version] The Japanese hunters who betray Korea in the original become hunters of a fictional country, the DFN. In the original the betrayal is planned from the start; in the Japanese version the DFN abandons the Japanese hunters only when things go wrong. Goto Ryuji becomes "Ryu" and Matsumoto Shigeo becomes "Geo". — [Anime Feminist](https://www.animefeminist.com/solo-levelings-adaptation-anti-korean-sentiment/)
- [anime] The anime also removes national flags and decor from both chairmen's offices and cuts some scenes, including the fight between Hanekawa and Park Jongsoo. — [Anime Feminist](https://www.animefeminist.com/solo-levelings-adaptation-anti-korean-sentiment/)
- [anime] Two versions exist: the Japan broadcast uses the Japanese names and setting, while the international (Crunchyroll) release keeps the Korean names and setting. — [Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/anime/why-did-solo-leveling-anime-change-names-japanese-version-explained); [EarlyGame](https://earlygame.com/news/shows-movies/solo-leveling-why-the-anime-has-two-versions); [TMDB discussion](https://www.themoviedb.org/tv/127532/discuss/6599b7f2bd588b014deae6f2) (search summaries)
- The changes originate in the Japanese novel release, not in the anime. — [Anime Feminist](https://www.animefeminist.com/solo-levelings-adaptation-anti-korean-sentiment/)
- The title in Japan is 俺だけレベルアップな件 (*Ore dake Level Up na Ken*); the webtoon runs on Piccoma there. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling)

### Inferences
- Any reference a design team finds may use either name set. A hunter called Shun Mizushino in "Tokyo" is the same character as Sung Jinwoo in Seoul, and English sources drawing on Japanese material mix the two.
- The sequel and the games add terms that are not in the original story, so facts should be tagged by work as well as by medium.

### Gaps
- Japanese-version names for the guilds were not found in any source read, and none appear in the saved wiki pages either. Unverified recollection: Ahjin Guild is rendered 我進ギルド in Japanese.

- Differences between the novel, manhwa and anime in the hunter system itself (as opposed to names) were not documented by any source read; the explainers treat the three as one canon.
