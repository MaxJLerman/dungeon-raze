## 1. Production facts: staff, seasons, films, future projects, live action, streaming, awards

### Takeaway
The anime is an A-1 Pictures production directed by Shunsuke Nakashige with action direction by Yoshihiro Kanno and music by Hiroyuki Sawano; two seasons (25 episodes) aired in January to March of 2024 and 2025. As of 16 November 2026 the only officially announced anime continuation is the theatrical film "Solo Leveling: Beyond the System" (announced 3 July 2026, no release date); a TV Season 3 has not been announced by Aniplex, Crunchyroll or A-1 Pictures, although the rights holder D&C Media lists it in investor materials with a 2027 to 2028 target.

**Production timeline and scale**
- Aniplex producer Sota Furuhashi brought the project to A-1's Atsushi Kaneko in 2020. Pre-production began April 2021 and animation in October 2022. Kaneko's core A-1 team was 12 people; the total workforce was about 500 to 600. Each episode took 8 to 10 months with teams working in parallel. It was A-1 Pictures' first webtoon adaptation. Furuhashi's stated ambition was to beat Sword Art Online's animation. — [Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/anime/from-light-novel-to-anime-sensation-solo-leveling-producer-and-action-director-discuss-the-breakout-hit-242140.html)
- Furuhashi first read the source around 2019 and started the project because he thought its visuals would make a striking anime. Crunchyroll joined as co-producer. — [Cocotame interview, part 1](https://cocotame.jp/en/series/110047/)
- A standard TV episode has 20 to 30 key animators; production stages listed are storyboard, layout, director and animation director revisions, key animation, in-betweens, digital paint, special effects, compositing, then editing and sound. — [Anime Corner behind the scenes](https://animecorner.me/how-the-solo-leveling-anime-was-made-behind-the-scenes-at-a-1-pictures-ahead-of-the-season-2-finale/)
- Nine key frames amounted to less than two seconds of screen time; the report also says Sony's mocopi motion-capture system was used (the fetched summary said "for most of the animation", which was not cross-checked). For Ep 19 (S2E7) a single key animator drew all first key animation. Nakashige personally drew key frames for Ep 21 (S2E9), the mother's awakening. — [AWN](https://www.awn.com/animationworld/faith-trust-and-drinking-friends-secrets-solo-levelings-success)
- A Crunchyroll documentary on the first season's production exists (about 40 minutes), titled "The Leveling of Solo Leveling". — [Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/anime/from-light-novel-to-anime-sensation-solo-leveling-producer-and-action-director-discuss-the-breakout-hit-242140.html); [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-director-original-cartoon-expressions-anime-why-cut/)

## 3. Changes from the source

### Takeaway
The anime follows DUBU's manhwa closely in plot and key images but adds connective scenes for side characters, seeds the Jeju arc from the first minute of Ep 1, drops the manhwa's comic chibi faces, and lengthens or restages most major fights; in Season 2 it cuts comedy and some foreshadowing lines for pace. The director's stated reason is that the manhwa moves too fast to adapt directly, and the rights holder approved the changes.

## 4. Localisation: the Japanese version, Jeju, the Japanese antagonists, and English names

## 5. Combat staging: principles and fight-by-fight notes

### Takeaway
The anime stages combat as an escalation in three registers: grounded, clumsy and bloody while Jin-woo is weak; fast, acrobatic dagger work with dashes and midair clashes once he levels; and by Season 2 exchanges so fast they are drawn as coloured light trails, punctuated by slow motion, impact frames and sketch-style finishing blows. Named characters are hand drawn, crowds and the shadow army are 3D, and the signature moments are long continuous cuts and stripped-back monochrome finishers designed by action director Yoshihiro Kanno.

### Cited Findings

**Principles stated by staff**
- Jin-woo's choreography is deliberately staged to improve with his power. He begins weak and clumsy; after his reawakening the style moves "a little away from reality"; the producers say the action must be "constantly evolving". — [VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/maximizing-the-strength-of-anime-for-solo-leveling/)
- Kanno describes Jin-woo's style as brutal yet amateurish and studied live-action fight footage for flow. — [Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/anime/from-light-novel-to-anime-sensation-solo-leveling-producer-and-action-director-discuss-the-breakout-hit-242140.html)
- Environments are part of the fight, most of all in urban settings: characters throw, break and smash into the background, which adds drawing work. — [Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/anime/from-light-novel-to-anime-sensation-solo-leveling-producer-and-action-director-discuss-the-breakout-hit-242140.html)
- The long-legged, realistic character design made running and jumping hard to animate; the team's answer was deformed, stretched bodies and faces to carry momentum. — [AWN](https://www.awn.com/animationworld/faith-trust-and-drinking-friends-secrets-solo-levelings-success)
- Named characters are hand drawn; mass enemies are 3D. The shadow army was planned in 2D but its simple dark forms lacked colour and line cues, so after tests it was moved to 3D, which gave more range. — [VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/maximizing-the-strength-of-anime-for-solo-leveling/)
- High Orcs are CG rendered to look 2D; background orcs reuse one model with varied tattoo patterns and colours. — [Anime Corner behind the scenes](https://animecorner.me/how-the-solo-leveling-anime-was-made-behind-the-scenes-at-a-1-pictures-ahead-of-the-season-2-finale/)
- Several animators work on each scene, giving fights varied texture, and key animators had room to improvise beyond the storyboard. — [VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/maximizing-the-strength-of-anime-for-solo-leveling/)
- Violence is meant to have consequences: Furuhashi's example is that a sword cut to the arm removes the arm, and the series opens on the hero's death. — [VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/maximizing-the-strength-of-anime-for-solo-leveling/)
- Density: Ep 4 had four major fight scenes in 21 minutes and almost every other episode was action heavy; animators were rotated constantly. — [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/solo-leveling-anime-director-casting-backlash-controversy/)
- Tags on Kanno's and Torii's clips list the working vocabulary: background animation, debris, impact frames, smears, lightning, sparks, smoke, wind, fabric, liquid, ice. — [Sakugabooru](https://www.sakugabooru.com/post?tags=solo_leveling)
- Season 2's shadow army scenes, with many characters fighting at once, needed substantial production time. — [Cocotame interview, part 2](https://cocotame.jp/en/series/110054/)

### Inferences
These are design readings of the cited material, offered for the game team; they are not statements by the anime's staff.
- **Speed is shown by subtraction.** At low speed the anime draws full bodies and environment damage. At top speed it removes the bodies and leaves colour-coded trails (blue for the hero, red for the enemy) and impact flashes. A game can mirror this: at high attack speed, hide detail and show trail, flash and result.
- **Finishers change the rendering style, not just the damage number.** Igris and Baran both end on monochrome, sketch-like frames. A brief palette-stripped hold on a killing blow is the series' signature punctuation.
- **Boss fights are phased with a visible trigger.** Igris: sword, then fists, then desperate stab. Kargalgan: curses, then giant form. Ant King: brawl, then winged retreat and transformation with a change of music. Each phase removes an option from the player character (weapons bounce off, stealth is revealed, army is wiped, he is disarmed).
- **The hero's kit is denied before it is rewarded.** Stealth is countered by mages in Ep 12, potions are locked, the escape item is knocked away twice. The fights get their tension from disabled tools because the hero rarely loses outright.
- **Scale is sold from the hero's eye line.** First-person shots, pans from behind the hero, storyboards that set his size against the boss, and bystanders bracing against shockwaves. Bystanders reacting is the cheapest scale cue.
- **Stealth reads as absence and ambush.** The one stealth duel is staged as an attack from nowhere and a crippling first strike, then resolved by a debuff aura ("killing intent" as a stat reduction).
- **Army fights are made of small vignettes.** Each summon gets a readable micro-fight; the hero duels the boss in the middle. Sound is thinned so individual hits read.
- **Long continuous shots are used once per big episode** as a showpiece (the 26-second cut).

## 6. Visual language: colour, light, gates, auras, shadows

### Takeaway
The anime is graded like live action, with a restrained palette in which a small set of saturated accents carry meaning: blue for Jin-woo, shifting to purple once he becomes the Shadow Monarch, and red for enemies and danger. That the System windows share his blue is supported here only by fan recreations (section 7). Large fights reduce to those colours, and shadows are dark simple silhouettes, which is why the army had to be built in 3D to stay readable.

### Cited Findings
- Nakashige avoided cartoon-like expressions and used compositions, colour and photographic processing similar to live-action footage, because he felt the work needed high-end visuals. ## 10. Reception of the action, and common criticisms

### Takeaway
Critics and awards bodies treat the action animation as among the best on television, and it won Crunchyroll's Best Action two years running and Best Animation for Season 2. The recurring criticisms are about what the fights mean, not how they look: low stakes, wins by luck or raw stats, thin side characters, a lead who becomes a power-fantasy pose, uneven CG in crowd scenes, and in Season 2 a pace that fans felt was too fast.

### Inferences
- The praise and the criticism point at the same design fact: the series optimises for the feeling of overwhelming growth, so its best-received fights are the few where the hero is under-levelled and has to solve something (statues, Cerberus, Igris, Baran), and its most-criticised ones are those he wins on stats. A hack-and-slash that wants the spectacle without the complaint should keep some mechanic the player must read and answer even when over-levelled.
- The CG complaint is specific to close-ups. Crowd CG at distance was accepted; the same models pushed toward the camera were not.
