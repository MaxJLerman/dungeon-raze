## Item grades and rarity: tiers, how grade relates to stats and price, System items versus hunter artifacts

### Takeaway
System items carry a letter rarity (E, C, B, A, S seen on named items, plus "None" for junk and "?" or "??" for things the System cannot measure), and attack values climb steeply with grade: +10 for an ordinary shop sword, +25 at C, +75 at B, +110 at A, +220 to +350 at S and +1500 for the unmeasurable Kamish's Wrath. System gear outperforms what human craftsmen can make, and Jinwoo's System loot was appraised at tens of millions of won per piece on the real market.

### Inferences
- A workable rarity ladder for an original game drawn from these facts: Junk (no grade, vendor trash), E (basic consumables and keys), C, B, A, S, then an "unmeasurable" tier for one-of-a-kind items made from a calamity-class monster. No named D-rank item surfaced in this research, so D probably exists only by analogy with hunter and gate ranks.
- Weapon attack roughly triples from C to B, grows by about half from B to A, doubles from A to S, and jumps about five times from S to the unmeasurable tier. Higher grades also add secondary effects (stat bonuses at A, named passive effects at S).
- Knight Killer at 2,800,000 gold against a 100 million won appraisal implies roughly 36 won per gold, but this is a single data point and the two currencies are not officially convertible.
- An ordinary hunter's ₩3,000,000 sword gives +10 attack while System items appraised at 30 to 100 million won give +25 to +75. That suggests real-market price rises faster than the attack number.
- Consumable grade does not track power the same way gear grade does: Kasaka's Venom Gland is A and the Holy Water of Life is S, while basic potions are E.
- Update 2026-11-16, from the labelled lines above: a drop's grade is not tied to the boss's rank. An A-Rank boss dropped an S helmet, and S-Rank bosses dropped A-grade items next to S-grade ones. Grade seems to belong to the item, so one boss can drop a mix.
- Update 2026-11-16: the "??" and "???" marks cover more than one-of-a-kind weapons. The wiki's tables also use them for quest ingredients, a recipe, a permit and a key whose purpose is hidden, so "unmeasured" also works as a label for quest items.

### Gaps
- No source seen states the grade rules as a rule (for example what an S-rank item must have). The ladder above is assembled from individual items.
- Which System weapon was appraised at 30 million won in chapter 79 is still unknown. The saved wiki page for that chapter does not name it and describes it as a weapon that could be bought in the Store, so the earlier guess of Kasaka's Venom Fang looks doubtful. Needs a read of manhwa chapter 79 or the matching novel chapter.
- No D-rank item is named in the wiki's item tables. Whether the novel names one is still open.
- No sale prices for S-rank artifacts or any auction hammer prices were found.
- Novel-specific wording of grades (for example whether the novel says "rank", "class" or "grade") could not be checked. [Web-2] Answered for the English editions: the field is "Acquisition Difficulty" in the Yen Press print webtoon (read directly), in the Yen Press novel (`[snippet]`) and in the licensed translation of the sequel. The Korean wording was not checked.

## Weapons used by Jinwoo: grade, stats, effects, origin and replacement

### Takeaway
Jinwoo's weapon line runs Kim Sangshik's Sword, Kasaka's Venom Fang, Knight Killer, Baruka's Dagger, Demon King's Daggers and finally Kamish's Wrath, with the Demon King's Longsword and Orb of Avarice passed to others. Only one was bought; the rest were boss drops or a gift.

### Cited Findings
- **Kim Sangshik's Sword.** Type Sword, Attack +10, no rarity given, bought by its original owner for ₩3,000,000. A magical steel blade belonging to the hunter Kim Sangshik, who abandoned it in the double dungeon; it was absorbed into Jinwoo's System inventory when he became a Player. It was his main weapon in the Instant Dungeon Arc and snapped against the Blue Venom-Fanged Kasaka. — [Fandom: Kim Sangshik's Sword](https://solo-leveling.fandom.com/wiki/Kim_Sangshik's_Sword); [Daddy Jim: Kim Sangshik's Sword](https://daddyjim.com/solo-leveling/item/Kim-Sangshik-s-Sword)
- Version difference: the sword is two-handed in the source art and one-handed in the anime. — [Daddy Jim: Kim Sangshik's Sword](https://daddyjim.com/solo-leveling/item/Kim-Sangshik-s-Sword)
- **Kasaka's Venom Fang.** C-Rank dagger, Attack +25. Effects: Paralyze (stops the target moving, at a set chance) and Bleed (target loses 1% of health per second, which can kill). ### Inferences
- Replacement order with the trigger for each swap: sword breaks on the first boss; C dagger from that boss; B dagger bought when gold allowed, timed for an armoured-enemy dungeon (its bonus is against armour, and the Job Change Quest is full of knights); A dagger from a boss; S pair from the Demon Castle's final boss; unmeasurable pair as a gift from the strongest human. Each step is tied to a story milestone, not to grinding.
- Jinwoo dual wields from the Knight Killer and Baruka's Dagger stage onward (both are reported in use against Baran), which is why later top weapons come as pairs.
- Weapons that do not fit the hero's style are not wasted: the longsword goes to a sword-using summon and then an ally, the orb goes to a mage summon. This is a ready-made "hand-me-down to companions" loop.
- Named effects are short and legible: a chance-based crowd control, a percentage damage over time, a conditional damage bonus, a stat-scaling bonus, an automatic area proc, a scaling-with-wielder property.

### Inferences
- With all three jewels worn, the set gives +30 Strength, +30 Vitality, +30 Agility, +30 Perception and +50 Intelligence in total (each piece's own +20s plus +10 to everything). This is arithmetic on the cited numbers, not a figure stated by a source.
- The three jewels come from three different Demon Castle bosses on different floors, so the set is a dungeon-long collection goal that completes at the final boss.
- Damage reduction scales with grade and slot: B gauntlet 3%, B chestplate 7%, S helmet 15%, A four-piece set 19% total.
- A boss-dropped helmet at S outclasses a bought A-rank full set on raw reduction per slot, so drops beat the shop at the same stage.
- Update 2026-11-16, from the labelled lines above: the second inference is now confirmed. The earring comes at floor 50, the necklace at floor 75 and the ring at floor 100, so the set bonus steps up as the dungeon is climbed.
- Update 2026-11-16: armour equipped through the System is invisible, so the hero's look does not change with what he wears. A game that wants visible gear would be departing from the source here.
- Update 2026-11-16: elite armour drops are named for the enemy type that carried them (knight, assassin, archer, magician).

### Gaps
- The brief's "High-Ranked Knight's Helmet", "Warden's set pieces", "High-Ranked Assassin gear", "Gauntlets" and "boots" could only be matched to the Red Knight's Helmet, Warden's Necklace, Assassin's Boots, High Knight's Gauntlet and Truth Seeker's Shoes above. - No information found on how many accessory slots the System allows, or on whether the Warden's Necklace and the Demon Monarch's Necklace can be worn together.
- The stats of the High Magician's Ring, Assassin's Shoes and Archer's Gloves are not on the wiki, and no saved page says where the ring came from.

## Consumables: potions, the Holy Water of Life, venom gland, ingredients, status recovery, random boxes

### Takeaway
Basic potions are E-rank and restore fixed amounts; the standout consumables are a risky buff elixir (Kasaka's Venom Gland) and a crafted S-rank cure-all (the Holy Water of Life) made from three boss-dropped ingredients in a batch of six bottles. Sources disagree on whether Jinwoo's potions can be used on other people. ### Inferences
- The Holy Water of Life is a model for a capstone crafting recipe: three ingredients, one from each of three escalating bosses in a single long dungeon, a fixed small batch, and a strict "illness, not injury" scope that keeps it from replacing healing.
- The venom gland is a model for a trade-off consumable that a later passive turns into a pure benefit.
- The potion transfer conflict is most likely a version difference (novel versus manhwa and anime) or a difference between handing over an item and applying it yourself. Designers should not treat either reading as settled canon.
- Update 2026-11-16, from the labelled lines above: the second reading holds on the wiki. Handing a potion over is blocked because the Player cannot transact, and pouring it into someone's mouth works. The same pages show this in the manhwa and the anime, so it is not a version difference.
- Update 2026-11-16: healing has three separate limits in the labelled lines. A healing potion fails below some health floor, the Holy Water covers illness only, and Status Recovery is tied to quest completion. Each tool has a gap the others do not fill.
- Update 2026-11-16: the two box types read as "the item you would pick" against "the item the story needs you to have". Both documented boxes held keys to later dungeons.

## Keys and special items: dungeon keys, Demon Castle key and permit, Black Heart, rune stones

### Takeaway
Keys are graded like other items (E for an ordinary instant dungeon, S for the Demon Castle) and act as tickets to System-only dungeons, with a separate permit gating floor progress. Rune stones are the one skill-teaching item ordinary hunters can also use: a monster-dropped stone that is broken to learn that monster's skill. Update 2026-11-16: the saved wiki pages show the Demon Castle Key came from a box won by overachieving on the daily quest, add a second key dropped by the gatekeeper, and place one floor permit with a demon clan that traded it for its survival; see the labelled lines.

### Inferences
- There are probably two distinct E-rank keys early on: one from a random box that opens the first instant dungeon and one dropped by that dungeon's boss that opens the next. The sources read state each fact separately.
- The Demon Castle uses a two-layer gate: an S-rank key to get in and a per-floor permit to go up. That is a direct template for a long dungeon with checkpointed progress.
- Rune stones are the bridge between the System and the ordinary hunter economy. The System simply hands Jinwoo the same kind of item that the market trades, including for human targets, which ordinary hunters could never get.
- Kamish's stone shows a class restriction on skill items: the strongest stone in the world went unused for years because it suited only mages.
- Update 2026-11-16: the first inference above, two distinct E-rank keys, has lost its support. It rested on the "rare drop" phrase, which the saved table attaches to the venom gland. The wiki documents one early Dungeon Key, from a random box.
- Update 2026-11-16: the Demon Castle gate has three layers on the wiki, not two: an S key that opens the dungeon at a set place in the real world, an A key from the gatekeeper boss that opens the castle door, and a permit for each floor after that.
- Update 2026-11-16: permits can be earned three ways in the labelled lines: kill the monster that carries one, search with summons, or bargain with a faction that holds one. The bargain was the fastest.
- Update 2026-11-16: keys act as timed story gates as well as tickets. The Black Key sat unreadable in the inventory until a level condition was met.
- Update 2026-11-16: a rune stone can be a quest reward as well as a drop, and the skill inside need not belong to the boss that the stone is named for.

## Materials and currencies: gold, essence stones, mana crystals, monster parts and crafters

### Takeaway
Two economies run side by side: System Gold, earned from kills, quests and selling loot to the store and spendable only there, and the real hunter economy built on essence stones from monsters, mana crystals mined from dungeons and artifacts sold at auction. The best gear in the setting is made from the remains of the strongest monsters.

### Cited Findings
- Gold comes from killing monsters, finishing quests and selling items back to the store. It exists only inside the System. — [Fandom System page via BreezeWiki](https://breezewiki.discard.no/solo-leveling/wiki/System); [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/weird-solo-leveling-system-facts-trivia/)

- Demon's Horns are a material for high-grade magic and fetch a high price. — [Fandom: Sung Jinwoo/Items](https://solo-leveling.fandom.com/wiki/Sung_Jinwoo/Items)
- **Essence stones** are found inside magic beasts and are the main income of hunters. Value depends on rank and quality. Low-rank stones fetch little because they flood the market; top-rank stones sell for millions. They are used to forge equipment and as an energy source. — [Fandom: Essence Stones](https://solo-leveling.fandom.com/wiki/Essence_Stones); [Daddy Jim: Essence Stones](https://daddyjim.com/solo-leveling/item/Essence-Stones)

### Inferences
- A three-material economy maps cleanly onto a game: a universal drop from every enemy whose value scales with enemy grade (essence stones), a gathered resource that needs a non-combat team (mana crystals), and rare boss body parts that decide what a crafted item can be (a fang long enough for daggers but not a sword).
- The 40% tax on independents is the setting's reason to join a guild. An original game could use the same lever for a faction or guild system.
- Crafting in the story is done by specialists (artificers, dwarves), not by the hero. A shop or smith who needs the player to bring boss parts fits the source better than free-form player crafting.

### Gaps
- No named human craftsman and no named maker of Kamish's Wrath were found. One source lists Thomas Andre as "crafter" but the wiki says he "sought to make" the weapon, which reads as commissioning it.
- No won prices per essence stone grade were found beyond "little" and "millions". The saved wiki pages add one won figure, about 1 billion won for a chamber of mana crystals, and none per stone or per grade.
- The term "magic core" appears on no saved wiki page. "Mana Stones", "magic gems" and "magic stones" do appear, in summaries, without being defined. Whether the novel uses different words for essence stones could not be checked. [Web-2] Partly answered: "Magical Core" is given as another name for an essence stone by Fiction Horizon and as a description of it by CBR, and the licensed Yen Press print webtoon prints "Essence Stone". The Yen Press novel's own word was not seen.
- Whether the System shop buys back equipment at a fixed fraction of its price is unknown. No saved page mentions a sell-back rate, and the prices in the wiki's tables are not marked as buying or selling prices.
- The wiki pages for Essence Stones, Mana Crystals, Guilds and the Korean Hunters Auction were not saved, so the colour-by-rank list, the mining details and the 40% tax example still rest on search summaries and one fan site.

## Notable gear of other hunters, gear given to shadows and allies, and the artifact market

### Takeaway
The story names very little equipment for other hunters. ### Inferences
- The scarcity of named gear for other S-Ranks is itself a finding: in this setting ordinary hunters are defined by class and skills, and named, statted equipment is a mark of the System or of calamity-class monsters.
- Top-tier remains are strategic national property, not market goods. A game could keep its best crafting materials out of the shop entirely and tie them to faction reputation or story choices.
- Update 2026-11-16, from the labelled lines above: the hand-down loop runs into the sequel, where the hero's son carries his father's first necklace and first dagger.

## Drop rules: normal monsters versus bosses, announcements, and whether ordinary hunters get drops

### Takeaway
For Jinwoo, kills produce System loot: gold, junk parts and the odd utility item from ordinary monsters, and named, graded equipment, ingredients and rune stones from bosses. Ordinary hunters get no System loot; they harvest essence stones from every corpse, mine crystals, and occasionally find a rune stone or dungeon treasure.

### Inferences
- A three-level drop table fits the source: trash mobs give currency, junk to sell and occasional utility consumables; elites give lower-grade equipment; bosses give one named signature item plus one or two of crafting ingredient, accessory, key or skill stone.
- Boss weapons are thematically derived from the boss (a fang, the boss's own dagger, the demon king's arsenal), which makes the drop predictable and motivating before the fight.
- Jinwoo's System loot appears in gates in the real world as well as in System dungeons, since Baruka's Dagger and the Jeju potions are used outside instant dungeons. No source read states this as a rule.
- Update 2026-11-16, from the labelled lines above: the boss table should drop the dungeon key from Kasaka. Keys on the wiki come from boxes and quests, or from a gatekeeper boss whose key opens the door behind it.
- Update 2026-11-16: there are two delivery channels. Kill loot lies on or in the body and has to be collected, by the hero or by his summons. Quest rewards arrive by message and can be claimed later, which lets a full heal be saved for the worst moment of a fight.
- Update 2026-11-16: ownership is enforced by the System, not by possession. An enemy who grabs the hero's weapon loses it at once, and potions cannot be handed over even to allies.

### Gaps
- The exact wording of the System's item-drop messages is still missing. The saved wiki pages only paraphrase them. They show drops lying on or in the corpse and quest rewards arriving by message, and do not say whether a picked-up drop goes to the inventory by itself. [Web-2] Answered for the sequel only: the licensed English translation of Ragnarok words the notice as "Item:" plus the quoted name plus "has been obtained" or "has been acquired", or "Obtained" plus the name after a kill, followed by an item window (see the labelled lines above). The item-drop line of the original novel, the manhwa and the anime was still not found in quotation.
- No drop rates are stated anywhere beyond the single phrase "rare drop". Whether another hunter can see a drop before Jinwoo picks it up is not covered; the saved pages only cover items he has already taken out.
- Which System items can be handed to other people is known by example only (potions no; coats, weapons and the Orb yes). No saved page states the rule that separates them.
- No source read says how often ordinary hunters find rune stones.

### Inferences
- "Rasaka" and "Raikan" look like older or alternative romanisations that survive on the wiki next to the official webtoon and anime spellings "Kasaka" and "Lycan". No source read says which translation each comes from.
- The brief's "Demon Monarch's Daggers", "Orb of Greed" and "High-Ranked Knight's Helmet" were not seen as such in any source read. They are plausible alternative renderings of the Demon King's Daggers, the Orb of Avarice and the Red Knight's Helmet.
- Update 2026-11-16, from the labelled lines above: the first inference needs correcting. The wiki tags "Raikan" and "Rasaka" as the webtoon forms and "Lycan" and "Casaka" as the novel forms, while "Kasaka" and "Lycan" are what its anime pages use. The webtoon the wiki editors read may have been a fan translation (its translation page says the webtoon was still fan-translated when that page was written), so the tags show which medium a name was seen in, not which name is official.
- Update 2026-11-16: a rough pattern in the labelled lines is that anime coverage prefers plainer or more literal words (Teleportation Stone, Elixir of Life, Sphere, Crimson, Full Recovery) and the webtoon tags carry the game-flavoured ones (Hearthstone, Warden's, Dominator's Touch).
- Update 2026-11-16: "Demon Monarch's Daggers" and "Orb of Greed" from the brief still appear on no saved wiki page.
- [Web-2] Update 2026-11-16, from the labelled lines above: "Demon Monarch's Dagger" is the name printed in the licensed Yen Press webtoon volumes, so that part of the brief was the official form all along. "Orb of Greed" was not seen anywhere in this round either; the licensed print webtoon has "Sphere of Avarice".
- [Web-2] Update 2026-11-16: three layers of English names are now visible. The wiki's page titles and "webtoon" tags (Demon King's, Baran, Orb, Rasaka, Dominator's Touch, Hearthstone) look like fan-translation forms. The Yen Press novel and print webtoon agree with each other on most terms (Kasaka, Demon Monarch, Balan, Demon's Castle, instance dungeon, Acquisition Difficulty). Anime coverage sits between the two. A game that wants to avoid echoing any one licensed text has all three sets to steer away from.
