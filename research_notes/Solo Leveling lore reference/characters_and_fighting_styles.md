## Sung Jinwoo: personality, physical change, dagger fighting style, relationships, arc

### Takeaway
Jinwoo starts as a timid, slight, permanently bandaged E-rank and becomes a tall, cold-in-combat dagger fighter whose style is assassin-like (speed, stealth, intimidation, twin daggers, later telekinesis and bare hands), even though his listed class is Mage (necromancer). His warmth survives only for family and a short list of people who stood by him.

### Cited Findings

**Personality and how it changes**
- Before his reawakening he is timid, insecure and privately ashamed of his weakness and his family's money troubles; afterwards he is bolder and confident, polite and easygoing in person, indifferent to fame and money, but ruthless and disdainful toward enemies in battle (version not distinguished) — [MAL: Jin-woo Sung](https://myanimelist.net/character/174185/Jin-woo_Sung)
- The turning point is the double dungeon (Cartenon Temple): being left for dead by his party teaches him that kindness invites exploitation, after which he stops risking himself for noble causes and concentrates on survival and power; he remains ruthless toward anyone who threatens his family, yet still acts selflessly at times (for example going to Jeju Island to rescue the remaining Korean S-ranks) (version not distinguished) — [Daddy Jim: Sung Jinwoo](https://daddyjim.com/solo-leveling/character/Sung-Jinwoo)
- [anime] In the Kang Taeshik episode he tells Taeshik he does not care about the murdered convicts, only about his two former party members; one reviewer read this as the first clear sign of his hardening — [Anime Corner, S1 ep 9 review](https://animecorner.me/solo-leveling-episode-9-hunter-vs-hunter/)
- [anime] After Kim Sangshik dies, Jinwoo is shaken because he had wanted the man alive so he could keep blaming him for the double dungeon — [Daddy Jim: Episode 9](https://daddyjim.com/solo-leveling/episode/Episode-9)

**Physical transformation**
- Before: small and slight, long bushy hair over his ears, a boyish face that looks younger than his teenage sister, plain blue hoodie and jeans, bandages on arms and legs as a matter of routine. After: tall, muscular, sharp-featured, grey eyes, black fringe at eye level; his eyes turn purple with power or strong emotion; he favours dark, streamlined clothes from tailored suits to long hooded coats. After the timeline reset he wears a black glove on his left hand to hide burn scars from the Antares rematch (version not distinguished) — [Daddy Jim: Sung Jinwoo](https://daddyjim.com/solo-leveling/character/Sung-Jinwoo)
- [anime, S1 ep 9] Former party members notice the change directly: Song Chiyul senses a different aura and Lee Joohee notices he has almost no injuries; he passes it off as daily exercise — [Daddy Jim: Episode 9](https://daddyjim.com/solo-leveling/episode/Episode-9)

### Inferences
- Jinwoo plays as an Assassin with a Mage's paperwork: the gap between his listed class and his actual close-range dagger style is a running plot device (rivals expect a summoner and get a duelist).
- His fights escalate in a readable order that maps well onto a game's progression: status-effect daggers versus armoured targets, then stealth and intimidation versus humans, then pure evasion, then bare-handed overpowering of a target his blades cannot cut.
- Visual shorthand for his power state is the purple eye glow; nearly every strong hunter has an equivalent eye-glow colour (see the class section).

## Korean S-rank hunters

### Takeaway
Korea's S-ranks are a deliberately mixed party: a sword fighter (Cha), a fire mage (Choi), a beast-shifter (Baek), a healer (Min), an archer (Lim) and a shield tank (Ma), with two much stronger Ruler-vessel outliers (Go Gunhee, limited by age, and Sung Il-Hwan) and one defector (Hwang Dongsoo). On the page they are consistently shown as weak by world standards, which is what makes Jinwoo's rise visible.

### Inferences
- The Korean S-rank roster is a textbook six-role party, and the Jeju raid is the story's one extended look at such a party working together and then failing against a single faster enemy.
- Go Gunhee is a ready-made template for a "burst window" character: overwhelming for a short time, then physically spent. The sources give no numeric time limit.
- Sung Il-Hwan mirrors Jinwoo's kit (daggers plus stealth plus telekinesis) with a different power source, useful as a model for a rival or mentor who fights like the player.
- Ruler-granted power comes with a self-destruct cost when overused by a human body (Go Gunhee, Sung Il-Hwan).

## National Level Hunters and other international figures

### Takeaway
The National Level Hunters and their peers are mostly Ruler's vessels who share one base kit (telekinesis, a "spiritual body" transformation, glowing eyes) under a personal style: Thomas Andre is a giant armoured grappler-tank, Liu Zhigang a twin-sword duelist, Christopher Reed a fire user. Several, including Bachchan and Jonas, are barely shown in the main story and are fleshed out only in *Ragnarok*.

### Inferences
- "Ruler's vessel" works like a shared subclass: every vessel has telekinesis and a transformation, with a personal style on top (grappler-tank, twin swords, fire, bare hands, daggers). That is a useful pattern for designing elite rival hunters with one common tell and one unique kit.
- Thomas Andre is listed as a Tanker but fights as an offensive grappler: pull the target in (Capture), then hit it (Power Smash) or flatten the area (Collapse). His kit is the clearest "pull, armour up, ground-slam" set in the series.
- Support hunters are valuable and physically vulnerable: both Orloff and the healers die the moment an enemy gets through.

## Lower-rank and supporting hunters

### Takeaway
The low and middle ranks supply the story's ordinary party roles (C-rank tanks with sword and shield, B-rank healers and assassins, an A-rank gauntlet brawler) and nearly all of its human villains. Most die or retire early; several return as different people in the reset timeline and in *Ragnarok*.

### Inferences
- Low-rank tanks in this setting are sword-and-shield fighters with a Taunt; the higher the rank, the bigger the shield and the more exotic the extra (Park Jongsoo's energy shield, Ma's gigantification, Thomas's golden armour).
- Song Chiyul is the series' proof that trained skill partly offsets rank: a one-armed C-rank mage can parry a B-rank assassin for a few exchanges with a borrowed sword and a buff.
- The human villains (Dongsuk, Taeshik, Kim Chul, Dongsoo) share one trait, contempt for weaker hunters, expressed through a different class each time.

## Hunter classes: which characters show how each fights, and their visual signatures

### Takeaway
Sources agree on six classes (Fighter, Tanker, Assassin, Mage, Ranger, Healer); "Support" appears as a descriptor (barrier mages, buffers, the Upgrader) more than a confirmed seventh class. Each class has one or two characters who define its look, and almost every strong hunter shares a common visual tell: eyes that glow a personal colour when power is in use.

### Inferences
- A workable one-line identity per class for original designs: Fighter = one weapon plus one self-transformation or speed state; Tanker = shield plus Taunt plus one body-altering defence; Assassin = stealth opener plus crippling strike; Mage = shaped element (projectile, wall, creature, weapon form); Ranger = conjured multi-arrow volleys; Healer = heal plus buff plus a way to stay hidden; Support = barriers and force multipliers.
- The series ties power display to two cheap effects that suit a low-art game: a per-character eye-glow colour and an aura. Neither needs detailed models.
- Classes blur at the top: the strongest Tanker fights as a grappler, the strongest "Mage" (Jinwoo) as an assassin, and a C-rank Mage as a swordsman. Class is a label assigned at awakening, not a hard limit on style.

### Gaps
- Whether "Support" is a formal class in the novel's own system was not confirmed; the sources reached list six classes.
- The eye-glow colours come from one secondary site's descriptions and were not checked against the art. Second pass: eight of the fourteen now match the wiki's own text (see the `[Fandom-API]` line under eye glow). Still open: the other six, and any check against the art itself. Third pass: of those six, Cha Hae-In (yellow, from a captioned manhwa image) and Liu Zhigang (yellow eyes) now have a second source on a `[Web-2]` line, as does Hwang Dongsoo's red. Choi Jong-In, Baek Yoonho, Christopher Reed and Woo Jinchul are still open, and nothing has been checked against the art directly.
- I found no accessible source describing manhwa visual effects by class (for example colour of mana trails) in detail.
- The *Arise* game gives many of these characters full playable kits (Prydwen has pages for Thomas Andre, Choi Jong-In, Baek Yoonho and Go Gunhee, seen in search results but not opened); these are game inventions and were not researched.

## Notable human-versus-human fights and what they show

### Takeaway
Hunter-against-hunter fights in Solo Leveling are short, decided by rank gap and by who gets the first clean hit, and nearly always happen where there are no witnesses (inside gates) or under a sparring pretext. Attackers go for the healer first, tanks and mages open with control, and the stronger side often does not draw a weapon at all.

### Inferences
- **Rank gap decides.** No fight in the sources is won by the lower-ranked side; the interest is in how long skill or a trick delays the result (Song Chiyul's parries, Lim's opening volley, Thomas's armour blunting daggers).
- **Healer first.** Taeshik's first strike is at Joohee, and in the factory fight a healer is put down before Hwang himself; the Ant King follows the same logic against the Japanese and Korean teams. Healer protection is the party's first job.
- **Gates are lawless.** Both early fights happen because nobody outside can see in, and both end with a cover story. Human threat inside a dungeon is a setting rule, not an exception.
- **Stealth is countered by recovery and intimidation, not detection.** Jinwoo does not see through Taeshik's Stealth; he absorbs the opening hit, heals it and freezes the attacker.
- **Sparring etiquette exists and breaks easily.** The S-rank bouts are bare-handed or held back until someone is hurt (Kumamoto's berserk kick) or proud (Goto), at which point a third party must intervene.
- **Mercy is a characterisation tool.** Jinwoo kills those who tried to kill him or his people (Dongsuk, Taeshik, Dongsoo) and spares those who merely stood in the way (Thomas, the Scavenger Guild).

### Inferences
- The project already avoids the series' names; the variant list above is mainly useful for searching and for making sure an original name does not collide with either the Korean or the Japanese cast.
- Because the Japanese localisation renames Go Gunhee to Gotō Kiyoomi, Japanese-language sources have two unrelated "Goto" characters (the chairman and Goto Ryuji); the alternate "Ryuu" for the latter may mean the Japanese edition avoids the clash by renaming him (speculative; not stated by any source).
- Anything about the post-Jeju cast should be treated as manhwa/novel material: the two aired seasons stop at the Jeju raid, so the international hunters' kits have little or no on-screen reference in them.
