# 3. Gates and dungeons

**Covers:** what the source material says about gates and the dungeons inside them: ranking, lifecycle and timings, dungeon breaks, the special gate types, interiors, raid layout, entry rules, how raids and gate sales are organised, harvesting, difficulty by rank, and time, communication and escape inside. Names follow the Korean originals in their common English spelling; the lead is Sung Jinwoo (also written Sung Jin-Woo).

**Date:** 2026-11-16.

**Source key:** `[O]` official or licensed text (the official anime site, publisher pages, staff interviews). `[P]` press, explainer, recap or review site; Wikipedia articles are filed here too. `[W]` the Solo Leveling Fandom wiki. `[S]` known only from a search-result snippet. `[F]` fan site, forum or fan opinion. `[low]` is added where the research notes mark a fact low confidence. A fact with `[W]` and another marker has independent support; one with `[W]` alone does not, and a chapter or episode reference carrying only `[W]` is the wiki's own and unchecked. Several of the press outlets may themselves draw on the wiki, so `[P]` beside `[W]` is not always fully independent, and several mix novel, manhwa and anime without saying which. The wiki material was obtained through the wiki's API after the site refused automated reads, and the project owner chose on 2026-11-16 to keep it, labelled. Version tags in the text: novel, manhwa (webtoon chapter numbers), anime (episodes 1 to 25 across two seasons), Ragnarok (the sequel).

## At a glance

Each rule is a summary; the section named holds the detail and the links.

1. **A gate is a blue portal with a dungeon inside, ranked E to S.** The rank follows the mana the gate gives off, read from outside with a meter. Size grows with rank, from a large door to something like a hurricane. Most gates are E to C. `[O][P][W]` (Section 1)
2. **The rank is an estimate and errs low.** Readings are "occasionally" wrong and every recorded error is an underestimate: a D-rank gate hid an S-rank dungeon, a C-rank gate was an A-rank red gate, an A-rank gate held a boss of S-rank strength. `[O][P][W]` (Section 1.3)
3. **Seven days to clear.** A gate not cleared within seven days of opening breaks: every monster still alive comes out, only hunters can kill them, and the gate stays open until its boss is dead. Monsters left outside breed and change. `[P][W]` (Sections 2 and 3)
4. **Killing the boss clears the dungeon.** The gate closes for good about one hour later, and everyone leaves by the portal they came in through. `[P][W]` (Section 2)
5. **An ordinary gate can be left and re-entered** until the boss dies. Each special type takes that away: a commanding boss can seal the exit, a red gate locks until the boss or the party is dead, a System dungeon locks until cleared. Nothing electronic works inside and nothing is recorded. `[O][P][W]` (Section 11)
6. **A dungeon is a horde led by one boss in its own room.** Low-rank bosses wait or sleep. From A-rank up the bosses shown command troops, speak and bargain. Killing the boss does not remove the other monsters, and only the System's instant dungeons respawn. `[P][W]` (Section 6)
7. **Red gate:** looks blue, seals when entered, cannot be measured, is never below B-rank, swaps the interior for a lethal climate, and runs one day inside per hour outside. It needs an A-rank hunter to survive. Two appear in the story. `[P][W]` (Section 4.1)
8. **Double dungeon:** a hidden high-rank dungeon found inside a cleared low-rank one. It has no monsters, only statues and three commandments with lethal penalties, and one person must stay behind. `[O][P][W]` (Section 4.2)
9. **System dungeons** exist for one player only: entered with a key from a loot box, soloed, locked until cleared unless an escape stone is broken, with rule cards such as no shop and no potions. A missed daily quest sends the player to a four-hour survival zone. `[P][W]` (Section 4.5)
10. **Entry is licensed and counted.** A C-rank dungeon needs a party of eight in the anime (ten on other evidence). The number is met with carriers, hired hunters who wait outside, or prisoners. The right to raid a gate is bought and held as a dated reservation. `[P][W]` (Sections 7 and 8)
11. **A guild raid kills the boss last.** The strike squad clears the monsters, mining and collecting crews strip the dungeon for hours with the boss alive in its room, then the boss is killed and all leave within the hour. `[P][W]` (Section 6.2)
12. **Two loot streams.** Essence stones come out of monster bodies, coloured by rank (white at E and D, red at A, purple at S) and worth more the higher the rank. Mana crystals are mined from the dungeon itself and are worth less. Some monsters drop rune stones that teach a skill. `[P][W]` (Section 9)
13. **Ranks are steep.** About ten C-rank hunters equal one B-rank. The boss of a top A-rank dungeon matches an S-rank hunter. An S-rank gate takes a country's S-rank hunters, can still kill half of them, and may hide a stronger boss. `[P][W]` (Section 10)
14. **Coverage differs by version.** The anime stops after the Jeju raid, so the Tokyo gate and the late giant gates are novel and manhwa only. No version disagrees on a rule; they differ in party sizes and staging. `[P][W]` (Section 12)

## 1. What a gate is and how it is ranked

### 1.1 What a gate is

- A gate is a passage linking another dimension to the ordinary world. The dungeon is the space inside it, where monsters live. Gates appeared suddenly, worldwide, a little over a decade before the story, and hunters make a living by clearing dungeons for rewards. `[O]` [Official site][o-site]
- The Korean term is "dimensional gate" (차원의 문), shortened to "gate" (manhwa ch. 1). `[W]`
- An ordinary gate is blue, described as a flaming blue portal (first seen manhwa ch. 2 / anime ep. 1). `[P][W]` [CBR, gates][cbr-gates]
- Gates turn up at random: crowded places, quiet alleys, highways, abandoned areas, schools. Some open in the sky. `[P][W]` [CBR, gates][cbr-gates], [Game Rant, waiting for breaks][gr-wait]
- Size grows loosely with rank. A weak gate is about the size of a large door (the wiki says D-rank); an S-rank gate is so large it looks like a hurricane. `[P][W]` [CBR, gates][cbr-gates]
- A C-rank gate is described as "glowing", with the hired party waiting outside it (manhwa ch. 35 / anime ep. 10). `[W]`
- Monsters carry mana, so conventional and technological weapons do not work on them; only hunters can kill them. `[P][W]` [ANN, episodes 1 to 3][ann-1-3]
- Why gates exist (novel ending, manhwa): the Rulers opened them on purpose to bring mana into the human world so that people would awaken and be able to fight the Monarchs. Essence stones were implanted in captured magic beasts for the same end. After Jinwoo resets time, people never meet gates at all; one timeline article says gates reappear about twenty years later. `[P]` [Popverse, ending][popverse], [CBR, essence stones][cbr-essence], [CBR, timeline][cbr-timeline]
- Monarchs can create gates themselves to bring soldiers to Earth. `[W]` The anime shows beings leaving Jeju through a gate. `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 2][wp-s2]

### 1.2 Ranks and how they are measured

- Gates are ranked E, D, C, B, A, S by the difficulty of the monsters inside. `[P]` [Beebom, red gates][bb-red]
- The rank follows the gate's mana output: the quantity and quality of magical energy it gives off, read by magic sensors from outside. `[P][W]` [CBR, every dungeon][cbr-every]
- The instrument is a Mana Meter built from magic crystals, and the same tool reads gates and hunters. A smaller crystal has a weaker measuring capacity; Jinwoo was measured with a small one in hospital after the double dungeon. In the anime a hunter is measured by touching a black crystal ball. `[P]` [CBR, hunter ranks][cbr-ranks], [Beebom, hunters and guilds][bb-guilds]
- S-rank output is too high for the device to capture. Jinwoo's was too great for it, and in the same episode he is classified as the country's tenth S-rank hunter (anime ep. 16). `[O][W]` [Official synopsis, ep. 16][o-16]
- Jinwoo's magic strength at the start is 10, where most E-rank hunters are around 70. No chapter is cited, and no source gives thresholds between ranks. `[P][W]` [CBR, hunter ranks][cbr-ranks]
- Who measures: national associations rank dungeons and put raid teams together. The Korean association certifies hunters, sets their rank, and locates and secures gates. `[P]` [CBR, raids][cbr-raids], [Fiction Horizon, Association][fh-kha]
- Most gates run from E to C rank; B and above need experienced parties. `[P]` [Game Rant, Jeju][gr-jeju]
- E-rank dungeons are the lowest risk and suit novices. An S-rank dungeon triggers a state of emergency, needs the strongest hunters and may mean evacuating citizens. `[P]` [Animehunch][animehunch]
- "False rankers" are hunters who suppress their mana output to test lower than they are. `[W]`

### 1.3 How reliable the rank is

- The reading can "occasionally" be wrong; no source gives a rate (cited to anime ep. 13). `[P]` [CBR, harsh realities][cbr-harsh]
- Every recorded error is an underestimate. No case was found of a gate reading too high. `[P][W]`
- A red gate passes for a blue one and may read B or A from outside. Once it shows itself it stops radiating mana, so officials can no longer measure it; a gate ranked B could really be S. `[P]` [Beebom, red gates][bb-red], [Game Rant, red gates][gr-red]

## 3. Dungeon breaks

### 3.1 Rules

- A break happens only when the seven-day period runs out. Every monster still alive is then pushed into the real world, and bosses are the monsters most likely to get out. `[P][S]` [Game Rant, dungeon break][gr-break]
- Only hunters can kill what comes out. In the anime's Jeju prologue the warships do not fire. `[P][W]` [Game Rant, dungeon break][gr-break]
- For high-rank dungeons, escaped monsters "can destroy cities or even countries". `[W]`
- Civilians take the heaviest casualties in a break (cited to anime ep. 20). `[P]` [CBR, harsh realities][cbr-harsh]
- A break does not close the gate. In Tokyo the boss still had to be found inside and killed. Whether the boss itself comes out varies: Kamish and the stone golem did; the chained Tokyo boss did not. `[P][W]` [CBR, Legia][cbr-legia]
- Monsters left outside keep changing. The Jeju ants grew wings within about four years. `[P]` [Game Rant, dungeon break][gr-break]
- Hunters sometimes let a gate break on purpose and fight on home ground. Reasons given: very few S-rank hunters can face S-rank gate monsters and they are too valuable to risk inside; some gates sit in the sky; preparing a raid can take longer than the gate has left. Part of this is the article author's own reasoning. `[P]` [Game Rant, waiting for breaks][gr-wait]
- Ragnarok adds "field-type dungeons": a broken gate left unchecked long enough infects its surroundings with mana until humans cannot live there, and monsters roam and hold territory. One example, under Seoul Station, has blue mist rising from the entrance, no electricity, dark halls, and walls and floors overgrown with moss and vines. `[W]`

### 3.3 Jeju Island

- The S-rank gate opened about four years before the story. Korea's S-rank hunters of the time could not clear it, and that failed first attempt is what caused the break, seven days after opening (manhwa ch. 56). `[P][W]` [Game Rant, Jeju][gr-jeju]
- Giant ants spread, thousands of civilians died and the island was abandoned. The ants held it for four years. `[P][W]` [Game Rant, Jeju][gr-jeju]
- Three raids to retake it failed. The third, about a year before the story, cost a leading S-rank hunter his life, and the government stopped trying. `[P][W]` [Game Rant, Jeju][gr-jeju]
- The Association left the island alone, believing the ants could neither fly nor swim and would starve. Short of food, they evolved wings and reached other islands and the mainland. `[P][W]` [TechWiser, Jeju][tw-jeju]
- A flying ant reaches South Korea and wipes out a whole town before local hunters stop it (anime ep. 19). `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 2][wp-s2]
- An individual ant lives only about a year, which is why the plan was to kill the queen and let the colony die out. `[P]` [Game Rant, Jeju raid][gr-jeju-dark]
- The fourth raid: 16 S-rank hunters, ten from Japan's top guild and six from Korea. The Japanese side was to draw the worker ants out while the Koreans went for the queen before most ants learned to fly. The queen was killed, but she had already produced the Ant King, which killed eight of the sixteen: seven Japanese and one Korean (manhwa ch. 99 to 100, anime ep. 23). `[P][W]` [Game Rant, Jeju][gr-jeju]
- After the Ant King died about 4,000 ants were still alive. The other hunters helped kill them and a clean-up crew worked the island afterwards (anime ep. 25). The wiki says the remaining ants were "in disarray" and took two more days to kill (manhwa ch. 100 to 107). `[P][W]` [Anime Corner, finale][ac-finale]
- Ant types: red foot soldiers, white soldier ants several times stronger, and the queen who produces them all. `[P]` [Game Rant, Jeju][gr-jeju]

### 3.4 Tokyo

- An S-rank gate is detected in Shinjuku. Japan, short of S-rank hunters after Jeju, waits outside the gate, asks Korea for help and hires a Russian S-rank barrier specialist for a very large fee to seal it (manhwa vol. 9 to 10). `[P][W]` [Outer Haven, vol. 9][oh-9], [Game Rant, waiting for breaks][gr-wait]
- The fee was USD 10 million a day. `[W]`
- A giant breaks the barrier with one strike and eats the specialist; giants pour out. The wiki dates the break to three days after the barrier mage was hired, not from when the gate opened (manhwa ch. 123 to 131). `[P][W]` [Outer Haven, vol. 9][oh-9]
- New S-rank gates are reported in Maryland, England and India at the same time, which the wiki gives as the reason the USA sent nobody. `[P][W]` [Outer Haven, vol. 10][oh-10]
- About 30 giants came out and killed millions. 16 S-rank hunters were sent and failed. `[W]`
- With the giants and their apparent leader dead, the gate still did not close, which told Jinwoo the real boss was inside. He found it chained in the depths, bound by restraints made by the Rulers, killed it, and the gate closed. The chained being is one of the Monarchs (manhwa ch. 131 to 137). `[P][W]` [CBR, Legia][cbr-legia], [Game Rant, Japan crisis][gr-japan]
- Jinwoo killed 28 of the 29 remaining giants; one had swum to China and was killed there. `[W]`
- The gate is called a "super-large S-rank gate" in the Japanese release, a "giant gate" in Korean and "humongous" by CBR. `[P]` [Japanese Wikipedia][wp-ja]

### 4.2 Double dungeons (Cartenon Temple)

General rule: a double dungeon is a rare, hidden, high-rank dungeon inside a low-rank one. `[O]` [Official season 1 site][o-s1]

First appearance (novel ch. 1, manhwa ch. 3, anime ep. 1 to 3):

- The second entrance was found after the D-rank dungeon's monsters were cleared. `[W]` Wikipedia's synopsis puts it after the first boss died, when the gate did not collapse. `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 1][wp-s1]
- Protocol is to report such a find to the Association for assessment. The party voted to go in, without telling anyone, so as not to lose the rewards; the vote split evenly and Jinwoo cast the deciding vote. `[P][W]` [Game Rant, truth of the double dungeon][gr-truth], [Wikipedia, season 1][wp-s1]
- The approach was a 40-minute walk down a tunnel, lit by a fire mage's spell, to a door. Inside, a giant hall lit itself with blue flames and the door slammed shut (anime ep. 1). `[W]`
- The hall holds no ordinary monsters. It has giant statues, some with weapons and some with musical instruments; a seated giant on a throne (the Statue of God) that fires lethal beams from its eyes and then gets up and walks; and a hooded statue holding a stone tablet in runic script. `[P][W]` [Game Rant, hidden boss][gr-hidden]
- The tablet's commandments: worship (or revere) God; praise God; prove your faith to God. The wiki adds a fourth line: those who disobey will not be spared. `[P][W]` [Wikipedia, season 1][wp-s1]
- 17 hunters went in and 6 came out; the anime has 13 go in and 6 come out. `[W]` Six survivors is also in Wikipedia. `[P]` [Wikipedia][wp-main]
- Rescuers found the survivor alone with no trace of the statues. The Association filed it as a special case, relieved it had not become a dungeon break (anime ep. 3). `[W]`

## 6. How a dungeon is laid out for a raid

### 6.1 Monsters and boss

- A dungeon generally holds a horde of monsters led by one boss. The boss is the strongest monster, often with unique abilities, and killing it is what clears the dungeon. `[P][W]` [Animehunch][animehunch]
- In a normal dungeon the goal is the boss, and a party can leave and try again later. `[P]` [Game Rant, every quest][gr-quest]
- The boss sits in its own room at the far end, sometimes behind its own gate or doors. `[P][W]` [ONE Esports, ep. 17][one-17]
- Monsters can forge gear, speak, and use a runic script and their own language. `[W]` High orcs are intelligent, organised and able to talk. `[P]` [TechWiser, Kargalgan][tw-karg]
- Killing the boss does not remove the other monsters: after the Ant King died the remaining ants still had to be cleared (anime ep. 25, a post-break case). `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 2][wp-s2]
- Respawning is stated only for the System's instant dungeon. `[P]` [CBR, every dungeon][cbr-every]

### 6.2 The order of a guild raid

1. The strike squad goes in and kills every monster except the boss. Top guilds run these as multi-day operations. `[W]`
2. The mining team and the collecting team go in and strip the dungeon over several hours, with the boss still alive in its room. `[W]` The anime shows the mining team at work while the boss is alive behind its own gate, with an S-rank hunter posted at the boss room (anime ep. 16 to 17). `[P]` [ONE Esports, ep. 17][one-17]
3. When the dungeon has been picked clean, the hunters kill the boss, which seals the gate, and everyone leaves within the hour. `[W]`

- A miner is ordered away from the boss's gate so the boss is not stirred up; the S-rank hunter warns the whole mining team could be wiped out (anime ep. 17). `[P][W]` [Anime Corner, season 2 ep. 4 to 5][ac-s2-45]
- Two accounts of when the harvest happens: weaker members collect crystals and stones early in the run so the party can leave before the gate closes; or hunters are encouraged to mine "after a clearing". The second fits the one-hour window only if "clearing" means the monsters and not the boss. `[P]` [CBR, gates][cbr-gates], [CBR, raids][cbr-raids]
- A collecting team for corpses is not mentioned by any source other than the wiki. `[W]`

### 6.3 Squads, roles and pace

- Six hunter classes: fighter, mage, assassin, tanker, ranger, healer. A balanced mix can decide a raid. A-rank hunters lead raids and are required for the harder dungeons. `[P]` [CBR, raids][cbr-raids]
- A strike team's roles are fighters, rangers, tanks, healers and mages, plus an optional porter who carries the squad's gear (manhwa ch. 68). `[O][W]` [Official synopsis, ep. 17][o-17]
- Large guilds are organised into a strike squad, a mining squad, a management team and a recruitment team. `[P]` [Game Rant, guilds][gr-guilds]
- The Hunters Guild keeps two strike teams ready: the first all A-rank under its two S-rank hunters, taking the highest gates; the second A and B-rank. `[P][W]` [Game Rant, guilds][gr-guilds]
- Pace: an average strike team takes about two hours on a C-rank gate. Jinwoo and one helper took one hour and planned three dungeons a day (anime ep. 10). `[W]`
- The Hunters Guild raid in the anime (ep. 16 to 17): the guild's mining team first collects crystals in a C-rank raid, then moves to the A-rank one. Jinwoo is on it as an equipment carrier. The leader of the second strike squad asks the mining team for a substitute carrier and takes him. The squad survives a first wave of high orcs, is surrounded by a second, votes to retreat and call reinforcements, and finds the gate shut by a barrier. `[O][P]` [ONE Esports, ep. 17][one-17]

## 7. Who may enter, and in what numbers

### 7.1 Who may enter

- Only licensed hunters may raid. The national association licenses a hunter after evaluation. `[P][W]` [Fiction Horizon, Association][fh-kha]
- Licensed hunters must answer Association summons; three consecutive misses cancel the licence. The licence brings healthcare, financial aid and death insurance. `[W]`
- Freelance (independent) hunters may raid if they follow Association rules, and the Association invites both guilds and freelancers to take dungeons. `[P][W]` [Fiction Horizon, Association][fh-kha]
- A minor may join a raid if awakened (manhwa ch. 35). `[W]`
- Rank limit: one source says a hunter may challenge only gates of their own rank or lower; another says lower-rank hunters may enter a higher-rank gate when stronger hunters go with them. The story's own raids, with an E-rank inside C-rank and A-rank dungeons as a carrier, fit the second. Neither cites a chapter (section 13). `[P]` [CBR, harsh realities][cbr-harsh], [Game Rant, guilds][gr-guilds]
- Hunters of D-rank and below are usually taken along to carry equipment or to fill empty slots when a dungeon has a party requirement. `[P]` [CBR, harsh realities][cbr-harsh]
- Jinwoo asks for his rank to be re-examined so that he can join high-level raids (anime ep. 16). `[O]` [Official synopsis, ep. 16][o-16]
- The Association chairman can assign a gate directly to one S-rank hunter; staff at the gate check the authorisation (manhwa ch. 110). `[W]`
- The Association can hold back an entry permit: it was reluctant to clear a regional guild for a high A-rank gate in Busan, because without an S-rank hunter the team's survival was not assured (manhwa ch. 116). `[P][W]` [Game Rant, hardest][gr-hardest]

## 8. How raids are organised and gates sold

### 8.1 The Association

- The Korean Hunters Association oversees all the hunters, gates and guilds active in Korea. `[P]` [Wikipedia][wp-main]
- It is a non-profit government body. It certifies, ranks and licenses hunters, locates and secures gates, deals with dungeon breaks, invites guilds and freelancers to take dungeons, usually has someone at the gate, runs a surveillance team, and keeps any one guild from growing too powerful. It also collects and oversees objects taken from dungeons. `[P]` [Fiction Horizon, Association][fh-kha]
- When a gate opens the Association cordons the area (a B-rank gate at a school, novel ch. 154). `[W]`
- The Association assembles some parties itself: it put probationary prisoners under its own supervisor into a C-rank party (manhwa ch. 28 to 30), and in the Hapjeong break it phoned round for healers. `[P][W]` [Wikipedia, season 1][wp-s1]
- Guilds must mobilise when the Association orders it and get tax breaks in return. `[W]`
- After the A-rank incident the Association swore the party to secrecy (anime ep. 18). `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 2][wp-s2]
- An S-rank operation is planned jointly by two national associations and uses their combined S-rank hunters (anime ep. 20 to 22). `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 2][wp-s2]
- For the first S-rank gate the US government invited S-rank hunters from around the world with lucrative pay. `[P]` [Beebom, first S-rank gate][bb-first-s]

### 8.2 Rights, permits and prices

- The right to raid a gate can be bought. A wealthy newcomer buys the rights to several C-rank dungeons at once (anime ep. 10). `[P][W]` [Wikipedia, season 1][wp-s1]
- The Association is said to sell the rights of gates. `[S]` [Sportskeeda, season 2 episode 6][sk-s2e6] No page that was opened describes a sale, auction or bid; bidding rules, prices and reservations are known from the wiki alone.
- A guild that "bought the permit to the dungeon" may send its strike team, and the permit is what makes its presence legal (novel ch. 154). `[W]`
- Gates are held as dated "reservations" that can be pushed back. A hired hunter turns visitors away from a reserved gate, saying the area is restricted (manhwa ch. 35 to 36). `[W]`
- A manager is told one construction company has monopolised the C-rank gates, and the two raiders talk of selling the gates on at an inflated price. `[S]` [ComingSoon, ep. 10][cs-10]
- In Ragnarok gates are still won by bid, a city official at the gate can refuse or grant a permit, and an app predicts where dungeons will appear (Ragnarok novel ch. 86). `[W]`

### 8.3 Guilds

- Guilds hold territories. Three of the big five work the Seoul metropolitan area, one the Honam region and one (the Knights Guild) the Yeongnam region; the same guild was responsible for all gates in Busan (manhwa ch. 116). `[P][W]` [Game Rant, guilds][gr-guilds]
- A top guild buys reservations for gates in its territory and trains recruits in low-rank gates under a B or A-rank guard. One Seoul guild recruits low-rank hunters and runs them through repeated low-rank gates. `[P][W]` [Game Rant, guilds][gr-guilds]
- Founding a guild takes a master, a vice master (who need not be a hunter) and members, and paperwork giving name, members and base. The master must have at least twenty raids' experience and pass a written test; S-rank hunters are exempt and only register. Once recognised, the guild may start taking gates. The smallest guild in the story has three people. One character still needed nineteen raids and asked for twenty (manhwa ch. 25; press cites anime ep. 7). `[P][W]` [Game Rant, guilds][gr-guilds]

### 8.4 What goes unpoliced

- Nothing inside a dungeon is recorded, so killings there are nearly unprovable, and deaths inside are never investigated, which lets a dungeon mission cover a murder (manhwa ch. 21, anime ep. 9). `[P][W]` [CBR, harsh realities][cbr-harsh]
- "Lizards" recruit weak hunters and abandon them. `[W]`
- One Association supervisor "took advantage of lawlessness within gates". `[P]` [Wikipedia][wp-main]
- Decisions inside an ad hoc party go to a vote: the leader, and whether to enter the hidden dungeon. `[P][W]` [Wikipedia, season 1][wp-s1]

### 9.2 Uses and value

- Essence stones are used to forge weapons and gear with special properties, and as a long-lasting clean energy source. Kamish's stone powered the headquarters of the US hunter bureau for nearly a decade. `[P][W]` [CBR, essence stones][cbr-essence]
- The government wants the stones and is researching them as an energy source, according to the Association chairman (anime ep. 1). `[P][W]` [CBR, anime vs manhwa][cbr-changes]
- A whole economy rests on gates and dungeons. `[P]` [Animehunch][animehunch]
- For the lowest ranks the cheap stones may not cover the cost of equipment. `[P]` [CBR, hunter ranks][cbr-ranks]
- 49 C-rank essence stones netted about USD 135,000 after a 40% tax for an independent hunter; at the guild rate of 10% that would have left about USD 200,000. `[W]`
- The Hunters Auction is an online and physical market for dungeon materials and magic gear; a black market also exists (manhwa ch. 65). `[W]`
- "Kamish's Wrath", a weapon named after the dragon, is described as the strongest magical weapon in the world. `[P]` [Wikipedia][wp-main]
- Items from the System's store could be sold in the real world but would draw attention. `[P]` [Game Rant, System abilities][gr-system]

### 9.3 How it is done

- Mining is menial work given to low ranks. D-rank hunters are usually the ones mining mana crystals, and guilds take them on for that. `[P][W]` [CBR, raids][cbr-raids]
- Crystals are mined with mana-infused pickaxes and moved in carts, because electricity does not work. Stripping a dungeon takes several hours. `[W]` The nearest independent facts are that electronics fail in dungeons and that "excavation tools" are needed. `[P]` [Film Fugitives, ep. 5][ff-5]
- Small raids skip the crews: the second hunter gathers nearby crystals while the first fights (anime ep. 10), and later summoned soldiers harvest the essence stones (anime ep. 15). `[W]`
- After Jeju a guild was sent to collect every stone on the island and confirm no ants were left. `[P][W]` [Wikipedia, season 2][wp-s2]
- In the anime, Jinwoo's help ends a mining shift early and he is offered the next day's shift at double pay (ep. 16 to 17). `[P]` [ONE Esports, ep. 17][one-17]

### 9.4 Who gets what

- Kills yield essence stones, which the fighters collect. The boss chamber's crystals are a separate, larger prize, and because they are not battle drops a non-fighting member can claim a full share of them (anime ep. 5). `[P]` [Film Fugitives, ep. 5][ff-5]
- In the manhwa a freelance raid contract said only essence stones would be divided and was silent on other goods, which is how the leaders meant to keep the crystal chamber (manhwa ch. 20). `[W]` The anime recap mentions no written contract. `[P]` [Film Fugitives, ep. 5][ff-5]

### 9.5 Terms

Outlets do not agree on words. CBR keeps "essence stones" (monster drops) and "mana crystals" (mined mineral) apart, as the wiki mostly does; Film Fugitives writes "mana stones"; CBR's rank article writes "magic crystals" for both; a search summary has Game Rant calling essence stones "otherwise known as mana crystals". This chapter uses essence stones and mana crystals. `[P][W][S]`

## 10. Difficulty by rank

### 10.1 What the sources say about scaling

- Monster strength usually follows the dungeon's rank, and higher-rank gates carry stronger monsters and more loot. `[P][W]` [Game Rant, guilds][gr-guilds]
- As a threat estimate: E to B "somewhat safer", A "tough", S a nightmare. Monsters in S-rank gates are far stronger than those below, and many can take on a capable hunter alone. `[P]` [Game Rant, S-rank gates][gr-s-tough]
- Low-rank dungeons suit E and D-rank hunters, with weaker monsters and poorer loot; high-rank dungeons need A or S-rank hunters. `[W]`
- The gap between hunter ranks is steep: B-ranks easily overpower C-ranks, and S-ranks are exponentially stronger than A-ranks. `[W]`
- The one numeric step found: about ten C-rank hunters are needed to beat one B-rank. A C-rank hunter is likened to a military tank in destructive power and durability. `[P]` [CBR, hunter ranks][cbr-ranks]
- Ragnarok is said to generalise this: facing a hunter one rank up takes roughly ten hunters of the rank below (Ragnarok novel ch. 308). `[S]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 308][wx-308]
- An S-rank hunter can clear alone a dungeon that would need a whole team of A-ranks. S-rank has no upper cap, so the spread inside S is the widest. `[W]`
- Reward scaling is stated only in words: higher-rank raids pay more because stronger monsters drop larger and darker stones, and at the bottom a raid may not cover its own equipment costs. `[P]` [CBR, hunter ranks][cbr-ranks]

### 11.1 Leaving, by gate type

| Double dungeon | The hall door shuts by itself; the doors reopen only through the commandments. | anime ep. 1 | `[W]` |
| System dungeons | No exit until cleared, except by breaking a Teleportation Stone. A daily-quest timer still runs inside and can pull the player out to the penalty zone and back. | anime ep. 4, 12 | `[P][W]` [Cinemaholic, ep. 4][cine-4] |
| Demon Castle | Can be left and re-entered later with its key. | anime ep. 7 | `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 1][wp-s1] |

- People can also block an exit: other hunters collapsed a chamber entrance with an explosion (anime ep. 5). `[P][W]` [Film Fugitives, ep. 5][ff-5]
- Leaving mid-raid is normal. Two hunters walk out of a C-rank dungeon while a third stays to finish the boss (anime ep. 9); a party in the A-rank gate votes to retreat and fetch reinforcements (anime ep. 17). `[P][W]` [ONE Esports, ep. 17][one-17]

### 11.2 Communication

- Electricity and electronics do not work inside dungeons. No source names phones or radios specifically. `[P][W]` [CBR, raids][cbr-raids]
- There are no cameras and events inside do not become public, so crimes inside cannot be proven (manhwa ch. 21). `[P][W]` [Wikipedia][wp-main]
- Messages go by runner: a porter is told to run for the exit and bring back the other strike team (anime ep. 17). `[W]`
- A sealed red gate cuts all outside communication. In the first one there was none for the three hours it was closed; the only messages were shouted through the gate as it sealed, and in the anime Jinwoo gets one brief exchange with a guild manager. `[P][W]` [Game Rant, red gates][gr-red]
- Outside a gate after a break, electronics work. The Ant King fight on Jeju was broadcast live to the world, with the signal dropping at times (anime ep. 24), and a radio transceiver was later found on a dead hunter there. `[P][W]` [Beebom, Jeju][bb-jeju]

### 11.3 Time

- The only gate type with a stated time difference is the red gate: one hour outside to one day inside. `[P][W]` [Beebom, red gates][bb-red]
- An episode summary describes the red gate as a dimension "where time flows differently". `[S]` [animemanga.com, ep. 14][am-14]
- In the Demon Castle the sources count days spent inside (two days on one floor, a few days for the last twenty) and say nothing about the clock outside. `[P]` [Game Rant, return to the Demon Castle][gr-return]
- At the very end of the novel Jinwoo fights for 27 years in a "dimensional gap" while two years pass outside. This is a place between worlds, not a gate, but it is the only other stated time difference. `[P]` [Popverse, ending][popverse]

### 11.4 Being lost inside, and surviving

- Jinwoo's father became stuck inside a dungeon during a raid and was declared dead. He was trapped for ten years and came out through a different gate, an A-rank gate in the United States (manhwa ch. 58, anime ep. 15). `[P][W]` [Wikipedia, season 2][wp-s2], [Beebom, Sung Il-Hwan][bb-father]
- The US authorities were alarmed, because humans are not supposed to emerge from gates the way monsters do. `[W]`
- In the first red gate the group that lived did so on a fire, shelter and bear meat. `[W]` Jinwoo handed out warm clothing from his System inventory. `[P]` [Beebom, red gates][bb-red]
- Gates are lawless enough for an Association supervisor to exploit (section 8.4). `[P]` [Wikipedia][wp-main]

### 12.3 The Japanese release

### 12.4 The sequel, Ragnarok

- "Solo Leveling: Ragnarok" ran as a web novel from 10 April 2023 to July 2025 (357 chapters) and as a webtoon from 31 July 2024. In it gates begin opening again and one character can control gate openings. `[P]` [Wikipedia][wp-main]

- Gates are still won by bid, a city official at the gate grants or refuses the permit, and an app predicts where dungeons will appear (Ragnarok novel ch. 86). `[W]`
- The first red gate's location returns as the "Glacier Dungeon". `[P]` [CBR, every dungeon][cbr-every]
- Ten hunters of the rank below to face one of the rank above is given as a general rule (ch. 308). `[S]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 308][wx-308]

## 14. Not stated or still open

### 14.1 The story never says

- What causes a red gate. A press source states it is never explained, even by the end of the series. `[P]` [CBR, plot holes][cbr-holes]

This is the only gap a source attributes to the story itself. Everything below is "not found in the sources read", which may mean the story is silent or only that no source reported it.

### 14.2 Not found

Gates and ranking
- Numeric thresholds between gate ranks, and gate sizes in metres.
- How long the Association takes to grade a new gate.
- How often a reading is wrong, beyond "occasionally".
- A chapter for the "about 70" mana figure of an E-rank hunter.

Lifecycle and breaks
- The chapter or episode that states the seven-day rule or the one-hour rule.
- Whether the seven days run from opening or from discovery.
- What happens to monsters still alive inside when a gate closes, and to a hunter still inside at the one-hour mark.
- Whether monsters respawn while an ordinary gate stays open, and whether ordinary monsters die with the boss.
- Any rescue protocol for a party that fails to come out of a normal gate.

Special types
- Red gates: whether they occur outside Korea, and whether the interior can exceed what the blue disguise suggested.
- Double dungeon: independent support for the party size, the height rule for bowing, the one-flame-per-minute timer and the 417-hour key text. All rest on the wiki.
- Tokyo: independent support for the three days to the break, the barrier's daily price, the count of giants, the 16 S-rank hunters and the giant that reached China. All rest on the wiki.
- Giant gates: any size; how long the Seoul gate hung before opening; where the other eight masses formed and whether each became a gate; the Canada location outside the wiki.
- Demon Castle: where its gate physically sits; independent support for "restored to its original state".
- Whether time is altered in instant dungeons or any gate other than a red one, and how the father's ten years are explained in gate terms.

Interiors
- How the manhwa and the anime each draw ordinary interiors and gates: crystal colour and glow, torches, the red gate's colour change on screen, boss-door design. No image was viewed.
- Whether the versions differ in the colour or shape of ordinary gates.
- Ordinary (non-System) castle, desert or ruin dungeons at specific ranks.
- Traps in ordinary dungeons.

Raids, entry and sales
- The full order of a guild raid stated in one place by a source other than the wiki, and any mention of a collecting team outside it.
- The Korean term behind "boss" or "master" of a dungeon.
- Minimum headcounts for ranks other than C, and any required size for an A-rank or S-rank raid.
- How the sale of gates works: who may bid, time limits, what happens with no bidder. The Association's cut and the tax base outside the wiki.
- Whether the Tokyo squad of 16 and the manhwa's Hunters Guild squad of 17 are right; neither has independent support.

Harvesting
- Prices per essence stone by rank beyond the one C-rank example, and any price for mana crystals.
- Essence stone colours for C and B-rank.
- What monster corpses are used for.
- An independent statement that crystals are mined by hand because powered tools fail.

Difficulty
- Typical monster counts and clear times for ranks other than C.
- A numeric gap for any rank step other than C to B, and any number for gates as opposed to hunters.

Versions
- How the novel words the core rules (seven days, one hour, the red gate ratio) against the manhwa.
- Whether a third anime season has been announced, and what the announced film will cover.

### 14.3 Leads that need checking before use

- The novel's C-rank minimum of ten, with eight regular members in the insect raid's squad: known only from a search summary of an unauthorised upload, which was not opened. Check against the licensed Yen Press volume 1. `[S][low]`
- More giant gates between the Seoul gate and the final one: the researcher recalled the novel describing several, without a source. Later reading found nine masses of magic matter (one becoming the Seoul gate) and a last stand "at several gates", which supports the idea without settling where or how many. `[P]` [Outer Haven, vol. 11][oh-11]
- The anime inspector re-measuring the Hunters Guild gate, and the Association selling the rights of gates: snippet only. `[S]`
- Tokyo's USD 10 million a day and sixteen S-rank hunters also turned up in search summaries of repost sites, which were not opened and are not counted as confirmation. `[S][low]`
- That the anime never states "seven days" or "one hour" on screen: an inference from two reviews. `[P][low]`
