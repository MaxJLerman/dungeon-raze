# 04. Hunters, ranks, classes, guilds and the economy

- **Covers:** how people become hunters in *Solo Leveling*, the E to S rank ladder and the National Level title, the six classes, ordinary hunters' abilities and gear, the Hunters Association and its rules, guilds, international bodies, what the story says about money, a low-rank hunter's working life, and the names used in the Japanese edition.
- **Date:** 2026-11-16.
- **Source markers:** `[O]` official or licensed text (publisher pages, licensed translations, the official anime site, staff interviews). `[P]` press, explainer, recap or review site; Wikipedia is counted here. `[W]` the Solo Leveling Fandom wiki; a chapter reference that carries only `[W]` is the wiki's own and unchecked. `[S]` known only from a search-result snippet. `[F]` fan site, forum or fan opinion. `[low]` low confidence, or a figure derived by arithmetic. A fact with `[W]` and another marker has independent support; one with `[W]` alone does not. The wiki material was obtained through the wiki's API after the site refused automated reads, and the project owner chose on 2026-11-16 to keep it, labelled.

Reading aids: "the original story" means the web novel, its manhwa (webtoon) adaptation and the anime. The sequel *Solo Leveling: Ragnarok* and the games are kept in their own section and in marked table rows. Anime episodes are numbered straight through, so ep 13 is the first of season 2 and ep 16 is season 2 episode 4. "Not stated" means the notes hold no value. Markers placed inside a sentence or table cell apply to the text before them. How gates themselves are measured and graded is outside this chapter.

Names: no single licensed English spelling is established. The Yen Press comic prints Western name order (Jinwoo Sung, Haein Cha, Dongsoo Hwang) and the licensed English novel spells the lead Seong Jin-Woo. This chapter uses the Korean-order spellings that almost every source uses (Sung Jinwoo, Cha Hae-In, Hwang Dongsoo) and lists the variants in section 11.

## At a glance

1. **Rank is fixed at awakening.** A hunter's magic power is set when they awaken and normally never grows. Rank measures that power only, not skill. A rare re-awakening is the one normal way up; Jinwoo is the exception. `[P]` `[W]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
2. **Six ranks, E to S, read by a device.** A Mana Meter turns mana output into a number. S means "too high to measure", so it is an open-ended catch-all. `[P]` `[W]` [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/solo-leveling-s-rank-hunters-abilities-explained/)
3. **National Level is a title above S, not a seventh rank.** Five hunters received it for killing the dragon Kamish; the original story names four. The requirement is to have cleared an S rank gate. `[P]` `[W]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-national-level-hunters-explained/)
4. **Each step is roughly tenfold, and S is wider than everything below it.** About ten C ranks match one B rank; the sequel states the same rule for every step below S. The author is reported as saying the spread inside S exceeds the distance from E to A. `[P]` `[O]` [ComicBook.com](https://comicbook.com/anime/news/solo-leveling-creator-s-rank-hunters-power-scaling)
5. **Known counts are few.** Korea has nine S ranks at the start and Jinwoo becomes the tenth. Japan had eleven. No source gives the number or share of hunters at any rank. `[P]` `[W]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
6. **Six classes, independent of rank:** Fighter, Mage, Assassin, Tanker, Ranger, Healer. Tankers draw monsters off Mages and Healers, Healers sustain and buff, the rest deal damage. Support and Summoner are descriptions under Mage, not classes. The list comes from explainers, not an official page. `[P]` `[W]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-the-six-hunter-types-explained/)
7. **Ordinary hunters get stronger only through gear.** Artifacts are both dungeon loot and crafted goods, weapons are made from monster parts, and rune stones teach a class-tied skill when broken. `[W]`
8. **The Hunters Association is licensing board, gate authority and police.** It ranks and licenses hunters, secures gates, certifies guilds, staffs raids from freelancers and polices hunters through its Surveillance Team. Working rules: no raiding without a licence, no gate above your rank, no solo raids, at least eight hunters for a C rank gate, and paid gate permits. `[P]` `[W]` [Fiction Horizon](https://fictionhorizon.com/solo-leveling-the-korean-hunters-association-explained/)
9. **Guilds are private companies.** A master, a vice master, strike squads, mining and collecting teams, management and recruitment. Founding one takes twenty raids, a written test and three members. Korea's top five are Hunters, White Tiger, Fiend (Reaper), Fame and Knights. `[P]` `[W]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-guilds-explained/)
10. **Value leaves a gate in three forms:** essence stones from monster corpses, mana crystals mined by hand, and artifacts or rune stones. Rights to a gate are bought and resold; an ordinary C rank gate goes for about 70 million won in the anime. `[P]` `[W]` [GameRant, ep 10 review](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-episode-10-review/)
11. **Most economy numbers do not exist.** No stated price for a stone, crystal, artifact or rune stone, and no salary for anyone. The figures that exist are in the table in section 9.3.
12. **Low-rank life is seat-filling and risk.** Weak hunters are hired to make up headcounts (3 million won per gate in the anime), are used as bait, and die in numbers; the work carries healthcare benefits. `[P]` `[W]` [GameRant, ep 10 review](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-episode-10-review/)

## 1. Awakening and re-awakening

### 1.1 Awakening

- Mana is foreign to the human world. It flows in through the gates and settles in some people's bodies, giving them magic and toughening them until ordinary weapons do little to them. `[W]`
- Awakening is presumed random and can happen at any age. Even the weakest hunter is stronger and more durable than an ordinary person. `[P]` `[W]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- New awakenings are said to happen almost every day. Some people tolerate mana too poorly ever to awaken, and people whose bodies cannot bear mana at all can fall into Eternal Slumber, a sleeping illness with no known cure. `[W]`
- Age and career do not matter. Han Song-Yi, a school friend of Jinwoo's sister, awakens as an E rank and may join raids although she is a minor (webtoon ch. 35, anime ep 10). `[W]` The actor Lee Minsung awakens as an A rank in mid career, stages a press event at the Association and has already agreed to join Lim Tae-Gyu's guild (webtoon ch. 76 and 77; anime season 2 ep 6 per Wikipedia, ep 19 per the wiki). `[P]` `[W]` [Wikipedia, season 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_2)
- Being awakened does not make someone a hunter. Only holders of a hunter's licence may join dungeon raids, and some awakened people stay in ordinary life. `[P]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-hunters-guilds-explained/)
- The amount of magic power is set at awakening and normally never grows. Rank reflects only that amount, not skill or combat talent, and ordinary hunters cannot level up. `[P]` `[W]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- Jinwoo is the one known exception to the fixed ceiling, because of the System. `[W]`
- The first hunters predate ranking. Sung Il-Hwan is described as one of Korea's first S rank hunters, never officially recognised because the system did not exist yet. The S to E system is said to be about eight or nine years old. `[W]`
- Frequency: one explainer says one person in a thousand awakens and one awakener in five thousand is D rank or higher. It cites no chapter, and the second figure fits badly with the number of C to A rank hunters shown. `[P]` `[low]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)

### 1.2 Re-awakening (second awakening, double awakening)

- A re-awakening is a rare, poorly explained event in which a hunter awakens a new level of magic power. It is the only normal route to a higher rank. `[P]` `[W]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- It is introduced in novel ch. 8 and webtoon ch. 11. The jump can be large: E ranks who rose to C and B ranks who rose to S are cited (novel ch. 8). `[W]`
- Only a few cases are said to exist in the whole world, and each drew heavy media attention (novel ch. 39). No count and no named ordinary hunter is given. `[W]`
- The Association sends agents to any hunter suspected of one. `[P]` [Fiction Horizon](https://fictionhorizon.com/solo-leveling-the-korean-hunters-association-explained/)
- Jinwoo's hospital test: after the double dungeon, Woo Jinchul of the Association suspects a re-awakening and measures Jinwoo's mana output, which shows him still barely a hunter (webtoon ch. 11). In the anime (ep 3, "It's Like a Game") the agents say a second awakening could lift an E rank to A or even S, Jinwoo puts his hand on a Mana Meter and it reads 10. The result is negative. `[P]` `[W]` [Wikipedia, season 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_1)
- Guild scouts watch for it too. Ahn Sangmin of the White Tiger Guild suspects a re-awakening after noticing that Jinwoo walked away from three deadly incidents (webtoon ch. 35). `[W]`
- A hunter can ask for a new evaluation. Jinwoo goes to the Association himself once hiding his strength gets in his way (Retesting Rank arc: novel ch. 75 to 79, webtoon ch. 61 to 64, anime ep 16). `[W]`

### 1.3 False rankers

- "False ranker" is an in-story term. The Korean is 부정 등록자, literally "dishonest registrant": a hunter who holds down their mana output to register at a lower rank. They are said to be very rare and mostly killers who prey on other hunters. First mentioned in webtoon ch. 22 and anime ep 6, when Yoo Jinho takes Jinwoo for one. `[W]`
- The wiki's list of known false rankers has only Jinwoo ("allegedly") and, in the sequel, his son Suho ("miscategorised"). Fan sites disagree on whether the series ever shows a true one. `[W]` `[P]` [Fiction Horizon](https://fictionhorizon.com/solo-leveling-what-is-a-false-ranker-explained/)

### 1.4 The Upgrader

- Norma Selner, an "Upgrader" attached to the US bureau, can raise a hunter's power beyond its natural limit. Hers is the only method shown for lifting an ordinary hunter's ceiling. `[P]` `[W]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-strongest-american-hunters/)
- She is introduced in the Recruitment arc (webtoon ch. 108 to 110, novel ch. 127 to 135); GameRant gives her debut as chapter 109. Hwang Dongsoo is said to have taken her upgrade before moving to America. When she examines Jinwoo she finds no limit (webtoon ch. 109). `[W]`
- The US bureau keeps her existence secret. `[W]` `[low]`

## 2. The rank ladder and how it is measured

### 2.1 The scale and the device

- The international scale runs E (weakest), D, C, B, A, S (strongest). `[P]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-hunters-guilds-explained/)
- Rank is read with a Mana Meter. The device turns the mana a hunter gives off into a number, and the number places the hunter in a rank. It is built from magic crystals, and smaller crystals have weaker measuring capacity. Beebom describes it as a black crystal ball the hunter touches. `[P]` `[W]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- S rank means the output is too high for even the most advanced device to read. Lower ranks have defined scales and S is a catch-all, which is why power differs so much among S ranks. `[P]` `[W]` [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/solo-leveling-s-rank-hunters-abilities-explained/)
- No source gives the band of numbers for any rank. `[W]` The only reading stated in the original story is Jinwoo's 10. `[P]` `[W]` One explainer adds that most E ranks read about 70; nothing else read confirms it. `[P]` `[low]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- A fan site offers mana bands (under 500 for E and D, 500 to 1,000 C, 1,000 to 3,000 B, 3,000 to 5,000 A, over 5,000 S, over 10,000 National Level). The site itself labels them a fan estimate from dialogue. They are not canon. `[F]` [Suki Desu](https://skdesu.com/en/solo-leveling-hunter-ranking/)
- Rank decides nearly everything about a hunter, income and reputation included. Training does not close the gaps. `[W]`
- The Association also grades non-hunters against the scale. Jinwoo's ordinary shadow soldiers are rated about C rank, elite ones about B rank "as measured by the Hunter's Association", and knight-grade shadows about A rank. `[W]`

### 2.3 Jinwoo's re-assessment, as the worked example of the procedure

- The standard device gives no result for Jinwoo. Staff retry it, and a senior employee concludes he is S rank. He is named South Korea's tenth S rank (webtoon ch. 62, anime ep 16; the anime episodes are season 2 ep 4, "I Need to Stop Faking", and ep 7, "The 10th S-Rank Hunter"). `[P]` `[W]` [Wikipedia, season 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_2)
- A second test follows. Staff say the last hunter the device failed on was Cha Hae-In about two years earlier, tell Jinwoo a more advanced device is needed and ask him to return in three days. At the second evaluation (webtoon ch. 77, anime ep 19) he shows his summons, is confirmed S rank and is handed a new licence. `[W]` `[low]`

### 2.4 National Level Hunters

- The Korean term is 국가권력급 헌터. Other translations in circulation are "Special Power Authority" and "Highest Class Hunters". First mention: webtoon ch. 78. `[W]`
- The title was created after the surviving S ranks killed the dragon Kamish. Each of the five survivors was given authority equal to a nation's (webtoon ch. 115). They are S ranks whose power matches a national military, and they are described as living like royalty and answering to nobody. `[P]` `[W]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-anime-national-level-hunters-explained/)
- Kamish came out of the first S rank gate, on the US West Coast, about eight years before the story (the wiki says before the conference arc), and wrecked the coast. The US government drew in top S ranks from around the world with large pay offers. `[P]` `[W]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-first-s-rank-gate-explained) The number who fought and died is disputed; see section 13.
- The minimum requirement, in Goto Ryuji's words, is to have cleared an S rank gate, and each title-holder has cleared at least one (webtoon ch. 78). `[P]` `[W]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-national-level-hunters-explained/)
- A National Level Hunter can clear an S rank dungeon alone. A top Korean S rank's mana is only a fraction of theirs. `[P]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-how-much-stronger-is-a-national-s-rank-than-a-normal-s-rank-hunter/)
- Five hunters received the title. The original story names four, with world ranks: Thomas Andre (USA, 1st), Liu Zhigang (China, 2nd), Christopher Reed (USA, 3rd) and Siddharth Bachchan (India, 4th, who never physically appears in the webtoon). `[P]` `[W]` [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling) The fifth is named only outside the original story; see sections 12 and 13.
- Their power is borrowed. The "top four" were given power by the Rulers over the previous decade, which is why they stand so far above other S ranks (webtoon ch. 143). The wiki's Rulers page lists seven human vessels: those four plus Sung Il-Hwan, Go Gunhee and Jonas, Brazil's strongest hunter. `[P]` `[W]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-how-much-stronger-is-a-national-s-rank-than-a-normal-s-rank-hunter/)
- Jinwoo cleared two S rank gates (Jeju and Tokyo) but was never formally given the title, because the war with the Monarchs began before the world got round to it. `[P]` `[W]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-national-level-hunters-explained/)
- Jonas of Brazil was a candidate who was never recognised. Adam White is cited as saying Sung Il-Hwan could have been one had he not vanished before ranking existed. `[P]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-national-level-hunters-explained/)

### 2.5 The world ranking

- Because S rank mana cannot be measured, S ranks are ordered internationally by a point system that scores achievements, not raw power or mana. The US Federal Bureau of Hunters set it up. `[S]`
- The wiki orders its National Level list "by Hunter Points" (webtoon ch. 149). `[W]` No source says how points are earned or where the table is published.
- The ranking is a numbered ladder: Lennart Niermann, Germany's strongest hunter, is 12th in the world (webtoon ch. 153). `[P]` `[W]` [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/solo-leveling-s-rank-hunters-abilities-explained/)
- China grades hunters on its own five-star scale and rates Liu Zhigang at seven stars. `[P]` [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/solo-leveling-s-rank-hunters-abilities-explained/)

## 3. Spacing between ranks, and the counts that are known

### 3.1 Spacing

- The only quantified step in the original story's coverage is CBR's: about ten C ranks to beat one B rank. `[P]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/) The sequel states the same tenfold rule for every step below S (section 12).
- The spread inside S rank is larger than the distance from E rank to A rank. This is the author Chugong's answer to a fan on a Korean forum, reported second-hand by ComicBook.com on 23 March 2025; for that reason he would not call Goto Ryuji the top of the ordinary S ranks. `[P]` [ComicBook.com](https://comicbook.com/anime/news/solo-leveling-creator-s-rank-hunters-power-scaling)
- Examples of the spread: Goto Ryuji, Japan's strongest, is far above the Korean S ranks on the Jeju raid yet is said to be completely outclassed by Liu Zhigang (webtoon ch. 100). Go Gunhee is called the second strongest hunter in Korea at his death. `[W]`
- Rank measures mana quantity only, so two hunters of one rank can differ greatly in usefulness depending on class and skills. This is the researcher's reading, supported by the sequel's remark that tanks, damage dealers and healers are hard to compare (section 12). `[low]`

## 4. Classes

### 4.1 The class list

- Hunters split first into combat and non-combat types, then into six class types: Fighter, Mage, Assassin, Tanker, Ranger and Healer. `[P]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-the-six-hunter-types-explained/)
- Class is unrelated to rank. Any class can appear at any rank: Lee Joohee is a B rank Healer and Cha Hae-In an S rank Fighter. `[P]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-the-six-hunter-types-explained/)
- A well-rounded party matters, but rank still caps what a party can beat. `[P]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- The six-class list comes from explainers. No official page read confirms it as a formal list. Wikipedia's character list uses "tank class" and "Healer", describes Lim Tae-Gyu by "archery magic" without the word Ranger, and does not use Support, Ranger, Fighter or Assassin as class names. `[P]` [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling)
- "Support" and "Summoner" are not classes in any source read. Barrier, curse and summoning magic sit under Mage. Yuri Orloff's class is Mage, with "Best Support-Type Hunter" and "Barrier Specialist" as epithets (novel ch. 151). `[P]` `[W]` [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/solo-leveling-s-rank-hunters-abilities-explained/) The wiki's Tanker, Healer and Ranger pages and its class list were not available, so the complete list is unchecked.
- Jinwoo is formally a Mage type, since his necromancy counts as summoning magic, although he fights like an Assassin with daggers. `[P]` `[W]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-the-six-hunter-types-explained/) Examiners and onlookers are described as calling him a "summoner-type" (webtoon ch. 77 and 92); that wording may be the summariser's. `[W]` `[low]`
- Some fan lists count seven types or treat Necromancer as an unofficial extra class tied to Jinwoo; others count five combat classes and leave Healer out. `[S]` `[F]`

More detail on three classes, all from the wiki:

- **Fighter sub-types.** Speed Fighters make fast, precise attacks and are usually a raid's main damage dealers (Cha Hae-In, Goto Ryuji, Kanae Tawata). Strength Fighters hit hard, take hits well and usually act as secondary tanks (Baek Yoonho, Woo Jinchul, Kenzo Tanaka). The split is the wiki's own grouping with no chapter reference. `[W]`
- **Assassin against Mage.** An Assassin's speed gives the edge over a Mage, who needs time to cast (credited to Song Chi-Yul, anime ep 9). `[W]`
- **Limits on ordinary Mages.** Most can call only two or three low-rank items by spatial magic (credited to Yoon Kijoong), and most can keep only one or two summoned creatures before their mana runs out (credited to Cha Hae-In). No chapter is given for either. This is the baseline that makes Jinwoo's army abnormal. `[W]`
- **Taunt** is first shown in webtoon ch. 19; listed users are Hwang Dongsuk and Kim Chul. `[W]`

### 4.3 How classes combine, and how common each is

- Tankers pull monster attention off the fragile Mages and Healers, Healers keep the party alive and buff it, and the rest deal damage. `[P]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- The clearest example of a balanced squad is Hunters Guild Strike Team B (the wiki's "second squad"): Son Kihoon (A, Tanker, leader), Han Semi (A, Healer), Gina, Lee Bora and Shin Seokjin (A, Mages), Seo Jiwoo (A, Fighter), Lee Geonguk (B, Tanker), Myung-chul and Kim Eunsoo (B, Fighters). `[P]` `[W]` [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling)
- Class mix of the sixteen S ranks on the fourth Jeju raid. Korea: two Fighters (Cha Hae-In, Baek Yoonho), one Mage (Choi Jong-In), one Tanker (Ma Dong-Wook), one Archer (Lim Tae-Gyu), one Healer (Min Byung-Gyu). Japan: five Fighters (Goto Ryuji, Minoru Hoshino, Kanae Tawata, Atsushi Kumamoto, Kenzo Tanaka), one Healer (Akari Shimizu), one Assassin (Ippei Izawa), one Mage (Kei) and two with no class given. `[W]`
- Seven of the fourteen with a stated class are Fighters. The wiki's Fighter page counts an eighth, Tatsumi Fujishima. This is the only frequency evidence found. `[W]` `[low]`
- Healers look scarce at the top: one of Korea's ten S ranks is a Healer, and his retirement and death are plot events. This is the researcher's reading of the roster. `[low]` A fan article calls healers the rarest kind of hunter and a high-rank healer "a second life" for a party, with no chapter. `[S]` `[F]`
- No source states how common any class is.

## 5. How ordinary hunters' abilities, rune stones and artifacts work

### 5.1 Skills and mana

- An ordinary hunter awakens with a fixed mana pool and a small set of skills that follow class: Mages cast elemental spells or barriers, Fighters enhance strength, speed and agility, Healers heal and buff, Assassins rely on stealth. `[P]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-skills-and-abilities-explained/)
- Individual hunters have named signature skills: Lennart Niermann's "Charge", Kei's "Mana Smell", Thomas Andre's "Reinforcement", "Power Smash" and "Capture", Min Byung-Gyu's presence concealment. `[P]` [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/solo-leveling-s-rank-hunters-abilities-explained/)
- Ordinary weapons do not work on magic beasts. Hunters need their own power and suitable gear. `[P]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-hunters-guilds-explained/)
- Because growth is closed off, equipment and party composition are the levers an ordinary hunter has, which is why money and guild membership matter so much. This is the researcher's reading; the sequel says the same about items in so many words (section 12). `[low]`
- How ordinary hunters spend or recover mana is not described, nor are potions outside Jinwoo's System shop.

### 5.2 Rune stones

- A rune stone is a magic stone that drops from a killed magic beast and holds a skill, usually one the monster itself had. A hunter learns the skill by breaking the stone. `[W]`
- Hunters everywhere prize them because they are a way to gain a skill beyond what awakening gave. The wiki's price fields read "Varies", with no figure. `[W]`
- They are tied to class. The stone found in Kamish's corpse sat unused in the US bureau's basement because none of the National Level Hunters was a mage-type (webtoon ch. 153). `[W]`
- Every named rune stone is Jinwoo's: Stealth (the System's reward for beating Kang Taeshik, unusual because he was not a magic beast), Ruler's Hands from Igris and Shadow Exchange from Baran. He uses the Stealth stone on entering a C rank gate (anime ep 10). No ordinary hunter is shown buying or using one. `[W]`

### 5.3 Artifacts, weapons and the market

- Gear quality matters and costs money. Jinwoo's cheap weapons broke quickly in the double dungeon and in the Hapjeong instance dungeon (anime season 1 ep 4). `[P]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-the-six-hunter-types-explained/)
- Artifacts are both loot and manufactured goods. Dungeons hold artifacts as well as mana crystals, and craftsmen make them. `[W]`
- Artifacts raise specific stats, and the Korean Hunters Auction appraises and sells them. When Jinwoo has the Orb of Avarice appraised there, the custodian measures a 100% boost to magic power and says the best artifact craftsmen manage only 50%. The orb is item class A and doubles magic damage. No chapter and no price is given. `[W]`
- Weapons are made from monster parts. Kamish's Wrath is a dagger pair cut from the dragon's fang, which Thomas Andre was allowed to keep. It gives +1500 attack and grows with its user's mana. Not being an assassin-type, Thomas left it in the Scavenger Guild's basement for eight years before giving it to Jinwoo (webtoon ch. 152 and 153). `[P]` `[W]` [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling) Whether it is the strongest weapon or the second strongest is a conflict (section 13).
- Buying: after his S rank is confirmed Jinwoo goes to a hunter auction site for heat-resistant artifacts, is shown a cloak and a ring kept behind protective glass, and finds them and an offered weapon too dear (anime ep 19). `[W]` Staff there treat him with deference once his S rank is public. `[S]`
- Selling: before an S rank dungeon Jinwoo sells items to pay for higher-grade gear (anime season 2 ep 7). `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_2) One review describes him using his last days as an unknown to sell high-level items while hiding where they came from, and questions the cover story, since anything found on a guild's mining job would arguably belong to that guild (anime ep 16). `[P]` [Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/review/solo-leveling-season-2-arise-from-the-shadow/episode-16/.220493)
- The Hunters Guild is said to guard the Korean Hunters Auction (webtoon ch. 79). `[W]`
- A black market for illegal items exists, according to a remark of Jinwoo's that the wiki's auction page reports (webtoon ch. 65 and 79 are among its references). `[S]` `[low]`
- No recap or page read states a price for any artifact, weapon or rune stone.

### 5.4 Money against rank

- Wealth substitutes for rank only partly. D rank Yoo Jinho arrives at his first raid in expensive equipment supplied by his family (anime season 1 ep 5). `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_1) His armour is described as a running joke, showy and useless. `[W]`
- Poverty shows in gear. In the manhwa Jinwoo enters his first shown dungeon unarmed. The anime gives him a cheap dagger with little magic in it that shatters on a goblin. `[W]`
- Mining tools count as equipment. The Hunters Guild mining team's work and a porter's load are part of a raid's kit (webtoon ch. 65 to 68, anime ep 17). `[W]` The tools are said to be reinforced with mana. `[W]` `[low]`

## 6. The Korean Hunters Association, its Surveillance Team and the rules

### 6.1 What the Association is and does

- The Korean Hunters Association is South Korea's government body for hunters, gates, dungeons, guilds and raids. It is a state institution, not a profit-seeking guild, and does mostly administration and oversight, only sometimes taking part in hunting. `[P]` `[W]` [Fiction Horizon](https://fictionhorizon.com/solo-leveling-the-korean-hunters-association-explained/)
- Its headquarters are in the Guro district of Seoul. `[P]` `[W]` [Fiction Horizon](https://fictionhorizon.com/solo-leveling-the-korean-hunters-association-explained/)
- Functions: it certifies hunters and sets their rank through an Evaluation Office; certifies guilds; locates and secures gates and responds to possible dungeon breaks; invites guilds and freelance hunters to clear dungeons; keeps a staff member near a gate, especially when it is cleared; and collects and oversees objects taken from dungeons. `[P]` [Fiction Horizon](https://fictionhorizon.com/solo-leveling-the-korean-hunters-association-explained/)
- It decides who may challenge which gates and sets the rules for founding a guild. `[P]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-guilds-explained/)
- One guide adds investigating crimes and distributing rewards to its duties, and says membership is mandatory for every certified hunter who wants to take jobs. `[S]`
- Its political role is to stop any one guild becoming too powerful, because the government and military cannot check hunters on their own. `[P]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- It acts as the national command for disasters: it negotiates with its Japanese counterpart and fields Korea's S ranks for the fourth Jeju Island operation (anime season 2 ep 8 to 13). `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_2)
- It recruits for itself. After Jinwoo's S rank result Chairman Go Gunhee offers him a job, on the ground that his joining one of the five guilds would upset the balance between them, and Jinwoo declines (anime ep 16, webtoon ch. 63 and 64). `[P]` `[W]` [Wikipedia, season 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_2)
- The Association cannot match what a top guild offers in money, fame and luxury. This is one reviewer's reading of the episode, and the wiki's chapter summaries say the same. `[P]` `[W]` `[low]` [Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/review/solo-leveling-season-2-arise-from-the-shadow/episode-16/.220493)
- The Jeju battle is filmed for the public by a reporter who is himself an A rank hunter (anime ep 24). `[W]` The Association arranged the broadcast, the cameraman's A rank licence is what lets him go in, the footage runs on a ten-minute delay and viewership passed eighty percent. `[W]` `[low]`

He is described as a hunter supremacist who held that hunters need not obey human laws. ### 6.5 S ranks and the law

- "The law is powerless to deal with S-ranks" is Woo Jinchul's remark when he warns Jinwoo and Jinho that Hwang Dongsoo may come for revenge and advises them to be careful or even leave the country (webtoon ch. 35, anime ep 10). `[W]` Explainers put it as S ranks being beyond the reach of the law. `[P]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- One reviewer's reading: S ranks are each country's protectors and greatest weapons, with no practical check on them but another S rank, so states let them act above the law. `[P]` [Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/review/solo-leveling-season-2-arise-from-the-shadow/episode-16/.220493)
- The powerlessness has limits. An S rank is still formally subject to criminal law (the extradition answer above) and to the Association's entry decisions. `[W]`
- Leaving the country: no law for or against it is stated. Hwang Dongsoo accepted the Upgrader's offer and moved to America "without a second thought", and what followed was social, not legal: Koreans called him a traitor. `[W]` The US bureau offered Jinwoo citizenship (webtoon ch. 108 and 109). `[W]` `[low]`
- Crimes inside dungeons are hard to prove because the only witnesses are the party; both murder plots in anime season 1 depend on that. This is the researcher's reading. `[low]`
- A legal-commentary blog analyses the Jeju raid under real international law and treats guilds as private military contractors. Its premises are the author's own and it cites no in-story statute. It is not a source on hunter law. `[F]` [The Legal Geeks](https://thelegalgeeks.com/2026/01/02/solo-leveling-south-koreas-internationally-lawful-approach-to-squashing-ants/)

## 7. Guilds

### 7.1 What a guild is

- A guild is a private group of hunters raiding under one banner, from three members (Ahjin) to hundreds (Hunters). `[P]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-guilds-explained/)
- The usual structure is a Guild Master, a Vice Guild Master and members. The Vice Guild Master need not be a hunter. `[P]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-guilds-explained/)
- Large guilds have divisions: a Strike Squad, a Mining Squad, a Management Team and a Recruitment Team. `[P]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-guilds-explained/) Guilds also employ Collecting Teams to gather the corpses of killed magic beasts, because essence stones sit inside the bodies. `[W]`
- Guilds in each country are ranked by how many high-rank hunters they hold, so they compete to recruit the strongest. `[P]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-hunters-guilds-explained/)
- Membership has trade-offs. Strike squads of specialists cover each other and give access to higher-rank gates with more loot. But a member is an employee who goes where the Guild Master sends them, and in a big guild there is no guarantee of a strike squad place, so an unselected hunter may struggle to earn. `[P]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-guilds-explained/)
- A corporation can own a guild. Yoojin Construction lets Yoo Jinho run the company guild (the "Yoojin Guild") on condition that he recruits Jinwoo (anime season 2 ep 3). `[P]` `[W]` [Wikipedia, season 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_2)

### 7.3 Founding a guild

- A hunter files paperwork (name, members, base), needs at least twenty raids of experience and must pass a written test. S ranks are generally exempt and only register. `[P]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-guilds-explained/)
- Yoo Jinho is the worked example. He asks Jinwoo for twenty raids and says he has nineteen left to qualify for a guild-master licence (webtoon ch. 25; anime season 1 ep 7). With the raids done he still faces the written exam (webtoon ch. 56). `[P]` `[W]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-guilds-explained/)
- A guild needs at least three registered members before it counts as official, and the third need not go on raids (webtoon ch. 94 and 112; Yen Press vol. 8). `[P]` `[W]` [The Otaku Author, vol. 8](https://otakuauthor.com/solo-leveling-volume-8-one-man-guild)

### 7.4 How a large guild works a gate

- A raid by a large guild is a pipeline: the strike squad kills, the collecting team recovers corpses, the mining team extracts crystals, and administration sells. The "pipeline" wording is the researcher's. `[low]`
- One review describes dozens of hunters, ordinary monsters killed with the corpses left whole for collection, and most of the gate's timer spent gathering resources before the boss is touched (anime ep 16). `[P]` [Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/review/solo-leveling-season-2-arise-from-the-shadow/episode-16/.220493)
- Miners go in once the strike squad has cleared most monsters, and the boss is left alive on purpose so the gate stays open while crystals are extracted (webtoon ch. 65 and 67). `[W]` `[low]`
- Mana crystals are mined by hand, so guilds keep mining teams of hunters dedicated to it. `[S]` D rank hunters are often hired for such non-combat work. `[P]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- Jinwoo signs on as a temporary worker with the Hunters Guild mining team (the "Excavation Team" in the Yen Press volume) under Bae Yoon-Suk, as an E rank, and finishes the day's work two hours early (webtoon ch. 65). The crew follows the strike squad into an A rank dungeon and is trapped with the boss Kargalgan in the next one (anime season 2 ep 4 and 5). `[P]` `[W]` [The Otaku Author, vol. 5](https://otakuauthor.com/solo-leveling-volume-5-may-i-kill-all-of-these-magic-beasts/)
- Porters: squad leader Son Kihoon takes Jinwoo into an A rank gate as a porter to carry the squad's baggage, with a risk allowance on offer (webtoon ch. 68, anime ep 17). Son hopes a successful raid will make him an official captain. `[W]` None of the regular miners will take the porter job despite the extra pay, because of the danger. `[W]` `[low]`

### 7.5 Recruitment and contracts

- Recruitment is done by scouts and by the masters in person. Ahn Sangmin approaches Jinwoo for White Tiger (anime season 1 ep 10), and after the S rank result Baek Yoonho and Choi Jong-In both try to sign him (season 2 ep 4 and 7). `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_2)
- Scouts do groundwork. Ahn Sangmin starts looking into Jinwoo when White Tiger learns that Jinho has spent 150 million won on rights to C rank gates (webtoon ch. 35), and he follows the Hunters Guild incident afterwards (ch. 76). `[W]`
- A guild hunter is under a contract with a term. Leaving before it ends costs a financial penalty, and an S rank can normally expect a large signing bonus from any major guild. Cha Hae-In offers to buy out her own contract and waive the bonus to join Jinwoo's guild, and he refuses (webtoon ch. 112; Yen Press vol. 8). No amounts are given. `[P]` `[W]` [The Otaku Author, vol. 8](https://otakuauthor.com/solo-leveling-volume-8-one-man-guild)
- Recruitment figures are in the economy table (section 9). No signing-bonus amount for an S rank is stated anywhere.

### 7.6 Territory, reservations and risk

- The White Tiger Guild has a territory in the city, and when a gate opens there it buys the reservation for the raid and clean-up. The Ahjin Guild arc likewise speaks of an A rank gate on the Knights Guild's "turf". `[W]`
- The red gate raid shows what a guild risks. A C rank gate bought by White Tiger for training recruits turned out to be a red gate. Fourteen hunters went in and five came back. Kim Chul, the only A rank, died; he is described as a large investment who was about to join the guild's elite strike squad. The deaths made national news (webtoon ch. 47, anime ep 13). The anime trimmed the party by four, leaving one A rank, five B ranks and two C ranks. `[W]`
- White Tiger's model is the nearest thing in the story to a training guild for low-rank hunters. This is the researcher's reading. `[low]`

## 8. International bodies, foreign guilds and the conference

Each major country has its own hunter authority and top guild. Guilds in China, the United States and Japan are much larger than Korean guilds. `[P]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/) One source counts more than 24 guilds worldwide across the series, most never described. `[P]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/strongest-hunter-guilds-solo-leveling/)

### 8.1 United States

- The US authority is the Federal Bureau of Hunters (FBH), a government body overseeing hunters, guilds and gates. English variants are "Hunter Bureau", "American Hunter Bureau" and, in one Beebom article, "American Hunters Association". `[S]` `[W]` `[P]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-first-s-rank-gate-explained)
- Staff: Director David Brennon (the wiki's spelling, not "Brennan"), Deputy Director Michael Connor, Branch Chief Adam White (deceased) and the Upgrader Norma Selner. `[S]` `[W]`
- Its headquarters are built over Kamish's corpse, which is kept in a basement behind mana-reinforced glass. `[W]`
- It arrested Sung Il-Hwan when he came out of a US dungeon after ten years (webtoon ch. 56; anime season 2 ep 3) and, doubting he was human, had Hwang Dongsoo interrogate him. `[P]` `[W]` [Wikipedia, season 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_2)
- The United States reportedly has the most S rank hunters and two National Level Hunters, and recruits talent from abroad. Norma Selner's upgrade is a main reason strong hunters move there. `[P]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-strongest-american-hunters/)
- The Scavenger Guild is the strongest US guild, led by Thomas Andre, with Hwang Dongsoo as a member. It works across the country, sends hunters abroad and was said to hold more S ranks than any guild in the world. Chapter debuts per GameRant: Andre 83, Norma Selner 109, Christopher Reed 138. `[P]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-strongest-american-hunters/)
- Thomas Andre is America's strongest hunter and first in the world (webtoon ch. 149). He refuses to take part in the Jeju raid unless paid his fee (ch. 84). When three S ranks bet a yacht and a house on Jinwoo dying in Tokyo, he stakes his guild's headquarters on Jinwoo surviving (ch. 134). The wiki's Rulers page calls Reed "one of the two strongest hunters in America". A separate S rank gate in Maryland is mentioned. `[W]`

### 8.2 Japan

- Goto Ryuji was Japan's strongest hunter and master of the Draw Sword Guild (Screen Rant: "Blade Guild"; both translate one name). Reiji Sugimoto (Sugimoto Reiji) is his vice master and successor. Matsumoto Shigeo chairs the Japanese Hunters Association. `[P]` `[W]` [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling) Matsumoto first appears in webtoon ch. 100. `[W]` `[low]`
- Beebom calls the Draw Sword Guild the second biggest guild in Asia, with eleven S ranks before Jeju and four after, now under Sugimoto. Placing all eleven in one guild is Beebom's claim alone. `[P]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/strongest-hunter-guilds-solo-leveling/)
- The fourth Jeju Island operation is a joint Korea and Japan raid built on Japan's plan. Goto and Matsumoto intend the Korean S ranks to die there and the deaths to pass as a tragedy of the raid, with Goto taking the credit and seeking National Level recognition. The plan collapses when the Ant King kills Goto and six other Japanese hunters (anime season 2 ep 8 to 11). `[P]` `[W]` [Wikipedia, season 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_2)
- Before the operation the two countries' S ranks hold a four against four training duel, which Japan wins (anime season 2 ep 9 and 10). `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_2)
- The two countries had agreed to split the essence stones recovered from the island. After Jeju, Matsumoto tries to hire Jinwoo by offering to give up Japan's share. Go Gunhee, who knows of the plot from a receiver found on Goto's body, refuses in a rage (webtoon ch. 126). `[W]`

### 8.3 China, India and others

- China: Liu Zhigang is its strongest hunter and a National Level Hunter. China grades hunters on its own five-star scale (section 2.5). His guild is never named in the original story; "China Guild Association" is a fan label. Beebom adds, without a source, that it became China's strongest after a "Gold Dragon Guild" died out, which may be sequel material. Nothing more is known of how China organises hunters. `[P]` `[low]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/strongest-hunter-guilds-solo-leveling/)
- India: Siddharth Bachchan is a National Level Hunter. His Asura Guild is named in the sequel (section 12). `[P]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/strongest-hunter-guilds-solo-leveling/)
- Germany: Lennart Niermann, master of the Richter Guild, 12th in the world (webtoon ch. 153). `[P]` `[W]` [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/solo-leveling-s-rank-hunters-abilities-explained/)
- Russia: Yuri Orloff, the strongest Russian S rank, a barrier specialist. A US bureau officer rates his barrier magic alone as National Level. `[P]` `[W]` [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/solo-leveling-s-rank-hunters-abilities-explained/)
- Brazil: Jonas, the country's strongest hunter, abducted and killed days before the conference. `[W]`

### 8.4 How countries compete over S ranks

- S ranks are treated as national strategic assets. Countries compete with money, status, enhancement and, in Japan's case, sabotage. Korea's shortage of S ranks drives its dependence on Japan for Jeju, its fear of American poaching and the Association's eagerness to keep Jinwoo. This is the researcher's reading of the facts below. `[low]`
- Poaching precedent: Hwang Dongsoo, a Korean S rank, was drawn to America with promises of fame, money and enhancement, joined the Scavenger Guild and was branded a traitor in Korea, resented even by his brother. `[P]` `[W]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-strongest-american-hunters/)
- The US offer to Jinwoo: Deputy Director Michael Connor offers access to the Upgrader. She cannot upgrade him and he leaves without signing (webtoon ch. 108 to 110; the anime left this sequence out). `[W]` The offer included American citizenship. `[W]` `[low]` Go Gunhee then tells Jinwoo that many countries are trying to recruit him (ch. 121). `[W]`
- States pay foreign S ranks for emergencies. For the Kamish gate the US government brought in top hunters from around the world with large rewards. `[P]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-first-s-rank-gate-explained)
- Hire is priced by the day. Yuri Orloff charges Japan 10 million US dollars a day to seal the Tokyo S rank gate and calls that cheap beside what Thomas Andre would ask (webtoon ch. 124). His barrier is said to need over 100,000 tons of mana crystals, and a giant breaks it in one blow. `[W]`
- Countries lend S ranks. Sixteen were sent to the Tokyo gate from Japan, China, Canada, India, Russia and the United States (webtoon ch. 135). The named dead include Yuri Orloff, a hunter of a "Black Hole Guild" and a Canadian S rank. Jinwoo went on his own decision (ch. 133). Japan Crisis arc: novel ch. 165 to 182, webtoon ch. 132 to 139. `[W]`

### 8.5 The International Guild Conference

- It is an annual conference held in the United States. `[P]` `[S]` [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling)
- The Federal Bureau of Hunters hosts it, only the strongest hunters of each country and their guilds are invited, and representatives of the major guilds discuss current trends and the future of the hunting industry. `[S]`
- The bureau is the body that announces Korea's representative. `[W]`
- Jinwoo attends as master of the Ahjin Guild, with Yoo Jinho, as Korea's representative guild. The publisher's blurb for comic vol. 10 says the two members of the Ahjin Guild go to the United States for it and also mean to find out what happened to the world's third-ranked hunter, found dead. The blurb for novel vol. 6 says rescuing a country the rest of the world had abandoned earns Jinwoo international renown, a larger guild and an invitation to "the most prestigious hunter conference in the world". `[O]` [Yen Press, comic vol. 10](https://yenpress.com/titles/9798400902543-solo-leveling-vol-10-comic) A review of the same volume says the guild was "selected to represent Korea". `[P]` [The Otaku Author, vol. 10](https://otakuauthor.com/solo-leveling-volume-10-the-deal-is-off/)
- The rescued country is unnamed in the blurb; going by arc order it is Japan. `[low]`
- The arc is the wiki's 18th, covering novel ch. 183 to 199 and webtoon ch. 140 to 149 (comic vol. 10 covers webtoon ch. 135 to 146). Its character list includes Thomas Andre, Liu Zhigang, Lennart Niermann, Christopher Reed, Choi Jong-In, Go Gunhee, Woo Jinchul, Yoo Jinho, Hwang Dongsoo and bureau staff, so heads of several countries' top guilds and Korean officials attend. It lasts more than a day: there is a conference hall, and Go Gunhee is killed "on the last day". `[W]`
- Events around it, with the wiki's chapters: Christopher Reed is killed by Monarchs (webtoon ch. 140) and Sung Il-Hwan is suspected (ch. 141); the bureau lets Jinwoo see Kamish's corpse under its headquarters and he briefly raises it as a shadow (ch. 142 and 143); Hwang Dongsoo kidnaps Yoo Jinho (ch. 144); Jinwoo kills Hwang and beats Thomas Andre and the Scavenger Guild (ch. 147); the National Level ranking by Hunter Points is cited to ch. 149. `[W]`
- Not stated: an agenda beyond the snippet above, a full attendee list, whether rankings are discussed there, and how many guilds a country sends.

## 9. The economy

### 9.1 How value flows

- Raids exist to collect magic crystals, and monster corpses are also sold as materials. Stronger monsters yield larger crystals, so high-rank hunters earn far more. Hunting is described as a multi-billion-dollar industry with large effects on the world economy, and guilds handle the business and politics for their hunters. `[P]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- Essence stones are embedded in the bodies of magic beasts and "can be sold for large profits", which is why corpses are collected. `[W]`
- Essence stones are in high demand by the government, hunters make a living by selling them, they go into weapons and gear, and the state is researching them as clean, safe energy. In the anime (season 1 ep 1) Go Gunhee and Woo Jinchul present essence stones and mana crystals together as a new power source: safer than nuclear, more efficient than hydro, wind or geothermal, cleaner than fossil fuel. Where the novel or manhwa states the energy claim is not identified. `[W]`
- Essence stones are graded on the same S to E scale as hunters, S being worth the most. Colour shows grade: D white, A red, S purple; no colour is given for E, C or B. Hunters trade them for money or goods, and the series never explains how they work as an energy source. `[P]` [Fiction Horizon](https://fictionhorizon.com/solo-leveling-what-is-an-essence-stone-magical-core-how-worthy-is-it/)
- Mana crystals hold less mana than essence stones and are worth less, but still sell well and are a secondary income. They are mined by hand. `[S]`
- The Mana Meter is itself built from magic crystals, an example of crystals as an industrial input. `[P]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- Collecting essence stones, corpses and mana crystals from a gate takes manpower that only large established guilds have, which gives them access to top gates and the best loot. `[S]`
- Raid rights are a tradeable commodity. Yoo Jinho buys the rights to several C rank dungeons and hires low-rank hunters to meet the headcount (anime season 1 ep 10). `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_1) The headcount rule and purchasable rights together make a labour market for low-rank hunters as paid seat-fillers; that framing is the researcher's. `[low]`
- Guild members get far better tax treatment than independent hunters when selling essence stones and similar goods. The reason given is the guilds' duty to mobilise on the Association's order. `[S]`
- The reward of an S rank dungeon can include items with no market price, such as a potion said to cure any illness (anime season 1 ep 7). `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_1)
- Spoils of a national raid are shared between states: Japan held a share of the Jeju essence stones (webtoon ch. 126). `[W]`
- After the Jeju raid Jinwoo has his shadows leave most of the island's magic crystals to pay for victim compensation and rebuilding, keeping only the Ant King's black crystal (webtoon ch. 106), and a clean-up expedition later collects the rest (ch. 107). He funds his new guild with essence stones from his own raids (ch. 112). No values are given. `[W]` `[low]`

### 9.2 Raid contracts and shares

- Freelance raid contracts set shares in advance. In the anime (season 1 ep 5) Jinwoo's contract says he will not fight and gets no share of battle drops; mana crystals in the boss room are not battle drops, so Yoo Jinho argues he is owed a full share of those. `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_1)
- In the manhwa the contract with Hwang Dongsuk's party says only essence stones gained in the dungeon will be divided and is silent on other goods. Jinho points at that wording when a chamber full of mana crystals turns up, and Dongsuk promises an even split (webtoon ch. 20). Both versions make mana crystals the loophole. `[W]`
- Jinwoo signs a "self-responsibility bond" before entering. `[P]` [Fugitives, ep 5 recap](https://fugitives.com/solo-leveling-season1-episode5-recap-ending-explained-2024-anime/)
- Afterwards Jinho gave all the salvaged crystals to Jinwoo. `[W]`

### 9.4 Rank and income

- A hunter's rank settles their income, and S ranks are very rich. No figure is given for any rank. `[W]`
- E ranks "receive very low pay", though the same wiki page says it is still very high next to most white-collar jobs. The wiki's history page for Jinwoo calls his own pay "meager". `[W]`
- Low-value crystals may not cover the cost of raid equipment, as with Jinwoo at the start. CBR also says Korea is in a depression that pushes people into hunting; that is CBR's characterisation alone. `[P]` `[low]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- Jinwoo's own finances: behind on rent when he takes the Hwang Dongsuk raid (anime ep 5); able to cover rent and his mother's hospital bills after it (ep 7); a much improved bank balance by webtoon ch. 46. No sum is stated. `[W]`
- If the tax snippet is right, tax policy is the state's lever for pushing hunters into guilds. This is the researcher's reading. `[low]`

## 10. Daily life of a low-rank hunter

### 10.1 The work

- A low-rank hunter is a freelancer who joins pick-up raids for small returns and a real risk of death. `[P]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- How a pick-up raid starts: hunters gather at the gate site, Jinwoo collects his "daily allowance" from an employee there (the wiki's summary says "guild employee"), and the party picks a leader. A retired hunter has come back because a second child is on the way and raids pay quickly (webtoon ch. 1). No amount is stated. `[W]`
- The opening raid is a mixed-rank party under a senior C rank, Song Chi-Yul, with B rank Healer Lee Joohee. The party votes on whether to go deeper and Jinwoo casts the deciding vote (anime season 1 ep 1 and 2). `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_1)
- Hunters are called up by notice when numbers are short. Song Chi-Yul, one arm gone and running a kumdo school, still receives an urgent raid notification and goes, over a student's suggestion that he retire (webtoon ch. 28). `[W]`
- The headcount rule makes even the weakest hunter useful, which is how Jinwoo keeps getting places. This is the researcher's reading. `[low]`

### 10.2 Jinwoo as the bottom of the ladder

- Jinwoo is known as the weakest hunter. He became a hunter to support his family, was mocked across the Korean hunter community as "The Weakest Hunter of All Mankind" (webtoon ch. 1), and came close to dying on nearly every raid for four years. `[P]` `[W]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- He is injured repeatedly even in E rank dungeons and would probably have died several times without Lee Joohee's healing. He hunts to pay his mother's hospital bills and his sister's schooling. `[P]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
- The system is unequal by design: awakening rank is outside a person's control, and hunting is the only option for many even when their rank offers little. `[P]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)

### 10.3 Benefits and insurance

- Hunter work "came with healthcare benefits" that covered his mother's hospital bills, a main reason he stayed despite poor pay and constant injury (webtoon ch. 10). Who funds the cover is not stated. `[W]`
- A "Hunters Hospital" is a named location in the anime (ep 3, 5, 8, 9), and in the manhwa its doctors worry when he stays away. `[W]`
- One guide says each licence brings healthcare, financial aid and death insurance paid to the hunter's family, and that these perks drew Jinwoo into the work. No chapter, amount or payer is given. This is the only statement found that a death benefit exists. `[S]` `[low]`

### 10.4 Risk

- Survivors of a bad raid: Lee Joohee is traumatised and later retires (anime ep 10); Song Chi-Yul loses an arm and is expected to retire. `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_1)
- Leaders can abandon the weak. In a C rank raid led by A rank Kim Chul, the leader abandons the lower-ranked hunters and most of his party is wiped out (anime season 2 ep 1). `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_2)
- The recurring danger for a low-rank hunter is other hunters as much as monsters, because nothing inside a gate is witnessed. This is the researcher's reading. `[low]`

### 10.5 Stigma

- An anime flashback shows hunters who had rescued Jinwoo scolding him, telling him to stay in a corner while they cleared the dungeon and denying him a share of the rewards because he was too weak (anime ep 3). `[W]`
- E ranks are "viewed as disposable". A guild scout is openly scornful of a new E rank recruit in a manhwa scene the anime cut from ep 13. `[W]`

### 10.6 Exploitation

- Private pick-up groups can be traps. C rank Hwang Dongsuk recruits Jinwoo and Jinho to fill the eight-person entry requirement, shuts them in with the boss as bait, and tries to force Jinho to kill Jinwoo (anime season 1 ep 5 and 6). `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_1)
- Conditions on that raid: Jinwoo has not raided since the double dungeon, money is short and the landlord has called about overdue rent. He is told he is only there to fill the quota. Only half the team are C rank, so a party can meet the headcount with members below the gate's rank. There is no healer, which he finds odd. He signs a release form before joining. His wiki history page calls the role "porter". `[P]` `[W]` [Fugitives, ep 5 recap](https://fugitives.com/solo-leveling-season1-episode5-recap-ending-explained-2024-anime/)
- Dongsuk was shielded from consequences because Korea feared his S rank brother Hwang Dongsoo. `[P]` [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/solo-leveling-strongest-american-hunters/)
- Who takes seat-filler work: licensed hunters who cannot raid normally because of injuries, alcoholism or inexperience, or who simply want quick cash. A schoolgirl E rank is among them. They are paid to wait outside and stay silent (webtoon ch. 35; pay in section 9.3). `[W]`
- When too few hunters are available locally the Association fills a party with convict hunters, and the inspector guarding them turns out to be a hired killer (anime season 1 ep 8 and 9). `[P]` [Wikipedia, season 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling_season_1) Low-rank hunters are witnesses to be removed: Kang Taeshik asked for more money because he expected to kill the rest of the party as well. `[W]`
- False rankers are feared as hunters who join low-rank raids to kill weaker ones. `[P]` [Fiction Horizon](https://fictionhorizon.com/solo-leveling-what-is-a-false-ranker-explained/)
- Fan sites describe guilds exploiting weak hunters and low-rank hunters dying in dungeons they do not understand. This is commentary, not a cited scene. `[F]` [Suki Desu](https://skdesu.com/en/solo-leveling-hunter-ranking/)

### 12.3 The sequel, *Solo Leveling: Ragnarok*

Nothing in this list is stated for the original story. Facts marked `[O]` come from the English publisher's free chapters and teasers; a teaser is only the opening of a chapter. The sequel takes place in a revised timeline after Jinwoo turned time back.

Awakening and ranks:

- A hunter's power stops growing once the rank is set, so dungeon items are the way to get stronger. The grading system is newly established, the measuring devices still make errors, and an E rank who reaches D rank may ask to be re-evaluated (ch. 9). `[O]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 9](https://www.wuxiaworld.com/novel/solo-leveling-ragnarok/slrk-chapter-9)

- Suho is listed as a "miscategorised" false ranker. `[W]`
- The tenfold rule, in narration: about ten hunters of one rank are needed to face one of the next rank up, at every step below S, as a rough estimate. Mana capacity is the fairest basis for comparison, but tanks, damage dealers and healers are hard to compare one to one, and S rank is "beyond measurement" so the rule does not reach it (ch. 308 teaser). `[O]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 308](https://www.wuxiaworld.com/novel/solo-leveling-ragnarok/slrk-chapter-308)
- S ranks are rare worldwide and are treated like celebrities (ch. 164 teaser). `[O]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 164](https://www.wuxiaworld.com/novel/solo-leveling-ragnarok/slrk-chapter-164)
- "Hunter" is a very popular job since the Great Cataclysm, and a school-age hunter files a "hunter leave application" through the school office to go on raids (ch. 8, ch. 164 teaser). `[O]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 8](https://www.wuxiaworld.com/novel/solo-leveling-ragnarok/slrk-chapter-8)

- There were "precisely five" National Level Hunters before time was turned back, five hunters survived Kamish, and the US government had offered large rewards to gather top hunters (ch. 175 teaser, which cuts off before any name). `[O]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 175](https://www.wuxiaworld.com/novel/solo-leveling-ragnarok/slrk-chapter-175)
- The fifth National Level Hunter is named Antoine Martinez, a Healer who helped kill Kamish, with country, guild and abilities unknown. Beebom and TechWiser list him without saying where he is first named; a snippet calls him the "Unnamed Healer" of the original whose name is given in *Ragnarok*. The chapter that names him was not found. `[P]` `[S]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-national-level-hunters-explained/)

- China's star scale: five stars is the top for nearly everyone, and Liu Zhigang alone holds six, a special grade given for defending the country in the Great Cataclysm (ch. 204 teaser). `[O]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 204](https://www.wuxiaworld.com/novel/solo-leveling-ragnarok/slrk-chapter-204)

Classes, skills and items:

- Narration sorts hunters by raid function ("tanks, damage-dealers and healers"), not by a class list (ch. 308 teaser). `[O]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 308](https://www.wuxiaworld.com/novel/solo-leveling-ragnarok/slrk-chapter-308)
- "Slaughter" is an Assassin skill, a burst of slashes too fast to see, held by only a few Assassins in the world and valued above Stealth (ch. 259). `[W]`
- A hunter's body can hold only so many rune-stone skills. Woo Jinchul, an A rank who collected many, is dying of it (ch. 300). `[W]`
- Dungeons yield items of differing rarity. No tiers or prices are given (ch. 9). `[O]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 9](https://www.wuxiaworld.com/novel/solo-leveling-ragnarok/slrk-chapter-9)
- A character remarks that hospital bills run high unless you have a close friend who is a healer (ch. 8). The chapter does not mention insurance. `[O]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 8](https://www.wuxiaworld.com/novel/solo-leveling-ragnarok/slrk-chapter-8)

Association, law and guilds:

- Chairman Woo Jinchul labels awakened people who commit crimes "Villains". `[P]` [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solo_Leveling)
- The Association has regional branches: its Busan branch staff work with police to hold back crowds at a dungeon break (ch. 164 teaser). The Central Police Station building serves as its headquarters (ch. 223 teaser). `[O]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 223](https://www.wuxiaworld.com/novel/solo-leveling-ragnarok/slrk-chapter-223)
- A guild figure plans a killing inside a dungeon because killing another hunter there "wouldn't be considered illegal", and gets in by pressing an official for an entry permit (ch. 85 and 86). `[W]`
- A hidden Black Market lets hunters trade, auction and gamble outside taxation and Association oversight (ch. 183). `[W]`
- A hunter must still clear dungeons to "meet the qualifications to become a guild leader" (ch. 85 and 86). `[W]`
- India's Asura Guild was founded by Siddharth Bachchan; one other member is named, Rio Singh (B rank). `[P]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/strongest-hunter-guilds-solo-leveling/)

Labour and money:

- A dungeon job has three teams: an assault team of D ranks and above that goes in first, a collection team that follows to cut up the beasts and cart out hides and stones, and a mining team. E ranks are barred from the assault team and do the manual work, which gives them the most openings, safer conditions and a steady income. D ranks, as the weakest on the assault team, face the most danger (ch. 8 and 9). `[O]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 8](https://www.wuxiaworld.com/novel/solo-leveling-ragnarok/slrk-chapter-8)
- E rank contracts are incentive-based, so pay follows output. Pay and workload are about the same from guild to guild, so E ranks pick a guild for safety, meaning the strength of its assault team (ch. 85 teaser). `[O]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 85](https://www.wuxiaworld.com/novel/solo-leveling-ragnarok/slrk-chapter-85)
- An E rank is paid by the day. A broken dungeon pickaxe is replaced, but its cost comes out of the miner's daily earnings (ch. 8). `[O]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 8](https://www.wuxiaworld.com/novel/solo-leveling-ragnarok/slrk-chapter-8)
- Dungeon entry is bought as a ticket whose price rises with the dungeon's rank and with the number of magic beasts inside. A small guild holding 1 billion won must choose between several low or middle rank dungeons and a single ticket to a higher one. No price per rank is stated (ch. 162 teaser). `[O]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 162](https://www.wuxiaworld.com/novel/solo-leveling-ragnarok/slrk-chapter-162)
- Hunting is described as offering large rewards with little danger if the dungeon suits the hunter's level (ch. 164 teaser). Wolf hides fetch little, and ordinary mana stones hold very little mana each (ch. 9). `[O]` [Wuxiaworld, Ragnarok ch. 9](https://www.wuxiaworld.com/novel/solo-leveling-ragnarok/slrk-chapter-9)
- In ch. 1 one character says E ranks earn well on mining teams and another complains the pay is poor. `[S]`
- No amounts are stated for any wage.

### 12.4 The games

Game material is not canon for the original story.

- *Solo Leveling: Arise* uses the same six classes for its roster. *Arise Overdrive* uses different player classes. `[S]`
- *Arise* mixes canon characters with original ones in the same world, so a character's presence in the game does not show that the original story named them. It keeps publishing "Hunter Origin" one-shot stories; the 31st is Antoine Martinez's. `[P]` [ComicBook.com](https://comicbook.com/anime/news/solo-leveling-returns-with-two-new-stories-amid-season-3-delay/)
- *Arise* added Antoine Martinez as a playable hunter on 7 May 2026, billed as "France's National Level Hunter": an axe user who mixes damage with healing and support. His origin story has him awaken in a car accident and lose his daughter. This is the only place a nationality is given for him. `[P]` [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/solo-leveling-arise/new-hunter-antoine-martinez/)
- In *Arise* Christopher Reed is an "Elemental Stacker", a label from a 2025 rework of the game's classes with no counterpart in the story. `[S]`

## 13. Where sources conflict

Each entry gives the competing claims and then which is better supported.

**Ranks and counts**

1. **How many National Level Hunters, and who is the fifth.** Screen Rant, CBR, the wiki's text and Wikipedia say five were titled. Wikipedia and the wiki's list name four, as does one Beebom explainer. Beebom's National Level explainer, TechWiser and CBR name the fifth as Antoine Martinez. Better supported: five titled, four named in the original story, and the fifth's name comes from the sequel and the game. `[P]` `[W]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-national-level-hunters-explained/)
2. **Whether Jinwoo is a National Level Hunter.** The wiki's Sung Jinwoo infobox lists the uncited epithet "6th National Level Hunter". The wiki's National Level page and Beebom say he was never formally given the title. TechWiser's list of seven adds Suho and Jinwoo while itself saying Jinwoo was never recognised, so the seven is a fan ranking. Better supported: never formally titled. `[P]` `[W]` [TechWiser](https://techwiser.com/solo-leveling-all-national-level-hunters-ranked/)
3. **How many S ranks fought Kamish.** "Hundreds" killed (CBR, the wiki with webtoon ch. 115, Beebom's National Level explainer); "over a hundred" assembled and more than a hundred dead (Beebom's article on the gate). All agree on five survivors. Neither size has a checked chapter, and both sit oddly beside Korea's ten S ranks. Unresolved; treat the size as unverified. `[P]` `[W]` `[low]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/solo-leveling-first-s-rank-gate-explained)
4. **Korean S ranks before Jinwoo.** Nine (CBR, and consistent with his title as the tenth, which Wikipedia and the wiki both give) against seven (one blog review of anime ep 7). Better supported: nine. `[P]` `[W]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)
5. **Japanese S ranks and the Jeju dead.** Eleven before Jeju (Beebom; Wikipedia names eleven) against ten in the raid party (wiki). Better supported: eleven in the country, ten on the raid, seven Japanese and one Korean dead. `[P]` `[W]` [Beebom](https://beebom.com/all-solo-leveling-hunters-who-died-in-jeju-island-raid/)

8. **How common awakening is.** CBR's one in a thousand, and one awakener in five thousand at D rank or higher, have no chapter and no second source. 9. **A typical E rank reading.** CBR's "about 70" is unconfirmed. The stated readings are Jinwoo's 10 and, in the sequel, Suho's 46, called low for an E rank. They are consistent with 70 without confirming it. `[P]` `[low]` [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/solo-leveling-hunter-ranks-explained/)

## 15. Touches in DungeonRaze

- **Portal grades.** The base hall's six portal frames are graded E, D, C, B, A and S, the same ladder as section 2. The owner's plan to scale enemy difficulty and kill rewards by grade relates to sections 2, 3 and 9.
- **Classes.** The game's hunter classes are the generic archetypes of section 4 with original kits. The project's recorded decision to leave a Healer class out relates to 4.2 and 4.3.
- **Enemy threat levels.** The game's `threat` value and the place where levels will plug in relate to the rank spacing in section 3.
- **Base of operations.** The hall as a hub relates to the Association's functions in section 6 and to guild structure in section 7.
- **Loot, inventory and an extraction loop.** These backlog items relate to sections 5 (rune stones, artifacts, monster-part weapons) and 9 (what leaves a gate and who is paid).
- **Rewards and death.** The backlog question of what a run pays and what dying costs relates to sections 9 and 10.

Two project rules apply to everything above. Names, characters and signature moves from the series are deliberately not used in the game. The shadow-raising class was dropped by the owner.
